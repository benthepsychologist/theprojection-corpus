## 2026-09-10 — OpenAI formally asks Congress for mandatory, capability-based national AI safety rules — while running a "reverse federalism" play through California first

- **OpenAI chief global affairs officer Chris Lehane published a call for Congress to pass mandatory national AI safety regulation** covering testing/independent-assessment protocols, stronger cybersecurity requirements, clear incident-reporting rules, mandatory monitoring for model misalignment, written-notice requirements when a model circumvents its own security controls, and "mandatory alignment-evaluation gates before deployment." This is the clearest single ask yet from a frontier lab for the binding federal rules this thread's own watch line asks about ("the announced framework's terms, who joins/refuses"). Notably OpenAI is pursuing this via "reverse federalism" — backing California state bills first (two were signed by Governor Newsom the same week, covering independent evaluation, auditor standards, biological-threat screening and child protections) to set a de facto baseline Congress could later codify, rather than waiting on Congress to act first. Critics quoted in coverage note the proposed regime mirrors the EU AI Act and that "every item on that list is something a well-resourced frontier lab already does" — i.e., a regulatory floor a large incumbent can absorb but a smaller competitor may not.
