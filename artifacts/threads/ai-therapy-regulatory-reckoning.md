## 2026-09-17 — The EU's KIDS Act proposal reaches AI companions and chatbots

- **The European Commission's KIDS Act proposal, adopted 09-17, would bar AI companions and chatbots accessible to minors from designs that simulate human relationships in ways likely to create emotional dependency, and would let under-13s use them only through parental controls.** By default such chatbots may not carry a child's earlier conversations into later ones, must be tested for risks to children before launch and monitored for harm afterwards, and, when built into a platform or game, may not switch on automatically, be pushed at children, or be hard to turn off; the same proposal sets an EU-wide minimum age of 15 for a child's own social-media account. It is a proposal, not law, and must still pass Parliament and the Council. ## 2026-09-14 — NPR tests OpenAI's four-month-old suicide-crisis feature and finds it invisible to the users it's meant to protect

- **NPR ran its own users through ChatGPT's "Trusted Contact" feature — the opt-in tool OpenAI quietly launched in May that lets a user name someone to be notified if the bot detects a serious suicide/self-harm risk — and found neither a first-time user nor two existing regular users, including the reporter, were ever prompted to set it up.** Asked directly, ChatGPT itself first denied having the feature ("Maybe you're thinking of another app") before confirming it, opt-in only, findable solely by digging into Settings. OpenAI spokesperson Gaby Raila told NPR the company has seen "good adoption" but declined to give numbers, and said the bot "may" surface it to users already showing distress — it is not offered proactively to new users before any crisis, the way an emergency contact is collected at a doctor's office. This is exactly the thread's evidence-gap thesis in miniature: a genuine safety feature exists, but its actual reach is unmeasured and, by NPR's own test, effectively undiscoverable to the population it's built for. Suicide-prevention researchers gave mixed reactions on the record — Johns Hopkins' Holly Wilcox said it "would be ideal to offer it to all users" before a crisis, not just after; University of Alabama's Jennifer Lockman doubted a bot can replicate a trained clinician's judgment about who those contacts should be, while still calling it "another opportunity" for human connection. The feature still doesn't work on ChatGPT voice, per OpenAI.
  ([NPR/Houston Public Media](https://www.houstonpublicmedia.org/npr/2026/09/14/nx-s1-5943118/npr-looked-for-chatgpts-new-crisis-feature-its-hard-to-find/)) ⟨daily 2026-09-14⟩
