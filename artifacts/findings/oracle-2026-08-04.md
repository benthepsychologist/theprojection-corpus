# Finding — Oracle: the leveraged Stargate bet, Q4 FY26 to now

**Crawled:** 2026-08-04 · **Bundle:**
`artifacts/bundles/oracle-2026-08-04/provenance.yaml`

Ben's brief: Oracle carries a `kingdom`-rank board entry (~$64B commandable
capital, but FCF **-$23.7B** and **$156B** debt) and shows up as supporting
cast on six live threads (`ai-circular-financing-risk`, `stargate-buildout`,
`ai-power-buildout`, `ai-datacenter-sites`, `nuclear-for-ai`,
`datacenters-as-targets`) — none of which are actually ABOUT Oracle's own
arc. This crawl asks whether that arc deserves its own thread, and
reconstructs it from Oracle's most recent quarterly print (Q4 FY26,
reported 2026-06-10) through the 2026-07-09 S&P downgrade to the most
current framing (2026-08-03).

**Verdict up front:** Oracle's own story is a leveraged, debt-funded,
single-customer-concentrated bet, not a diversified hyperscaler capex
story like Microsoft/Google/Amazon/Meta. It just posted a **$638 billion**
RPO backlog — up 363% year-over-year — confirmed directly from Oracle's
own SEC 8-K, which supersedes the board's carried "~$450B+ RPO, conf LOW,
unverified" figure with a primary-sourced, much larger number. But the
credibility question this crawl surfaces isn't whether the number is
real — it's whether the number can be **collected**: roughly half of that
backlog traces to a single counterparty, OpenAI, whose own reported 2026
shortfall against internal targets is now the single biggest variable in
whether Oracle's negative-cash-flow buildout pays off. S&P downgraded
Oracle one notch to BBB- (one step above junk) on exactly this thesis,
and Moody's has it on a negative outlook. This is a financing/credit-risk
story, not a disputed-guidance story like Arm's royalty regime — the
RPO figure itself is well-sourced; the doubt is about the payer, not the
receipt.

## 1. Q4 / full-year FY2026 results (fiscal year ended 2026-05-31, reported 2026-06-10)

- **Total revenue:** FY26 **$67.4B** (+17% USD); Q4 **$19.2B** (+21% USD,
  +20% constant currency) — both records.
- **Total cloud revenue:** FY26 **$34.0B** (+39%); Q4 **$9.9B** (+47%).
- **Cloud infrastructure (OCI/IaaS) revenue** — the fastest-growing,
  most AI-exposed line: FY26 **$18.1B** (+77% USD, +75% cc); Q4 **$5.8B**
  (+93% USD, +92% cc).
- **RPO (Remaining Performance Obligations):** ended the quarter at
  **$638B**, up **363%** year-over-year and up **$85B** sequentially from
  Q3. Most of the Q3-and-Q4 RPO increase is large AI contracts where the
  customer either prepaid for GPUs or supplied its own hardware to
  Oracle; the prepaid/customer-supplied-hardware slice of these large AI
  contracts now totals **$75B**. Management guides **12% of RPO
  converting to revenue over the next 12 months (~$76.6B)**, up from the
  **$67B** actually generated in FY26 — i.e., management itself is
  projecting only a low-double-digit-percent annual drawdown of an
  eight-years-of-revenue backlog.
- **Operating cash flow:** FY26 **$32.0B** (+54%) — the cash-generation
  side looks healthy in isolation.
- **Free cash flow:** FY26 **-$23.7B** — negative, because capex outran
  operating cash flow by a wide margin.
- **Capex:** FY26 **$55.7B**.
- **Financing:** FY26 raised **$43B in debt** + **$5B in equity**; FY27
  guided to raise **~$40B more debt**.
- **FY27 guidance:** total revenue confirmed at **$90B**; non-GAAP EPS
  raised to **$8.05** (+18%); Q1 FY27 (next print) guided to **27–29%**
  total revenue growth and **57–63%** cloud revenue growth.
([Oracle Q4 FY26 press release / 8-K exhibit, SEC EDGAR](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001341439/000119312526265848/orcl-ex99_1.htm), 2026-06-10 — high confidence, primary source)

## 2. The escalation into FY27: bigger bet, same shape

- **FY27 capex is stepping up again** — reported **~$70B** planned for
  the coming fiscal year, up from FY26's $55.7B (Larry Ellison / Oracle
  announcement, via The Register, 2026-08-03 — medium, single outlet but
  consistent with the S&P capex projection below).
- **S&P projects FY27 capex at $90–95B** and Oracle's **free operating
  cash flow deficit widening to roughly -$42B** — nearly double FY26's
  -$23.7B shortfall — with **adjusted debt-to-leverage reaching the
  mid-4x area** in FY27.
([S&P downgrade coverage, Yahoo Finance, 2026-07-09](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/oracle-stock-shrugs-off-p-185346661.html) — medium-high, corroborated across multiple outlets)

## 3. The credit-rating action (2026-07-09) and what it cites

- **S&P Global Ratings downgraded Oracle one notch, BBB → BBB-, outlook
  stable, on 2026-07-09** — one step above speculative-grade ("junk").
  Cited reasons, in S&P's own framing as reported: the FY27 capex jump to
  $90–95B, the FCF deficit widening toward -$42B, leverage rising to
  mid-4x, and — explicitly — that **OpenAI accounts for roughly half of
  Oracle's RPO**, a concentration risk S&P named directly.
([S&P downgrade coverage, Yahoo Finance, 2026-07-09](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/oracle-stock-shrugs-off-p-185346661.html) — medium-high)
- **Total debt figures diverge across sources and are not fully
  reconciled this crawl:** the board's carried figure is **$156B**;
  post-downgrade coverage cites **$167B** in one outlet and a **$130–156B**
  range (net debt ~$131B, long-term borrowings >$122B) in another —
  plausibly different snapshots (gross vs. net, or dated before/after the
  FY26-close $43B debt raise) rather than a contradiction, but flagged as
  an open reconciliation gap rather than silently picking one number.
  ([Downgrade coverage compilation via WebSearch](https://www.computing.co.uk/news/2026/ai/oracle-under-pressure-after-ratings-downgrade-over-ai-expansion) — medium, multi-outlet but not cross-checked against a single primary filing this crawl)
- **Moody's has assigned Oracle a negative outlook** (exact date not
  independently pinned down this crawl — thin, WebSearch-summary level).
  A separate, earlier data point (surfaced by the 2026-07-28 CoreWeave
  crawl, corroborating rather than new to this one): Moody's named
  **"Oracle and CoreWeave as AI's weakest credit link"** (2026-07-25,
  Bitget — thin, single outlet), and **Oracle's CDS hit a cycle high**
  around 2026-07-20/21 (Seeking Alpha/MSN — medium, 2-outlet
  convergence). The Register's 2026-08-03 piece additionally states
  Oracle's CDS spread "has spiked" further since, without a specific
  basis-point figure (medium, single outlet, directionally consistent).

## 4. The RPO credibility question — sourced, but is it collectible?

- **The number itself is now strongly sourced**: $638B RPO is Oracle's
  own SEC 8-K disclosure, not a press estimate — this materially upgrades
  the board's prior "~$450B+ RPO backlog post-Stargate, conf LOW,
  unverified" note, which is now stale and superseded by a higher,
  better-sourced figure.
- **The concentration, not the figure, is the live dispute.** Multiple
  outlets converge on **OpenAI representing roughly half of Oracle's
  RPO**; one specific breakdown puts it at **~$300B / 54.25%** tied to
  OpenAI through the Stargate-linked contract (medium confidence — a
  named-analyst-level figure, not itself SEC-disclosed at that
  precision). Analyst Daniel Luria (cited via aggregated coverage) said
  concentration "significantly reduces" enthusiasm, particularly if "more
  than 90% came from OpenAI" — flagging that even the ~50% figure may
  understate true single-relationship exposure once sub-contracts are
  netted out. (thin-medium, analyst-quote level, not independently
  verified against a filing)
([RPO skepticism roundup via WebSearch](https://news.ssbcrack.com/oracles-638-billion-backlog-key-to-success-amid-skepticism/), [OpenAI concentration analysis](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/openai-shortfall-puts-oracle-ai-050252163.html) — thin-medium)
- **OpenAI reportedly missed its own internal user and sales targets for
  2026** — reported around 2026-04-28/29, with **no clearly identified
  primary source** in the coverage found this crawl (the claim
  propagates through secondary aggregation without a named original
  outlet) — **flagged explicitly as thin/unconfirmed**, but material
  because it is the direct mechanism by which "can OpenAI pay" doubt
  entered the Oracle credit story. Stock reaction: Oracle and several
  chip stocks fell on the reports (exact price moves not independently

([Secondary aggregation, 2026-04-29](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/openai-shortfall-puts-oracle-ai-050252163.html) — thin, unconfirmed primary source)
- **A separate "round-trip revenue" critique** (2026-05-25) argues Oracle's

## 5. Stargate: Oracle's actual role vs. OpenAI/SoftBank/MGX

- **Stargate's initial equity funders are SoftBank, OpenAI, Oracle and
  MGX** (Abu Dhabi). Structurally, **SoftBank holds financial
  responsibility and OpenAI holds operational responsibility** for the
  joint venture — Oracle is not one of the two lead entities in that
  split; its role is as **the compute/infrastructure builder and a
  co-investor**, not the JV's financial or operational principal.
  Initial commitment $100B, ramping toward **$500B over four years**.
- **Distinct from the JV structure**: Oracle separately signed its own
  **~$300B, five-year cloud-services contract with OpenAI** (announced
  July 2025, for **4.5GW** of capacity) — this bilateral contract, not
  the shared JV equity stake, is what constitutes the bulk of the
  OpenAI-linked RPO concentration described in §4.
- **Confirmed Stargate sites (as of 2026, per OpenAI's own site
  announcements):** Abilene, TX (flagship, 1.2GW); Shackelford County,
  TX; Doña Ana County, NM; Lordstown, OH; a Wisconsin Midwest site —
  cumulatively approaching **~7GW** planned capacity and **$400B+**
  committed investment over three years, described as ahead of pace
  toward the full $500B/10GW four-year target.
([OpenAI's own Stargate site announcements](https://openai.com/index/five-new-stargate-sites/); [S&P Global JV structure summary](https://www.spglobal.com/market-intelligence/en/news-insights/research/softbabnk-openai-oracle-and-mgx-commit-to-100b-for-stargate-ai-infrastructure) — medium-high, primary-adjacent and corroborated)

- On the earnings call: **"Oracle will build more cloud infrastructure
  data centers than all of our infrastructure competitors combined."**
- On AI strategy: **"The Oracle AI Data Platform makes all your data —
  ALL of your data — accessible to AI models,"** framing the Oracle AI
  Database/Data Platform combination as breaking down data silos for
  model access.
- Most recent (2026-08-03, on Oracle's AI-coding stance): **"The code
  that Oracle is writing, Oracle isn't writing. Our AI models are

  contributions to OpenJDK on safety/security grounds, a tension The

  about the bet versus how Oracle manages its own technical risk).
