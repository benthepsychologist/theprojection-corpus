## 1. Q2 2026 earnings — guidance raised twice, bookings disclosure quietly stopped

ASML's headline financial story this window is a **guidance ratchet**, not
a single earnings beat:

- **2026-04-15 (Q1 2026 results)** — full-year 2026 net-sales guidance set
  at **€36-40B**, gross margin 51-53%. CEO Christophe Fouquet's own
  statement (SEC 6-K, primary source): *"we expect that the bandwidth in
  our 2026 guidance accommodates potential outcomes of ongoing discussions
  around export controls"* — guidance was explicitly built with
  export-control uncertainty priced in from the start. (high)
- **2026-07-15 (Q2 2026 results) — the big raise.** Full-year 2026 guidance
  lifted to **€43-45B net sales, 54-56% gross margin**. Q2 itself: **€9.3B
  net sales, €2.9B net income (31.3% margin), 54.0% gross margin, €7.59
  EPS**, 86 new systems sold (+5 used), system mix Logic 51%/Memory 49%,
  free cash flow €1.3B. Q3 2026 guidance: **€11.0-12.0B revenue, 55-57%
  gross margin**. (ASML press release / GlobeNewswire, high)
- **Bookings disclosure has gone dark.** ASML no longer reports a hard
  quarterly net-bookings figure — H1 2026 order intake was characterized
  only as "very strong" on the call. The last hard number found anywhere
  in this crawl is **Q4 2025: €13.2B net bookings, €7.4B of it EUV**; a
  YE2025 backlog figure of **€38.8B** was also reported. A separate,
  thinner Q1-2026 claim of a "record EUV backlog of 45 units" (driven by
  simultaneous 2nm-and-below orders from TSMC, Samsung Foundry, and Intel
  Foundry) surfaced from a single aggregator, not confirmed against a
  primary ASML disclosure — treat as directional, not exact. (medium/thin)
- **2027 posture:** management called 2027 EUV order coverage **"close to
  being fully covered"** and committed to a **30% capacity increase** for
  both low-NA EUV and DUV immersion systems in 2027, while "investigating"
  a further 30% expansion for 2028. A secondary count (TechPowerUp) puts
  **56 low-NA + 10 High-NA EUV tools scheduled for 2027 delivery**.
  (medium)
- **Not found:** ASML's own capex figure or full-year 2026 capex guidance
  — every search surfaced customer-side capex (TSMC, hyperscalers)
  instead. Also not found: any Q3 pre-announcement between the July 15
  print and this crawl's 2026-08-04 date, beyond a thinly-sourced interim
  dividend of €1.88/share paid 2026-08-05.

## 2. High-NA EUV — Intel ships first, TSMC opts out, Samsung and SK Hynix queue up

High-NA (0.55 numerical aperture, up from 0.33 — model lines EXE:5000
R&D / EXE:5200-5200B production, ~$380-386M per unit) is the tool the
whole industry is watching for the next lithography generation, and this
window produced the first real production milestone:

- **2026-07-15 — Intel Foundry becomes the first company anywhere to ship
  high-volume commercial logic made with High-NA EUV** — select layers of
  Intel 18A-node Core Ultra Series 3 ("Panther Lake") chips. The
  High-NA-processed layers are "dual-qualified" (interchangeable with the
  older 0.33 NA scanner) and ASML said yields already match the
  established platform. This is a production-readiness demonstration on
  select layers, not a full-chip High-NA process yet. (ASML press
  release, corroborated by SiliconANGLE/Tom's Hardware/TechTimes, high)
- **TSMC is explicitly skipping High-NA for A14 (1.4nm, targeting 2028
  production).** TSMC SVP Kevin Zhang stated at the TSMC North America
  Technology Symposium that High-NA would drive up to a **2.5x cost
  increase** versus continuing with standard EUV multi-patterning, and
  that TSMC can hold process complexity flat without it. This is TSMC's
  stated position as of mid-2026, not a confirmed permanent pass beyond
  A14. (Tom's Hardware, corroborated by wccftech/SemiWiki/eeNews Europe,
  high)
- **Samsung** reportedly purchased **two EXE:5200B High-NA systems** for
  its 2nm SF2 process (Exynos 2600, reportedly also eyed for Tesla AI-chip
  production) and HBM4 memory, targeted for 1H-2026 mass production —
  but no source in this crawl confirms the tools are actually installed
  or running; treat as "scheduled," not "operational." (TrendForce/
  guru3d/tweaktown, medium)
- **SK Hynix placed a record ~$7.9-8B EUV order** (SK Hynix's own
  regulatory filing) covering deliveries through **December 2027** —
  roughly 30 EUV machines for its M15X HBM plant (Cheongju) and the new
  Yongin Semiconductor Cluster, of which an estimated **2 units are
  High-NA**. (SK Hynix filing via Tom's Hardware/MobileWorldLive, high on
  the order figure, medium on the High-NA-unit breakout)
- **Micron:** no High-NA-specific order or shipment news found anywhere in
  this crawl.
- The installed R&D base for the platform (pre-production) is **5 EXE:5000
  scanners**: Intel (2), TSMC, Samsung, and the joint ASML-imec High-NA
  Lab in Veldhoven. ASML's own 2026 revenue-recognition guidance is for
  **4-5 High-NA systems**. (medium)

## 4. China and export controls — the recurring chokepoint fight, now with a legislative vehicle

This is the single busiest thread of the crawl, running on three fronts
at once: **revenue exposure**, a **new legislative vehicle in Washington**,
and **China's own domestic-tool response** (which overlaps this repo's
existing `china-duv-lithography` thread).

**Revenue exposure — falling, with one source conflict flagged rather than
resolved.** China's share of ASML sales has been sliding through 2026 off
a stockpiling-driven 2025 peak: **33% for FY2025** (36% of system sales in
Q4 2025 specifically) → **19% of system sales in Q1 2026** → a reported
**16% (€2.6B) in H1 2026 actual**, dropping China to third-largest market
behind Taiwan and South Korea. Against that trend, **CFO Roger Dassen on
the 2026-07-15 Q2 call said directly: "We still look at China as
approximately 20% of our total net sales, so that percentage hasn't
changed"** — attributing the level to domestic-led logic-chip demand
moving in sync with global demand. The 16%-actual and ~20%-stated figures
may reflect different bases (H1 actual vs. full-year forecast, or system
sales vs. total net sales) — no single source reconciles them, so both
are carried here rather than picking one. (Dassen quote: primary
transcript, high; the trend figures: medium, multiple outlets)

**The MATCH Act — a real legislative vehicle, not yet law.** Introduced
**2026-04-02/08** in both chambers (House sponsor Rep. Michael Baumgartner;
Senate companion S. 4281, Sens. Pete Ricketts/Andy Kim, co-sponsored by
Senate Democratic Leader Chuck Schumer), the bill would classify **all DUV
immersion lithography machines** (not just EUV) as "covered semiconductor
manufacturing equipment," give Commerce 150 days to certify that the
Netherlands and Japan have matched US-style country-wide controls, and
otherwise extend US jurisdiction unilaterally via the Foreign Direct
Product Rule. It passed the House Foreign Affairs Committee (a stripped
countrywide cryogenic-etch ban aside, DUV restrictions survived), reported
by lawmakers as the "largest export control markup in the history of
Congress." One analysis judges it will **pass the House this year but not
become law in the 119th Congress**, with the Senate side possibly folded
into the NDAA instead of getting its own markup. ASML shares reportedly
fell ~3.44% on the initial 2026-04-02 news. (medium-high; the "won't become
law" read is a single analytical source, flagged as such)

**Dutch government pushback.** PM Rob Jetten identified ASML export
controls as a primary reason for **King Willem-Alexander and Queen
Máxima's April 2026 state visit to Washington**, including a White House
dinner with President Trump — talks were described as "constructive but
without consensus." Dutch government irritation over the MATCH Act's
extraterritorial reach was still being reported as of **2026-06-24**.
(medium-high, multiple convergent Dutch-press sources)

**The Lutnick inquiry — open, unresolved, and unusually direct.** Starting
**April 2026**, Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick began privately
questioning senior ASML executives over a concern that an EUV system may
have reached China in violation of export controls — which would breach
the multilateral Wassenaar Arrangement if true. ASML publicly denied this
(**2026-06-19/21**): the company says **none of its 314 operational EUV
machines worldwide (plus 26 decommissioned units) are located in China**,
calling the allegations "false and reputation-damaging." No outlet found
by this crawl confirms a resolution either way as of 2026-08-04 — the
matter appears to remain open. (medium — the inquiry's existence and
ASML's denial are convergently reported; the underlying evidentiary basis
for the US concern was never described beyond "specialized transport
machinery" sightings, per one outlet)

**China's domestic DUV alternative — same company this repo already
tracks under `china-duv-lithography`, now with fresher detail.**
Shanghai Aishengna Electronic Technology Group (state-owned, founded
2023, ~7B yuan/~$1B registered capital, absorbing engineering teams from
Huawei-affiliated startup Yuliangsheng and state scanner maker SMEE) is
producing China's first domestically-made immersion DUV tools — **5 units
targeted for 2026, 20 for 2027**, first deliveries to **SMIC, Hua Hong,
and CXMT** this year for production-line validation (SMIC has reportedly
been testing a unit since 2025-09). The tool prints 28nm-class features in
a single exposure (7nm-class only via multi-patterning) and is
consistently described as "a generation behind" ASML, which still holds
an estimated **~98.7% of the global immersion-lithography market**. Two
items surfaced newer than this repo's existing thread entry (2026-07-29):
- **2026-07-30 (Tom's Hardware):** an AI Futures Project forecast (June
  2026) puts a **commercially viable domestic 7nm immersion scanner
  between 2032 and 2038, median 2035** — a far longer horizon than the
  "delivered this year" framing of the initial breaking story. (medium —
  headline/snippet confirmed, full body not retrievable)
- **2026-08-04 (Tom's Hardware, today):** a roadmap review confirms the
  ~5-units/year DUV pace and notes China's separate EUV prototype effort
  (Shenzhen, Huawei-coordinated, first attempted since early 2025,
  targeting a first exposed wafer by 2028 though Reuters' own sources
  reportedly consider 2030 more realistic) has **produced no usable chips
  yet**. (thin-medium, headline-level)
- **2026-08-02 (TechTimes, thin/unverified):** reports CXMT nearing
  LPDDR6 production but flags a concrete performance gap (12.8 Gbps vs.
  14.4 Gbps) attributed to the DUV-only manufacturing ceiling — full
  article body could not be fetched, headline only.
- **The best single explainer found (War on the Rocks, 2026-07-29, full
  text retrieved, high confidence):** ASML's EUV machines contain
  100,000+ components (700,000 at subsystem level) from ~5,000 suppliers;
  standard units run ~$180M, High-NA ~$380M. China's reverse-engineering
  push (former ASML engineers, per a December 2025 report) can capture
  component-level knowledge but not the "tacit, path-dependent"
  systems-integration and yield knowledge that actually matters — and
  faces supply-chain chokepoints entirely outside its reach: Japanese
  photoresist makers JSR/TOK/Shin-Etsu, Tokyo Electron's near-monopoly on
  EUV photoresist coaters/developers, Hoya/AGC on mask blanks, KLA on
  metrology, and Lasertec's near-monopoly on actinic mask inspection.
  ASML also employs ~10,000 field-support engineers requiring nearly a
  year of training each — a services moat, not just a hardware one.

**Standing baseline, not new this window but the active legal mechanism:**
BIS's **2024-12-05** Federal Register rule (0% de-minimis for certain
lithography equipment) gives US jurisdiction leverage over ASML indirectly
via Cymer, its San Diego-based excimer-laser subsidiary. Dutch export
licensing for DUV sales to China (in force since **2024-09**, via Dutch
Customs/CDIU) is unchanged in this window — **41 Chinese companies hold
valid licenses**, no new 2026 Dutch rule found. Japan's Nikon/Canon
after-sales-service tightening for Chinese customers (November 2025) is
also unchanged — **no fresh 2026 Japan action found**. **No new 2026
Entity List addition specifically tied to ASML customers was found**, and
**no lawsuit and no new 2026 Federal Register rule naming ASML, EUV, or
DUV lithography equipment directly** was found (a direct Federal Register
API query returned nothing newer than the 2024 rule).
