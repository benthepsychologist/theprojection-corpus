# Q10 — Does AI-in-MH regulation converge or fragment? (research memo, 2026-08-07)

Standing question Q10 on the mental-health feed asks whether regulation of AI in mental health is converging toward one shared framework or fragmenting into incompatible regional/state patchworks. This memo tests that question against the actual 2021→2026 record — what got signed, what got enforced, what got deferred — using primary and near-primary sources (federal agencies, law-firm summaries of primary text, official EU/UK sources) collected 2026-08-07. It also tests four specific forward-looking predictions from a manuscript-in-progress (*The Evidence Gap*, `/workspace/the-evidence-gap-src/outlines/chapter-3.md`, Acts 2 and 4) against what actually happened, since three of those four tracks had calendar deadlines that have now passed.

## TL;DR

- **Fragmentation wins, decisively, at every layer examined.** Four US federal "regulatory-completion" tracks the book names are each stalled or in limbo rather than converging (MHPAEA non-enforced since May 2025; DEA still on its fourth "temporary" telemedicine extension with no permanent Ryan Haight rule; FTC has brought zero mental-health-specific actions since April 2024's Cerebral settlement); only the CMS Physician Fee Schedule billing-code track is actually advancing on schedule.
- **US states are the opposite of converging: at least 7 states now ban AI from independently delivering therapy outright (Illinois, Nevada, Maine, Rhode Island, Vermont, Tennessee, Colorado), a separate 4+ states require disclosure/crisis-referral only, and nearly 100 chatbot bills were introduced in 2026 alone** — no two laws identical, no federal preemption, and (confirmed across five independent sources) **zero enforcement actions under any of these laws as of today**, so the patchwork is untested as well as uncoordinated.
- **The book's own EU AI Act expectation for Act 2 did not hold as written, and the feed's ledger had it right first.** The manuscript frames August 2, 2026 as a scope-testing milestone for high-risk medical/mental-health AI; in fact the EU's "Digital Omnibus" deferred the Annex III (standalone) high-risk obligations to December 2, 2027 and Annex I (embedded) to August 2, 2028 — confirmed directly against a law-firm primary-text summary. What *did* activate on schedule 2026-08-02 was AI Office enforcement power over general-purpose models plus Article 50 transparency duties (chatbot-disclosure, deepfake-labelling) — neither of which is the high-risk MH-AI test the book anticipated.
- **Canada's fragmentation premise holds up:** Wellness Together Canada is confirmed wound down with no federal successor found, and no evaluation-framework replacement (CDA-AMC-level or otherwise) turned up in this search — though the specific Sockalingam RCT outcome-publication status could not be confirmed either way (record-dependency, unresolved).
- **The UK is the one jurisdiction showing real (if small) forward motion:** MHRA's "AI Airlock" regulatory sandbox is funded through 2029 (£1.2M/year, 2026-2029) with a WHO/Europe-partnered mental-health-AI knowledge community underway, but no 2026 NHS DTAC iteration or mental-health-specific Airlock cohort was found live yet — it is a real infrastructure build, not yet a mental-health-AI approval pathway.

## Current state of the dispute

**Within the US, the record is one-sided: every federal "completion" mechanism except CMS billing codes is stalled, while the state layer is fragmenting faster than at any point since 2021.** Illinois's WOPR Act (signed August 1, 2025) opened the wave; by mid-2026 independent trackers count differently depending on definition — one source counts 4 outright-ban states (Illinois, Nevada, Maine, Rhode Island) as of its snapshot date, another separately reports Colorado, Tennessee, and Vermont also enacting outright bans in 2026 with effective dates of June-August 2026 — reconciling the effective-date record puts the true outright-ban count at **at least 7 states** by 2026-08-07, with a further 4+ states (Utah, New York, California, Nebraska) requiring disclosure/crisis-referral without banning the practice outright, and a much larger tier of general child-safety chatbot laws (Connecticut, Georgia, Hawaii, Idaho, Iowa, Oregon, South Carolina, Washington, Wyoming) that touch AI-companion risk without being MH-specific. Nearly 100 chatbot-related bills were introduced across states in 2026. **Across five independent sources checked today — including a direct search against Illinois's own regulator — no enforcement action (fine, cease-and-desist, investigation) has been taken under any of these laws yet.** This corroborates, rather than surprises, this repo's own `state-therapy-chatbot-bans` thread, whose watch line already flags "first ENFORCEMENT action under any enacted law (none found yet)" as of 2026-08-07 — today's research confirms that line is still accurate, not stale.

**Internationally, the dispute is really about tempo, not direction.** No jurisdiction examined is moving toward convergence with the US voluntary-assessment pattern or with each other on a shared timeline. The EU is executing a single continent-wide instrument but has now slipped its own high-risk deadline once (Aug 2026 → Dec 2027) with the book's own source material flagging a second slip as a live scenario. The UK is building sandbox infrastructure (AI Airlock, DTAC) rather than a hard deadline regime. Canada has less national machinery in 2026 than it did in 2023, with WTC's federal wind-down unreplaced. Germany is the only jurisdiction tightening its evidence bar on a fixed schedule (the 2026 DiGA reform). That is textbook fragmentation by regulatory *architecture* — HTA-gated (Germany/NHS), enforcement-gated (EU), and voluntary-assessment (US) are structurally different mechanisms, not converging variants of one model — layered on top of the US's own internal fragmentation between a stalled federal layer and an accelerating state layer.

## What the book already establishes

Chapter 3's Act 4 (`chapter-3.md:92-105`) frames all four US tracks as having "a trajectory observable now and a decisional milestone past 2026," explicitly hedging that the chapter "cannot claim what the 2026-2030 record shows because the 2026-2030 record is not yet complete." That hedge holds up well against what this memo found: none of the four tracks has actually completed, which is itself informative — the book's own three-way framing ("stable under continued completion," "destabilized by rollback," "reshaped by mechanisms not anticipated") maps cleanly onto what happened, with MHPAEA and DEA landing closest to "destabilized/stalled" and PFS landing as the "continued completion" case.

Act 2 (`chapter-3.md:40-59`) frames three jurisdictional patterns — single-payer/HTA-gated (Germany, NHS), regulatory-and-enforcement-gated (EU AI Act, FDA), and voluntary buyer-facing (US: PHTI/CHAI) — and asks whether the US voluntary pattern is optimal, suboptimal-but-compatible, or incompatible elsewhere by 2028-2030. That three-way taxonomy still reads as accurate structurally; what needs correcting is the specific EU milestone the book anchors the Act-2 argument to (see below).

## For the book

**Act 2's anchor claim needs a direct correction, not just a caveat.** As written (`chapter-3.md:46`), Act 2 treats "the EU AI Act's August 2, 2026 milestone for high-risk-AI provisions" as an observable scope-test event. It is not one: the Digital Omnibus deferred that exact obligation to December 2, 2027 (Annex III) / August 2, 2028 (Annex I), and this deferral was itself enacted via a Parliament vote on 2026-06-16 — i.e., **before** the book's own "what's already observable" cutoff, meaning the deferral was knowable at writing time and should be the Act-2 beat, not the original Aug-2026 date. This is a genuine premise update, not a hedge: replace "the EU AI Act's August 2, 2026 milestone" with "the EU AI Act's high-risk-obligation deferral (Digital Omnibus, June 2026 vote; Annex III to Dec 2027, Annex I to Aug 2028) — and the GPAI-enforcement/Article-50-transparency activation that *did* land on schedule Aug 2, 2026, which is a narrower and different event than the high-risk MH-AI test the chapter needs." This repo's own steering ledger (`attention/upcoming.yaml`, entry `eu-ai-act-high-risk-deferred`, logged 2026-08-02) had already caught this correctly five days before this memo was commissioned — worth noting as a case where the feed's discipline of forcing a source-verified claim onto every ledger entry outperformed a manuscript's own advance research.

**Act 4's DEA beat is the cleanest "not anticipated at writing time" candidate for the chapter's own three-way Act-4 framing.** The book (`chapter-3.md:100`) already flags DEA/Ryan Haight as extended "through 2024-2026" with the final rule's issuance shaping telehealth economics; what's now confirmed is a *fourth* extension (not the first or second), explicit administration uncertainty about whether to finalize the prior administration's proposal at all, and no public signal of what a final rule would even contain. That is closer to Act 6's "institutional dissolution" reading (a mechanism that just keeps not resolving) than to "stable under continued completion" — worth flagging for whichever Act-6 reading the chapter ultimately argues for.

**A genuine open gap for the book to flag rather than resolve:** this memo could not confirm the Sockalingam WTC-RCT outcome-publication status (Act 2's own named record-dependency, `chapter-3.md:45`) — neither a completed-results paper nor its absence was locatable via PubMed under that author/topic pairing in this search. That is worth a direct pre-pub pull rather than treating as settled either way.
