## 1. AUM — the print holds, and one symbolic milestone

- **~$12T global AUM** is the figure carried across 2026 coverage
  (Motley Fool/Yahoo Finance, 2026-07-20) — matches what the board
  already carries. (high)
- **VOO (Vanguard S&P 500 ETF) crossed $1T in assets, June 2026** — the
  first ETF in history to do so (Morningstar, InvestmentNews,
  2026-06-03/04). (high)
- **International assets (ex-US) topped $1T, January 2026** (Reuters,
  citing FT, 2026-01-24). (high)
- Read: growth is still happening entirely through the existing
  index/passive machine — no new asset class is driving it.

## 2. AI infrastructure / private markets — the abstention, confirmed, with one caveat

- **The abstention on direct AI-infrastructure capital holds.** No
  Vanguard-branded datacenter, chip, or power deal turned up anywhere in
  this pass — no GIP-style alternatives arm, no balance-sheet bet on
  compute, in contrast to BlackRock's Aligned/GIP buildout. (confirmed
  by absence — searched specifically, nothing found)
- **But it is now building the retail pipe for other people's private
  capital.** Vanguard + Blackstone + Wellington launched **two actual
  closed-end funds, 2026-07-22** (WSJ, Reuters, Pensions & Investments),
  blending public and private-market exposure for wealthy/retail
  investors — Wellington manages the semi-liquid vehicles, which hold
  Blackstone and Vanguard investments. (high)
- **Next phase flagged: retirement-specific (401k) versions of the same
  wrapper** (401k Specialist, 2026-07-23). (medium)
- **The explicit strategic framing is "partnerships, not acquisitions"**
  — CEO Salim Ramji, quoted 2025-06-26 (Pensions & Investments), still
  the operative doctrine a year later. Vanguard hired a former Nuveen
  exec to run private-markets strategy (Aug 2025). (medium)
- Read: this is a distribution move, not an allocation move — Vanguard
  is monetizing its retail shelf-space by selling access to Blackstone's
  deals, not deploying its own AUM into AI-adjacent private assets. The
  abstention from being a *principal* in AI infrastructure is intact;
  what changed is it now takes a cut of other people's exposure to it.

## 3. Expense ratios — still grinding toward zero

- **2026-02-02, "landmark cost savings"**: cut fees on **53 mutual
  funds/ETFs**, average expense ratio down to **~0.06%**, ~**$500-600M**
  in aggregate investor savings since 2025 (Vanguard corporate release
  via PR Newswire; Morningstar; ETF.com; USA Today; Kiplinger). (high)
- **2026-07-21**: cut the fee on its **$77B FTSE All-World ETF** by
  **25%** — the *second* cut to that fund within a year (ETF Stream,
  Investment Week, RankiaPro — Europe's best-selling global-equity ETF).
  (high)
- Read: no change in kind, just continued pressure — the at-cost model
  keeps ratcheting the whole industry's floor down.

## 4. Proxy voting / stewardship — the real "abstention IS the position" evidence

- **Investor Choice program** (lets index-fund investors pick their own
  proxy-voting policy/advisor) expanded to **$1.4T of S&P 500 fund
  assets** (Pensions & Investments, 2025-10-21), added infrastructure
  vendor BetaNXT (2026-04-29), and launched a **UK pilot for
  professional investors** (Citywire, 2026-01-06). (medium-high)
- **2026-02-26 — the clearest data point**: Vanguard settled a
  Republican-state-led antitrust suit (Texas et al.) alleging it,
  BlackRock, and State Street used ESG-driven voting to suppress coal
  production, for **$29.5M**, and made **"renewed passivity pledges"**
  (Reuters headline; WSJ, ESG Dive, Investment Executive corroborate the
  settlement and amount). Exact commitment text not resolved past
  headline level this pass **(thin)** — worth a direct-source follow-up
  if this becomes a live thread. (medium — settlement fact high,
  pledge specifics thin)
- **Not found this pass**: any specific record of how Vanguard voted its
  hyperscaler stakes (Microsoft, Google/Alphabet, Amazon, Meta, Nvidia)
  on AI-governance shareholder proposals in the 2026 proxy season.
  General coverage exists of shareholders "pressing Big Tech over AI
  energy use," but nothing ties a Vanguard vote to it. **(gap, flag
  thin — needs proxy-statement-level research, not news search, to
  close)**.
- Read: Vanguard is *legally recommitting* to not using its votes for
  activist ends — it paid $29.5M this year specifically to keep not
  voting its enormous stakes toward outcomes (coal, and by extension
  presumably AI-risk oversight). That is the abstention made explicit
  and priced.

## 5. Leadership — Ramji, hands-on inside a narrow lane

- **CEO Salim Ramji** (since 2024) recruited senior hires from
  Goldman Sachs and BlackRock for key roles (Bloomberg, 2026-02-05).
- **2026-06-16**: personally absorbed the 401(k)/workplace-retirement
  chief's duties — "gets more hands-on" (RIABiz). (medium)
- Reported plan to split **$10.4T** across two distinct fund-management
  teams — figure and structure not independently corroborated beyond one
  source this pass **(thin)**.
- **The one real reversal, and it's crypto, not AI**: dropped its
  long-standing ban and opened **bitcoin/ETH/XRP/SOL ETF trading** to
  its **~50M brokerage clients** (Dec 2025), then opened a search for a
  dedicated **"digital assets leader"** (2026-07-07) — described in
  coverage as a "180° reversal" after "years fighting crypto." (high)
- Public framing unchanged: "investing should feel like saving, not
  gambling" — the plain-index ethos persists even as the product
  aperture (crypto, private-markets access) widens.
