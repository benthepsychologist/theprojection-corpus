## 1. The thing this thread hasn't had yet: how the bet is actually funded

The existing record has the $40B OpenAI bridge loan's lenders and rate.
What it's missing is where SoftBank is getting the *other* cash it has
committed — and the answer is a systematic sell-down of everything that
isn't the AI stack:

- **SoftBank sold its entire Nvidia stake — 32.1 million shares — for
  $5.83B**, disclosed in its November 11, 2025 investor presentation
  (shares actually sold in October 2025). Proceeds were explicitly
  earmarked to help fund the (then) $22.5B OpenAI investment tranche.
  ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/11/11/softbank-sells-its-entire-stake-in-nvidia-for-5point83-billion.html), [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/softbank-exits-nvidia-with-5-83-billion-sale), [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/11/11/softbank-nvidia-openai-masayoshi-son-sam-altman-investment/)) (medium-high, 3+ convergent outlets)
- **SoftBank also sold part of its T-Mobile stake for $9.17B**, same
  funding round of disclosures. (medium, same cluster)
- **A self-imposed 25% loan-to-value limit is constraining how fast
  SoftBank can raise more cash against what it still holds** — reported
  as a real ceiling on the margin-loan route, not just a policy
  statement. (medium, Reuters-sourced via aggregator)
- **CreditSights (independent credit research) sizes a $32B funding gap
  over the next two years**, combining bond maturities with signed deals
  SoftBank still owes cash on — the $5.375B ABB Robotics acquisition
  (below) named specifically as one of the obligations inside that
  number. ([ad-hoc-news.de](https://www.ad-hoc-news.de/boerse/news/ueberblick/softbank-s-infrastructure-bet-creates-a-32-billion-funding-gap/69276518), cited via search; original WebFetch to this URL returned HTTP 410) (medium)
- **$30B in obligations fall due specifically in the second half of
  2026** — this is very likely the same money as the bridge loan's
  scheduled 2026-10-01 tranche already on the thread, but sourced here
  independently as a standalone "what analysts are demanding specifics
  on" figure ahead of the Aug 6 print. (medium)

**Read:** the $40B bridge loan the thread already tracks is not
SoftBank's only liquidity lever — it's one piece of a broader
liquidate-the-old-portfolio-to-fund-the-new-bet program that also
includes the full Nvidia exit and a partial T-Mobile exit. The "everything
collateralizes everything else" framing already on the thread understates
it slightly: SoftBank isn't just pledging assets, it's actively selling
some of them.

## 2. A genuinely new front: the "Physical AI" pivot (ABB Robotics + Roze)

None of the six threads that tag `softbank` as an entity carry this — it's
a real gap, not a duplicate:

- **SoftBank agreed to acquire ABB's robotics division for $5.375B**
  (≈¥818.7B), board-approved 2025-09-22, agreement executed 2025-10-08.
  ABB carves the unit into a new Zürich-headquartered holding company;
  SoftBank buys 100% of it. Expected close is **mid-to-late 2026**,
  pending EU/China/US regulatory approval. The unit employs ~7,000 people
  and generated $2.279B revenue in FY2024.
  ([SoftBank Group press release](https://group.softbank/en/news/press/20251008), direct fetch, high confidence)
  SoftBank's own framing: *"Together with ABB Robotics, we will unite
  world-class technology and talent under our shared vision to fuse
  Artificial Super Intelligence and robotics."*
- **The acquisition funds into a new venture called Roze** — a
  US-incorporated, US-listed company being spun out of SoftBank Group,
  bundling SoftBank's energy/land/digital-infrastructure assets with the
  ABB Robotics hardware to build data centers using autonomous robots
  instead of conventional construction labor. SoftBank is targeting an
  IPO **as early as second half of 2026**, at a reported target valuation
  of **up to $100B**; KPMG has been engaged to prepare the IPO financials,
  and an analyst day was held in July 2026 at a Texas data center to
  build investor interest.
  ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/sectors/technology/articles/softbank-plans-100b-u-ipo-115700056.html), [Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/ipos/26/04/52168304/softbank-eyes-2026-ipo-for-new-ai-robotics-venture-targets-up-to-100-billion-valuation-report), [Tech Startups](https://techstartups.com/2026/04/30/softbank-targets-100b-u-s-ipo-for-ai-and-robotics-startup-roze/)) (medium, multi-outlet convergent, first reported 2026-04-30)

**Read:** this is a fourth lever alongside OpenAI/Arm/Stargate that the
existing thread doesn't carry at all — a bet that AI-driven physical
infrastructure buildout (robots literally constructing data centers) is
itself investable as a standalone asset, timed to list in the same window
the OpenAI bridge loan and Stargate financing are stacking up. It also
adds a fifth debt-adjacent obligation ($5.375B) into the funding-gap
picture above.

## 3. Vision Fund broader performance — real diversification, or one bet wearing a fund wrapper

This was an explicit gap flagged going into this crawl, and the answer is
fairly unambiguous:

- **Full fiscal year 2025 (year to March 2026): Vision Fund posted a
  ~$46B gain, driven mainly by the OpenAI stake.**
  ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/05/13/softbank-earnings-fy-2025-vision-fund-openai-stake.html), cited via search synthesis — direct WebFetch blocked, 403) (medium)
- **Fiscal Q4 2025 (Jan-Mar 2026) alone: ~$20B gain, "nearly all driven by
  OpenAI," while SoftBank explicitly took losses elsewhere in the same
  quarter — named laggards: Coupang, DiDi Global, Klarna.** (medium, same
  source cluster)
- **Fiscal Q3 2025 (Oct-Dec 2025): $4.2B OpenAI mark-up in the December
  quarter alone; $17B cumulative OpenAI gain across the April-December
  2025 period.** ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/02/12/softbank-vision-fund-openai.html), cited via search) (medium)
- **A separate markdown hit landed on the ByteDance/TikTok stake** in the
  same reporting stretch — named explicitly as a portfolio drag alongside
  Coupang/DiDi/Klarna. (medium)
- **CFO Yoshimitsu Goto's own framing: ~60% of SoftBank's total assets are
  now "ASI-oriented investments."** (medium, quoted via search synthesis)

**Read:** this directly answers the "is Vision Fund a genuine hedge or
window dressing" question this crawl was asked to check. It is not a
diversified fund generating a diversified return — OpenAI alone is
carrying the fund's headline gains quarter after quarter, while several
of its largest non-AI legacy positions (Coupang, DiDi, Klarna, ByteDance)
are actively losing value in the same periods. The "Vision Fund" wrapper
still exists organizationally, but the P&L concentration is now
functionally identical to a single-position bet — consistent with, and
now quantified beyond, what the thread's `genre: hedge` annotation already
calls "the un-hedge, actually."

Vision Fund **1** specifically is not adding new material to this
picture — it appears to be in a long-tail realization/exit phase (its
investment period ended in 2019 at ~85% capital deployed) rather than a
live driver of the current concentration story. (thin — no fresh
2026-dated VF1-specific performance figure was found this crawl; treat
the current-year story as entirely a VF2 story.)

## 4. Credit ratings are diverging, not falling in step

Not on the thread at all — a live disagreement between the two major
agencies:

- **Moody's upgraded SoftBank Group Corp to Ba2 from Ba3 (senior
  unsecured to Ba2, subordinated to B1), outlook revised stable, on
  2025-09-17** — explicitly citing sustained leverage reduction, reduced
  secured debt, and improved transparency.
  ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-09-17/moody-s-upgrades-softbank-s-debt-rating-on-credit-fundamentals), [Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/softbank-group-upgraded-to-ba2-by-moodys-outlook-stable-93CH-4242809)) (medium-high)
- **S&P moved the opposite direction in March 2026: outlook revised to
  negative** (no downgrade), explicitly over doubts about SoftBank's
  "financial soundness and creditworthiness" discipline while running an
  up-to-¥2.5 trillion (~$24B) share buyback program at the same time it's
  raising up to $41B via asset sales to fund the AI buildout.
  ([S&P Global Ratings](https://www.spglobal.com/ratings/en/regulatory/article/-/view/sourceId/101673310), cited via search synthesis — direct WebFetch blocked, 403) (medium)

**Read:** the two main rating agencies read the same balance sheet and
reached opposite conclusions about direction of travel — Moody's crediting
past deleveraging, S&P flagging the buyback-while-borrowing pattern as a
live discipline problem. Worth carrying both, not just one.

## 5. How the market is actually pricing this, going into the Aug 6 print

- **SoftBank stock is down roughly 50% since early June 2026** — a
  striking number given the Vision Fund gains reported over the same
  broad window; this is the clearest single data point that the market
  is pricing funding/concentration risk ahead of portfolio marks. (medium,
  multiple convergent outlets)
- **A "beat-and-drop" pattern across the last three reported quarters**:
  SoftBank beat EPS consensus by +103.4%, +32.2%, and +407.6% in
  successive quarters, and the stock fell on the day of each report
  anyway (-1.55%, -6.70%, -3.62%) — read by the reporting outlet as
  investors front-running the portfolio markups pre-earnings and selling
  the confirmation.
  ([Investing.com Q1 preview](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/softbank-q1-earnings-preview-low-bar-arm-risk-and-the-beatanddrop-pattern-93CH-4832632), direct fetch) (medium — note the outlet's own fiscal-quarter labels look internally inconsistent; treat the pattern as real, the specific quarter labels as approximate)
- **Deutsche Bank downgraded SoftBank to Hold from Buy**, citing limited
  further upside, yen-depreciation dynamics, and specifically OpenAI
  valuation-durability concerns inside the portfolio. (medium, same
  source)
- **Credit default swap costs on SoftBank debt have risen** — a
  market-priced increase in perceived default risk, reported alongside
  the stock decline and lender reluctance on the OpenAI-stake margin loan
  the thread already tracks as stalled. (medium)
- **A technical "dead cross" formed 2026-07-28** (25-day moving average
  crossing below the 75-day) and **SoftBank fell 2.6% on 2026-07-30 in
  sympathy with Arm's smartphone-royalty warning** — this sits alongside,
  and slightly predates, the thread's existing 08-01 note on Arm's
  post-earnings slide and SoftBank's 3.2% same-week drop; treat as the
  same broader Arm-sympathy move the thread already has, not a second
  distinct event.
- **Arm ownership, primary-sourced: Arm's own SEC 6-K filing puts
  SoftBank's beneficial ownership at approximately 86.4% as of
  2026-05-21** ([SEC EDGAR](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001973239/000197323926000114/arm-20260630.htm)) — close to, but
  measurably below, the 87.1% figure currently on the board; secondary
  sources in the same window range from 86-90%. Worth a board refresh
  to the SEC-filed 86.4% figure specifically, since it's the one primary
  source found. (medium-high — primary filing, but a single as-of date)

## 7. Son's own framing — "Izanagi" and the ASI mission

- **SoftBank has branded its current AI strategy internally/publicly as
  "Izanagi"** (named in coverage of a 2026-02-26 stock surge tied to the
  strategy). (medium, single-outlet — worth confirming against a second
  source before treating as a fixed proper-noun campaign name)
- **At a SoftBank shareholder meeting, Son stated: "SoftBank's mission is
  to drive human evolution and realize ASI (Artificial Super
  Intelligence)"** and separately predicted ASI would become **"10,000
  times smarter than humans within a decade."** (medium, quoted via
  search synthesis of shareholder-meeting coverage — exact date of the
  meeting not independently pinned down this crawl)

**Read:** Son's own public register frames the concentration not as risk
management gone wrong but as a deliberate, mission-scale bet — "ASI" as
an explicit endpoint, not a product roadmap. This is consistent with,
and gives voice to, the board's existing gloss ("the largest single
lever into the stack") and the thread's own `genre: hedge` annotation
calling it "the un-hedge."
