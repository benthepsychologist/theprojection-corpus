## The cross-pocket payoff (why the axis model earns its keep)

- **Optionality is the axis that carves the money layer.** Index giants
  (BlackRock/Vanguard/State Street/Fidelity), every insurer bar Berkshire, and the
  China champions all command $100B–$15T of **constrained** capital. **Free**
  deployers are rare: Berkshire, SoftBank, Musk. Size doesn't separate them —
  freedom does.
- **Commands-vs-owns is a signature ratio.** Vanguard ∞ (owns ~$0), State Street
  ~100–190×, BlackRock ~90×, Prudential ~36× — vs operating cos and SoftBank ~1×.
  A high ratio = a capital director; ~1× = a capital owner.
- **Gravity ≠ capitalization, repeatedly.** Arm (tiny capital, ~$1T+ gravity) and
  BlackRock/insurers (huge capital, thin gravity) are opposite extremes; Nvidia/TSMC
  gravity ≫ capital; **Berkshire is the one actor where gravity ≈ capital** (a real
  operating conglomerate, not a pool).
- **The infra layer has chokepoints the money/power layers don't:** TSMC (fabs,
  ~90% of leading-edge), SK Hynix (HBM), Arm (the ISA under ~99% of phones),
  Nvidia (accelerators). Single points of failure = outsized sovereignty.
- **State capital saturates infra:** CHIPS money in every US fab, a US equity stake
  in Intel, Mubadala owning GlobalFoundries, CXMT state-directed. The chips/foundry
  pockets are half state-capital already.
