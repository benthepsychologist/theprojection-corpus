# Q4 — Does the evidence machine hold? (research memo, 2026-08-07)

Standing question: who referees the mental-health evidence base — funders, guideline
bodies, digital-health evaluators — and can that referee infrastructure keep producing?
This memo extends the corpus's therapy-science crawl (`ebp-crawl-therapy-science.md`
§8) and the manuscript-workshop book at `/workspace/the-evidence-gap-src` (read-only;
cited, not re-researched) with what's new as of 2026-08-07, focused on six named
follow-ups: the Done Global sentencing, the NIH/NIMH funding-cut litigation, PHTI's
institutional state, CHAI's post-pivot output, the NICE/APA guideline fights, and
Cochrane Global Mental Health's actual output.

## TL;DR

- **Done Global sentencing is confirmed and it moved.** The April 28, 2026 date the
  book had as unconfirmed was itself continued to **July 7, 2026**. That day, Judge
  Charles Breyer sentenced founder/CEO **Ruthia He to 72 months (6 years) + a $1
  million fine**, and clinical president **David Brody to 24 months (2 years) + a $1
  million fine** — the first federal criminal sentences ever imposed on digital
  mental-health-company executives, closing the trial-court-to-sentencing arc DOJ
  opened with the June 2024 arrests.
- **The NIH funding fight didn't resolve — it fractured into two tracks, and the
  budget-level threat evaporated.** Grant-by-grant litigation is still open (a First
  Circuit appeal was argued January 6, 2026, after the Supreme Court's August 21,
  2025 jurisdictional ruling shunted grant-termination claims to the Court of Federal
  Claims, which can pay damages but can't force reinstatement) — but Congress
  **rejected** the administration's proposed 27-institutes-to-8 consolidation and the
  $47B→$29B NIH cut outright in the FY2026 appropriations law signed February 3, 2026.
  The structural collapse the book's "institutional dissolution" reading depends on
  did not happen at the budget level; the individual-grant fight is still live.
- **PHTI has gone quiet on mental health specifically.** No 2026 follow-up to the
  May 2025 *Virtual Solutions for Depression and Anxiety* report (V1.2, September
  2025) was found; PHTI's most recent 2026 output is a cross-cutting *Payment for
  Clinical AI* report, and its next named assessment is GI conditions (targeted March
  2026). No published horizon date exists anywhere for the Peterson Foundation's "$50
  million" commitment — every source, including PHTI's own site, calls it "initial"
  or "seed" funding with no end date stated.
- **CHAI answered the book's open "will it credential?" question, and the answer is
  a deliberate no.** On July 23, 2026, CHAI's Mental Health Chatbot work group
  (Headspace, Mental Health America, National Health Council, Stanford, others)
  published a Best Practice Guide and a Testing & Evaluation Framework specifically
  for generative-AI wellness/mental-health apps — CHAI's first concrete 2026 output
  in the mental-health lane — and it explicitly is **not** a certification or
  pass/fail standard. This is the clearest evidence yet for the book's "capture
  deferred, not resolved" reading of the September 2025 pivot.
- **The guideline fights are still open and asymmetric.** NICE's anxiety-guideline
  campaign escalated (BACP added its backing in January 2026, on top of the March
  2026 stakeholder and cross-party letters) with no NICE response found yet; APA's
  depression-guideline update panel (seated 2025) has produced no output as of its
  most recently updated page. Cochrane Global Mental Health, by contrast, is
  demonstrably active and not just a standing mission statement — Corrado Barbui and
  Marianna Purgato's group published in *The Lancet Regional Health – Europe*
  (October 2025) and the *International Journal of Social Psychiatry* (February
  2026).

## What changed 2021→2026

In 2021 the referee layer for mental-health evidence was two separate, mostly
disconnected worlds. On the clinical-trial side, NICE, APA, and Cochrane were the
long-standing guideline/synthesis bodies, and NIH/NIMH funding was assumed stable
infrastructure nobody had to argue about. On the digital-health side, there was no
referee at all — FDA's Pre-Cert pilot was a company-audit experiment that hadn't yet
been declared dead, and vendors like Cerebral, Pear, and Akili raised money and went
public on self-reported efficacy claims nobody outside the company verified before a
deal closed (`chapter-1.md` Act 1–2).

By 2026 both worlds changed, but not symmetrically. The digital-health referee layer
that didn't exist in 2021 now does: PHTI launched (June 2023), published a
buyer-facing mental-health assessment (May 2025), and CHAI pivoted from a failed
assurance-lab model to an infrastructure-and-guidance role that just produced its
first real mental-health-specific output (July 2026). The enforcement layer that
began with state-AG and FTC settlements in March 2023 escalated all the way to actual
federal prison sentences in July 2026 — Done Global's He and Brody. That's a genuine,
verifiable maturation of the "does anyone check the claim" infrastructure the book's
Chapter 1 traces.

The traditional guideline/funding layer, by contrast, moved from "stable" to
"actively contested and financially threatened" over the same period. NICE and APA
guideline processes that used to be background infrastructure are now the subject of
open methodological fights (the EMDR/PTSD critique, the NICE anxiety campaign). And
the biggest single change of the period is the 2025 NIH/NIMH grant-termination wave —
a shock to the funding base that produces essentially every RCT and meta-analysis
cited anywhere in this corpus's therapy-science tracking. That shock is still
resolving in 2026, on two separate and now-diverging tracks: litigation (still
contested, still unresolved) and appropriations (resolved, in the field's favor, in
February 2026).

## Current state of the dispute

**Enforcement:** resolved, at least for this case. Done Global's sentences (He: 6
years + $1M; Brody: 2 years + $1M) are final as of July 7, 2026, closing the arc the
book uses as its Chapter 1 Act 4 bookend. This doesn't end the broader enforcement
story — Hims & Hers litigation and the Zealthy asset-freeze (both already in the
book's Act 5) continue — but the single most-anticipated verified data point in this
research task is now a documented fact rather than a placeholder.

**Funding:** split-decision, and still moving. The Supreme Court's August 21, 2025
ruling in *NIH v. American Public Health Association* was a fractured 4-1-4
jurisdictional decision (not a merits ruling): district courts can hear challenges to
NIH's termination *guidance*, but claims seeking actual grant restoration belong in
the Court of Federal Claims under the Tucker Act — a forum that can award money
damages but has no power to order a grant reinstated. Justice Jackson's dissent
called this a "bizarre claim-splitting regime" that could leave plaintiffs with no
effective remedy at all. The First Circuit heard the guidance-side appeal January 6,
2026; no ruling was found as of this research. Meanwhile, at the appropriations
level, Congress flatly rejected the administration's proposed structural overhaul
(27-institute consolidation to 8, budget cut to $29B) in the FY2026 spending law
signed February 3, 2026 — NIH's current 27-institute structure and roughly its
funding level survived intact, even as the administration's FY2027 request (reported
via Becker's Hospital Review) proposes further cuts. Net read: the field dodged the
structural-collapse scenario for now, but the grant-by-grant fight — and with it,
individual labs' ability to actually spend money already awarded — remains unresolved
eighteen months after the terminations began.

**Digital-health evaluators:** active but incomplete. PHTI exists, has published, and
is institutionally durable enough to be issuing cross-cutting reports (Payment for
Clinical AI) beyond its original per-category assessment model — but it has gone
quiet specifically on mental health since the September 2025 V1.2 update, with no
2026 follow-up found and no vendor-share/procurement-shift data yet available (the
book's Chapter 3 Act 1 "convergence question" — its answer is not yet visible in the
2026 record). CHAI's credentialing question, explicitly left open by the book as
"record-dependency," now has a concrete 2026 answer: guidance and testing frameworks,
not certification. That's a real answer, and it points toward the book's "capture
deferred" reading rather than "capture resolved" — CHAI did something in 2026, but
what it did was calibrated to avoid ever becoming the gating body its original
Assurance Labs proposal would have made it.

**Guideline bodies:** unresolved on both fronts the book flagged, with one correction
to the book's own implicit picture. Cochrane Global Mental Health is not merely a
2017-vintage mission statement — it has a functioning, currently-publishing research
program under Barbui and Purgato at Verona, with output landing in 2025 and 2026.
That's worth noting because the ebp-crawl file's §8 treatment ("standing critique...
datedness, narrow evidence-type inclusion") reads Cochrane mental-health work mostly
through the lens of critique; the Global Mental Health branch specifically is more
active than that framing implies.

4. **Chapter 3 Act 3's "record-dependency" on 2026 CHAI credentialing decisions is
