# Q9 — Where is the money landing, who are the players? (research memo, 2026-08-07)

Scope: the BUYER side of clinical-evidence evaluation in mental-health technology — who pays for and consumes expert judgment about whether a digital-MH/AI-MH product actually works, not who sells the products. Frame is the 10-pocket buyer taxonomy from `/workspace/the-evidence-gap-src/outlines/chapter-2.md` (book material, read-only). Cross-checked against what `theprojection-corpus` currently tracks in `attention/watchlist.yaml` and `attention/board.yaml`. Research: WebFetch-first (Rock Health, PHTI, PBGH, CHAI, ARPA-H, Wellcome), 6 WebSearch calls (PE roll-ups, benefits-consultant/PHTI ties, AI-liability insurance, CHAI governance, Character.AI litigation, PHTI council membership — the last returned no result, session search budget exhausted at that point).

## TL;DR

- **Money is flowing to clinician-staffing, not chatbots.** H1 2026's two largest mental-health venture deals — Talkiatry ($210M) and Grow Therapy ($150M), both human-clinician marketplaces — together outraised the two leading AI-safety-oversight startups (Jimini Health $17M, The Path $14.3M) by roughly 14-to-1. Mental health has been the single top-funded digital-health clinical category for seven straight years, but the money inside it is buying human clinicians augmented by software, not autonomous chatbots.
- **Two buyer pockets that barely existed in 2021 are now the fastest-growing: private equity clinical due diligence, and AI-liability insurance underwriting.** PE closed 56 behavioral-health deals in 2025 (up 47% year-over-year through Q3), and a wave of carriers (Chubb, Berkley, AIG, W.R. Berkley, Great American) started writing explicit generative-AI exclusions into D&O/E&O/fiduciary policies in January 2026 — a ~$6.8B global AI-liability-insurance segment that functionally didn't exist as a distinct line five years ago.
- **The litigation bar pocket just closed its first chapter:** Character.AI and Google settled five wrongful-death/harm lawsuits confidentially in January 2026, ending (for now) the highest-profile AI-mental-health court test without a public verdict on liability standards — which means the legal question of what counts as adequate AI-MH safety remains formally unresolved even though the highest-profile case is over.
- **The feed's watchlist has real, structural blind spots on the buyer side.** Of the taxonomy's 10 buyer pockets, `theprojection-corpus` has direct entity coverage for only 3–4 (VC funding rounds via themes, PHTI/ICER/ORCHA/CHAI as evaluators, payers as generic health-pocket board entries, one foundation cluster tracked under `global-capital` rather than mental-health). It has **zero** entity coverage for: PE clinical-DD firms doing behavioral-health roll-ups, benefits consultants (Mercer/Aon/WTW), employer coalitions beyond PBGH, the litigation bar, AI-liability insurance underwriters, and investigative-media buyers.
- **Federal-as-methodology-buyer is contracting hard while everyone else's engagement model matures.** NIMH lost 128 grants in the 2025 termination wave (worst-hit NIH institute), 383 NIH-funded clinical trials were terminated, and the FY2026 budget proposes cutting NIH from $47B to $29B while collapsing 27 institutes into 8 — the pocket that funded most of the underlying evidence base other buyers now evaluate is the one visibly shrinking.

## Current state (detail behind the table)

**VC/CVC and the clinician-vs-chatbot money split (Rock Health, H1 2026 report).** Digital health overall: $10.5B (2024) → $14.2B (2025, +35% YoY, per Rock Health's "a tale of two markets" framing — the market is bifurcating into haves and have-nots) → Q1 2026 $4B across 110 deals (59% into just 12 mega-deals) → H1 2026 $7.4B across 244 deals. Mental health has been the **top-funded clinical indication for seven consecutive years**. Inside H1 2026's mental-health total, the two anchor mega-deals were **Talkiatry ($210M)** and **Grow Therapy ($150M)** — both are human-clinician staffing/marketplace models, not AI-native chatbot companies. The AI-safety-oversight sub-category (companies explicitly building clinician-oversight or third-party safety evaluation into AI tools) raised an order of magnitude less: **Jimini Health ($17M)** and **The Path ($14.3M)**. Rock Health's own framing: "dedicated mental health chatbots pale in comparison" to organic consumer adoption of general-purpose frontier-lab chatbots for MH conversations, which are "increasingly incorporating safeguards" themselves — meaning the safety burden for AI-in-MH is partly migrating to Anthropic/OpenAI/Google rather than staying inside dedicated MH-AI vendors. Also notable: 64% of mental-health startups that raised in H1 2026 sell direct-to-consumer, vs. 29% for digital health broadly — the category still skews consumer-facing even as the biggest dollars go to clinician-augmented delivery.

**PE clinical due diligence — the sharpest "new buyer" story.** Multiple independent trackers (Private Equity Stakeholder Project, CT Acquisitions, Stoneridge Partners) converge on the same shape: 56 behavioral-health acquisitions in 2025, a 47% YoY increase in deal count through Q3 2025 (75 deals), and 2026 guidance pointing to platform acquisitions trading at 8–12x EBITDA for "quality programs with commercial-payer dominance and clean compliance histories." IDD/autism (ABA therapy) and substance-use-disorder treatment are the strongest sub-segments, both valued by PE for Medicaid-funded, recession-resistant revenue. This matches the book's Act 3 claim that the $5–25K VC memo becomes a $40–150K PE clinical-due-diligence workstream — the PE pocket is real and active, but this pass did not find fresh 2025-26 confirmation of the specific named firms (Bain Capital Health, KKR, Welsh Carson, Vistria, Linden, Frazier, Gryphon, Avista) doing behavioral-health-specific deals; the tracker data is aggregate, not firm-attributed.

**AI-liability insurance underwriters — genuinely new activity, not speculative.** January 2026: ISO (Insurance Services Office, the standard-setting body for US P&C policy language) issued three new generative-AI exclusions for commercial general liability policies. Within weeks, Berkley Insurance and Chubb began cutting AI-related coverage across D&O, E&O, fiduciary, and corporate policy lines; AIG, W.R. Berkley, and Great American are separately seeking regulatory clearance for AI exclusions targeting the same lines. The global AI-liability-insurance segment is now sized at ~$6.8B, with rate increases up to 15% hitting exposed organizations. Coverage is shifting from comprehensive to modular/narrow-trigger, and regulatory compliance is becoming a coverage prerequisite rather than just a pricing input — the EU AI Act's high-risk obligations for AI used in life/health risk-assessment and pricing become fully operational August 2026. This is the clearest evidence in this pass of insurance actually pricing AI-MH-adjacent risk as a product line, not just discussing it in the abstract — though none of the reporting found in this pass named mental-health-specific exclusions or products; the activity is AI-liability-general, with MH as one exposed category among many.

**Litigation bar — the marquee case just closed, quietly.** Character.AI and Google settled five wrongful-death/severe-harm lawsuits (Florida, Colorado, New York, Texas) confidentially in January 2026, with no admission of liability. These stemmed from the October 2024 Megan Garcia/Sewell Setzer III case, the first AI-chatbot wrongful-death suit filed in the US. The settlements close the highest-profile test case without setting a public liability standard for AI-mental-health harm — meaning the litigation bar's engagement model (contingency-fee plaintiff firms plus retained defense-side clinical experts) is active and well-funded, but the case law that would let insurers, PE due-diligence teams, and health-system governance committees price the actual legal risk still doesn't exist.

**Health-system AI governance committees — the fastest-standardizing pocket.** The Joint Commission and CHAI (Coalition for Health AI) released initial joint guidance in September 2025. CHAI followed with eight full governance playbooks on May 27, 2026, built from community workshops involving 150+ health-AI leaders, covering organizational AI policy, risk assessment, data management, and third-party oversight. The Joint Commission is building a voluntary AI certification off the finalized playbook set — meaning the health-system pocket is moving from "every hospital improvising its own committee" (book's Act 4 framing) toward a shared, semi-official standard within about 8 months of the initial guidance. This is the pocket most likely to become the dominant reference standard other buyers (PE due-diligence teams, insurers) cite.

**Employer coalitions and benefits consultants — could not confirm the book's Act 2 framing has kept pace.** PBGH's public-facing 2025-26 work (CalHIVE behavioral-health-integration collaborative, a 2024-25 Children and Youth Collaborative Learning Exchange, a 2024 behavioral-health-financing landscape assessment) is focused on integrating behavioral health into primary care delivery, not on refreshing its 2021 digital-MH vendor-evaluation framework for the AI era — this pass found no 2025-26 PBGH publication specifically evaluating AI-MH vendors. The benefits-consultant transmission belt (Mercer/Aon/WTW publicly adopting PHTI methodology) could **not be confirmed either way** in this pass: PHTI's own site references a "Purchaser Advisory Council" but does not publicly name its members, and general web search on Mercer/Aon/WTW + PHTI returned no direct evidence of formal integration. This should be read as an open question, not a negative finding — the underlying pages (PHTI's council membership list) were not fetchable in a form this pass could read.

**Federal-as-methodology-buyer — the pocket funding everyone else's evidence base is contracting.** Per the sibling evidence crawl (`therapy-science.md` in this session's scratchpad), NIMH lost 128 grants in the February–August 2025 NIH termination wave — the hardest-hit NIH institute — with 383 NIH-funded clinical trials terminated affecting over 74,000 participants, and the FY2026 budget proposal would cut total NIH funding from $47B to $29B while consolidating 27 institutes into 8. ARPA-H's EVIDENT program page returned a 404 in this pass (could not verify current 2025-26 funding status directly), though the book's outline names it as a $139M behavioral-health initiative. This is a structural risk worth flagging on its own: several other buyer pockets (foundations, PE due diligence, health-system governance committees) implicitly lean on the federally-funded evidence base for their own evaluations, and that base is visibly shrinking at the same time those other pockets are maturing and demanding more of it.

## What the book already establishes

`/workspace/the-evidence-gap-src/outlines/chapter-2.md` (Chapter 2 outline, working titles "Who's Buying the Read" / "The Ten Pockets" / "The Buyers in the Gap") frames this exact question as the chapter's entire subject: **the buyer landscape for clinical-evidence evaluation in mental-health technology, 2017–2026.** It structures the 10 pockets on a historical arc (six "acts" retracing the same chronology as Chapter 1), introducing each pocket at the point it became evaluable as a market rather than presenting the taxonomy cold:

- **Act 1 (2017-19):** VC/CVC, federal-as-methodology-buyer (Pre-Cert/AHRQ EPC/SAMHSA/early CMS Innovation Center)
- **Act 2 (2020-21):** Payers/PBMs, employer purchaser coalitions (PBGH's Jan 2021 framework, NEBGH, Midwest BGH, National Alliance/HR Policy)
- **Act 3 (2022-23):** State AGs/FTC (NY AG-Cerebral, FTC-BetterHelp/Cerebral), PE clinical DD (as VC retreats), litigation bar
- **Act 4 (2024-25):** Foundations (Peterson→PHTI $50M seed, Wellcome, Pivotal, McGovern, Ballmer, Hopelab, Macy, Commonwealth), federal subcontractor channels (ARPA-H EVIDENT $139M, federal primes Mathematica/Westat/RTI/Abt), health-system AI governance committees (post-JC+CHAI)
- **Act 5-6 (2025-26):** Investigative media, insurance underwriters, plus a chatbot-wave revisit of all 10 pockets

The book also names an **11th, non-buyer "evaluator/methodologist" reference layer** — PHTI, ICER, KLAS, ECRI, ORCHA, MIND/PsyberGuide, academic centers (BIDMC Digital Psychiatry, Schueller/UC Irvine), and consulting boutiques (Avalere, Trinity, Health Advances) — inputs other buyers reference rather than buyers themselves. The chapter's closing frame: the 2026 buyer map is "a 10-pocket landscape, most of which didn't exist in evaluable form in 2017," and the live opportunity has shifted from generic "evaluation is needed" advocacy to serving specific buyers at specific moments. This memo's research confirms that frame is still accurate as of August 2026 — if anything the PE and insurance-underwriter pockets (both "just becoming evaluable" in the outline) are now the two moving fastest.
