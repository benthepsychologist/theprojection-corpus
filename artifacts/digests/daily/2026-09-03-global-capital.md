## Today's throughline

A sitting Fed governor split from his chair in public and the long end
came back down. Christopher Waller said he would hold in September "if
disinflation continues" — "give disinflation a chance, we can wait one
meeting" — and CME's September-hike odds fell roughly twelve points to
about 54.6% by late morning, with lower prints circulating into the
afternoon; the 10-year eased to ~4.75% from Wednesday's 4.79% close and
equities rallied more than a percent on the relief. The data underneath
argued both ways again and was not what moved anything: claims 206,000,
ISM services 55.4 with its employment index contracting at 47.8, a $88.6bn
trade deficit. Tomorrow's payrolls print is the first data Waller named,
and it is now on the ledger.

after crossing 3% on Tuesday, but the yen kept surging — into the mid-150s
per multiple outlets — as Bessent's pressure on Tokyo got specific ("also
rate hikes," to both Katayama and Ueda, per NHK) and the 09-18 BOJ meeting
became the next dated event on this lens. And oil whipsawed: Brent from a
$95.38 close through $99.38 at 8am on fresh Iranian strikes on Kuwait and
the UAE, back to ~$95 by afternoon as Washington's barrel-count claims and
Kpler's ship-count data kept diverging. On the deal tape, Nvidia's $12.93bn
Hugging Face 8-K, Moonshot's confidential Hong Kong filing, and Broadcom
