## Today's throughline

**The most consequential thing said about AI today was said by the
chairman of the Federal Reserve, and it was mostly questions.** In his
first Jackson Hole keynote, Kevin Warsh described AI as "a new
variable—potentially a new factor of production," said progress "has been
faster even than its evangelists predicted," and put **annualised token
sales for the two leading labs alone at more than $100 billion, up more
than 500% in a year** — a figure he attributes to "reports," not to Fed
data. **What makes it notable is not the number but the venue.** The
questions he then set out are the ones this lens exists to track: whether
AI produces a sustained productivity rise and when, whether tokens
complement or substitute for labour, whether the next model generation
demands more capital intensity or less, and where the surplus lands
between "AI labs, chipmakers, energy producers, and cloud providers." A
Fed task force is on it, and he was careful to say its findings will not
touch near-term policy. **The central bank has started modelling the AI
buildout as a macroeconomic object rather than a sector story.**

**⚠️ Added on the 15:10 ET run, and it displaces the above as the day's
real lead.** A federal judge **permanently enjoined the Pentagon's
blacklisting of Anthropic**, ruling that designating the company a
national-security supply-chain risk was retaliation for its criticism of
the government. The dispute is about two usage restrictions Anthropic
refused to drop in contract renegotiation — **a ban on using Claude for
mass domestic surveillance of Americans, and a ban on fully autonomous
lethal weapons.** That is the most direct test yet of whether a frontier
lab can hold a safety red line against its own government and survive
commercially, and a court has now said it can. **This map missed it for
sixteen hours** despite tracking Anthropic's IPO timeline in the same
digest's upcoming section; see Map changes.

## Policy & governance

- **A federal judge permanently enjoined the Pentagon from blacklisting
  Anthropic, finding the designation was retaliation for the company's
  criticism of the administration.** Judge **Rita F. Lin**, US District
  Court for the Northern District of California, ruled on **cross-motions
  for summary judgment** in *Anthropic PBC v. U.S. Department of War, et
  al.*, No. **3:26-cv-01996-RFL** — a docket this session verified
  directly against the court's own earlier order rather than taking it
  from coverage. The reported disposition: summary judgment for Anthropic
  on **First Amendment, Fifth Amendment due-process and Administrative
  Procedure Act** claims, vacating the "supply-chain risk" designation
  Secretary **Pete Hegseth** made under **10 U.S.C. § 3252**, and
  converting March's preliminary injunction into a permanent one. **The
  underlying fight is about two red lines Anthropic would not drop** in
  contract renegotiation — no mass domestic surveillance of Americans, no
  fully autonomous lethal weapons — after which the government issued a
  directive telling all federal agencies to stop using the company's
  technology. Quoted from the order across the wire coverage: *"The empty
  invocation of national security is not a blank check to punish and
  retaliate against government critics."* ⚠️ **Three things are flagged
  rather than asserted.** The quoted language traces to **a single AP wire
  story** and could not be checked against the August order itself, which
  was unreachable — what *was* read directly is the March preliminary
  injunction. **A second designation survives**: a separate challenge to
  a **41 U.S.C. § 4713** FASCSA designation is still pending in the D.C.
  Circuit (No. 26-1049) and is untouched by this ruling, so Anthropic
  remains under one of the two. And the ruling issued **Thursday evening
  08-27**, not today. Anthropic's statement: *"We remain focused on
  working productively with the government to harness AI for our national
  security."* The White House did not immediately comment.
  ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/28/judge-blocks-pentagon-blacklist--anthropic-.html),
  [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/08/28/judge-blocks-pentagon-anthropic-blacklist),
  [the March preliminary-injunction order, read directly](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cand.465515/gov.uscourts.cand.465515.135.0.pdf))

- **The administration is drafting a rule to cut off China's *remote*
  access to US chips — closing the cloud-rental path the existing export
  controls leave open.** The Information reported the effort this
  morning; follow-up coverage adds that this "cut-down AI diffusion rule"
  could be shared with industry **as soon as September 2026**. **The
  mechanism is the point**: current controls stop chips crossing a
  border, and a Chinese firm renting the same chips inside a US or
  third-country datacenter never triggers them. ⚠️ **No rule text
  exists** — this is a drafting effort reported by one outlet with no
  government document published, so scope and mechanics are unconfirmed.
  ([Tom's Hardware, ~11:47 ET, relaying The Information](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/policy/new-us-export-controls-reportedly-target-chinese-access-to-remote-ai-servers-trump-admins-cut-down-ai-diffusion-rule-could-be-shared-with-industry-as-soon-as-september))

- **Warsh's AI section is the first time this map has logged a sitting Fed
  chair framing the buildout as a factor-of-production question.** The
  substance, from the released text: a "hinge point in history," "a kind
  of hyper–Moore's law," scaling laws "changing both the method and speed
  of innovation," and an explicit open question about **market
  structure** — *"It's not obvious where the returns on capital will land
  or on what timescale."* He also asks whether token prices will
  stratify, with "growing sums paid for access to the best models at the
  frontier" while "token prices for older models fall to the level of
  their marginal cost." **That is a commodity-versus-frontier split
  thesis, stated by the Fed**, and it bears directly on the threads here
  that track whether inference revenue can carry the capex. One of five
  Fed task forces is studying productivity and jobs; Warsh states its
  recommendations "have no bearing on decisions we make in the current
  policy conjuncture."
  ([Federal Reserve, released text](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/speech/warsh20260828a.htm))

- **Five separate local fights against datacenters landed on the same
  day, and one of them redefines what counts as a datacenter at all.**
  **Cary, North Carolina**'s town council approved an **18-month
  moratorium** unanimously, and the mechanism matters more than the pause:
  it drops the threshold at which a facility counts as a "data center"
  from **25MW to 1MW** — roughly the draw of a thousand homes. **That is
  not a stricter version of the usual moratorium, it is a twenty-five-fold
  wider net**, and it catches edge and colocation builds that every other
  local ordinance this map has logged would exempt. Alongside it: a
  federal **noise class action** in **Vineland, New Jersey** against
  DataOne USA covering roughly **1,013 households within a mile**, where
  thermal imagery showed **45 of 62 generators** running in mid-August;
  Atmosphere Data Centers **withdrawing** its legal challenge to
  Montgomery County, Maryland's permitting moratorium; new opposition in
  **Wisconsin Rapids** organised around the developers' Russian corporate
  connections; and **Easton, Pennsylvania** moving to harden its zoning.
  ⚠️ **Each is single-outlet, from a named local or regional publication,
  and none was cross-verified against a second source** — the cluster is
  reported as a same-day pattern, not five independently confirmed facts.
  ([WRAL/ABC11 on Cary](https://www.wral.com),
