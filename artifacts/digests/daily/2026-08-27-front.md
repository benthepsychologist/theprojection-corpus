## 🚨 Flash

🚨 **ONE FLASH FILED — `nepal-tibet-glacier-collapse-flood`.** The first
since 08-21, and the first this map has ever filed as an explicit **late
catch**.

**What it is.** A glacier section broke away at ~5,200m on the
Nepal-China border, fell about 1,200m, dammed the Lhende Khola above the
Bhote Koshi valley and burst. The **USGS has formally reclassified its own
record** — event `us7000tbwb`, first published as a M4.4 earthquake, is
now titled a M5.2 **landslide**, with the agency stating no earthquake
occurred. Tolls were still climbing through the day: Nepal's Emergency
Operations Centre reported 165 bodies recovered and **826 missing** as of
Thursday morning, wires later put the Nepali dead above 350, and Xinhua
reports 3 dead and **558 missing** on the Tibet side. Several hundred of
the missing are foreign nationals.

**Why it clears the bar.** The test is not "is this our biggest story" —
the executive summary carries that. It is *would this lead a general news
front page on its own facts.* **The precedent settles it rather than
leaving it to taste:** `colombia-earthquake-m7-4` (281 dead, 379 missing)
took the rail on 08-11. This is larger.

**How its 24 hours are counted.** `date: 2026-08-25` (the event's own ET
date), `filed: 2026-08-27`. A late catch runs 24h **from the catch** —
being missed neither buys a fresh lifespan nor costs one. Verified against
the loader, not assumed: **it renders today and drops tomorrow.**

⛔ **The part that matters more than the flash.** Three mechanisms could
have caught this and **none of them could have.** Every one of the twelve
coverage-critic benchmarks is an AI, health-tech or finance publication,
so a natural disaster sits outside the recall guarantee **by
construction**. The mechanical world-news pool has been unbuildable for
eleven days on an expired credential. And the flash rail only fires if the
world-news sweep notices first — on 08-25 it did not. **That is a
structural gap, not a bad sweep**, and it is logged as one.
