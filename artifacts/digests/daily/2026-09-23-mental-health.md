## Research & evidence

- **A University of Illinois Chicago trial of "Coach Lumen," a
  voice-based AI coach delivering problem-solving therapy, posted results
  on ClinicalTrials.gov on 09-23: at 18 weeks, participants coached by the
  AI improved more than a delayed-treatment control, and those coached
  by a human improved somewhat more than the AI-coached group on most
  measures, in unadjusted registry means.** On the Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale's distress
  total, the mean change was −5.85 for the AI-coached arm (97 completers),
  −7.33 for human coaching (48) and −2.76 for the delayed-treatment
  control (49); the session-level PHQ-9 and GAD-7 changes were −4.56,
  −5.81 and −1.63, and −4.53, −5.60 and −0.80, in the same order. Three
  cautions from the record itself: the registry posts descriptive means
  only, with no between-group tests or confidence intervals; the trial's
  pre-specified primary outcome was change in a functional-MRI activation
  measure, on which the arms are not visibly different, with the symptom
  scales all listed as secondary; and the PHQ-9 and GAD-7 figures come
  from smaller session-level samples (72, 43 and 30). The 200-person Phase
  2 ran 2023 to April 2025 and is NIMH-funded with Penn State, Washington
  University and Stanford as collaborators; whether a journal paper exists
  was not checked. Academic, with a human comparator and a delayed-treatment
  control, it is the kind of evidence this thread asks whether the field can
  produce.
  ([ClinicalTrials.gov NCT05603923](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05603923))

## 🧪 Clinical trials

The 184 mental-health-tagged registrations in Wednesday's collector file
are mostly term collisions: "Two Chairs," "Big Health," "measurement-based
care" and "single-session intervention" fire on cardiology, oncology,
pain and rehabilitation studies, and the multi-term digital-health rows
(a yoga-for-headache trial, a dialysis relaxation study, a proton-therapy
cognition study) are not mental-health trials. The rows that hold up were
confirmed live on the registry, and a direct ClinicalTrials.gov query by
psychiatric condition and 09-23 first-posted date turned up the rest.

- **Transcend Therapeutics registered EMPOWER-2 on 09-23
  ([NCT07836647](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07836647)), a second
  300-patient Phase 3 of TSND-201, its methylone-derived drug for PTSD,
  with primary completion estimated February 2028.** The design mirrors
  EMPOWER-1 ([NCT07456696](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07456696),
  first posted 03-06, primary completion estimated December 2027): weekly
  dosing for four weeks at one of two dose levels or placebo, an eight-week
  follow-up, and change in the clinician-rated CAPS-5 as the primary
  outcome. EMPOWER-2 lists two US sites so far, against 37 for EMPOWER-1.
  Transcend holds an FDA National Priority Voucher and a Breakthrough
  Therapy designation for TSND-201. Sponsor-registered design only, no
  data.

- **A Jerusalem Mental Health Center randomized trial compares a closed
  psychiatric ward built around Soteria principles with a conventional
  closed ward for adults in acute psychosis
  ([NCT07836348](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07836348), posted
  09-23, enrolling since September 2025).** 200 participants planned;
  outcomes include psychotic symptom severity, therapeutic alliance,
  one-year readmission and antipsychotic dose. A rare randomized test of an
  alternative inpatient model rather than a drug or app.
