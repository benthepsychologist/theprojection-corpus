## Today's throughline

US stocks rallied on Friday after a weak jobs report, the Nasdaq up 1.2%, but Treasury yields ended higher and Brent closed near $102 despite the G7's 100-million-barrel oil and diesel release. The Nasdaq had touched a record intraday. The report, 29,000 jobs added against about 84,000 expected, had pulled the 10-year down to about 5.18% in the morning and cut the odds of an October Fed rate rise to under a fifth, but yields reversed through the session and the 10-year closed near 5.28%, up from 5.24% on Thursday, with October hold odds near 78% at about 3pm ET and a December hike still widely expected. Unemployment rose to 4.2% and wage growth slowed to 3.0% a year, the weakest since May 2021, while July and August were revised down by 60,000 jobs. US stocks opened toward their August record, the S&P 500 up 1.1% at 10:44am ET, but it closed up 0.7% at 7,722.72, still about 0.3% lower on the week, with the Dow down about 1.3% on the week and the Nasdaq up about 0.5%; European stocks closed 0.3% to 1.1% higher. Euro-area inflation jumped to 3.8%, a three-year high, on energy costs rising 18.8%. France had proposed that EU countries release 50 million barrels of crude and 50 million of diesel after Washington asked European capitals to tap stockpiles or face a US fuel-export ban, and after a leaders' video call at about 8:30am ET the G7 said it would release up to 100 million barrels through the International Energy Agency; Brent had dipped under $100 before the announcement and traded near $100.8 shortly afterwards before closing at $102.25. France's 10-year yield rose to about 5% in the morning, its highest since 2002, and Blue Owl again capped withdrawals from two private credit funds.
