## Today's throughline

The G7 agreed on Friday to release up to 100 million barrels of oil and diesel over four months, while a weak September jobs report pulled the 10-year Treasury yield down to about 5.2% and stocks toward a record. The jobs report, 29,000 jobs added against about 84,000 expected, took the 10-year yield down from its 24-year high and cut the odds of an October Fed rate rise to under a fifth. Unemployment rose to 4.2% and wage growth slowed to 3.0% a year, the weakest since May 2021, while July and August were revised down by 60,000 jobs. US stocks rose toward their August record, the S&P 500 up 0.9% at 9:35am ET, with Fed-funds futures still pricing a December hike. Euro-area inflation jumped to 3.8%, a three-year high, on energy costs rising 18.8%. France had proposed that EU countries release 50 million barrels of crude and 50 million of diesel after Washington asked European capitals to tap stockpiles or face a US fuel-export ban, and after a leaders' video call at about 8:30am ET the G7 said it would release up to 100 million barrels through the International Energy Agency. France's 10-year yield briefly topped 5% and Blue Owl again capped withdrawals from two private credit funds.
