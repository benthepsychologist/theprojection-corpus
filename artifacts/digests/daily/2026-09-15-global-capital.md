## Deals & filings

- **SoftBank wired off the final $25.9bn balance on its $40bn OpenAI-stake
  bridge loan today, its largest-ever US-dollar borrowing, repaying early
  against a March 2027 maturity as it shifts the financing onto
  longer-term debt.** This resolves this map's own `softbank-openai-
  bridge-matures` expectation (due today) as a **HIT** — SoftBank had
  signaled the accelerated 09-15 date on 09-09, and today's wire confirms
  it, six months ahead of the original schedule. The payoff removes the
  OpenAI stake as pledged collateral and shifts the risk onto unsecured
  bondholders in the $10-20bn junk-bond issue SoftBank is roadshowing out
  of Citigroup (09-14 to 09-17) to fund the refinancing — that pricing is
  a separate, distinct ledger item due 09-17. The same day, short
  interest in SoftBank stock climbed to 2.27% of free float (roughly
  $3.9bn), the heaviest reading since July 2025 and the highest nominal
  short position since at least early 2015, as shares fell over 4% in
  Tokyo — a market pricing real refinancing risk into the name even as
  the loan itself got cleanly repaid. SoftBank separately told investors
  it expects its cumulative OpenAI investment to reach roughly $64.6bn by
  October (about 13% of the company).

## Power & lobbying

- **San Francisco Supervisor Shamann Walton introduced an urgency
  interim ordinance today for a 45-day moratorium on new data-center
  construction and expansion citywide, co-sponsored by Connie Chan,
  Jackie Fielder and Chyanne Chen and aimed squarely at the NOVVA
  facility in Bayview (9MW online this summer of a planned 36MW build).**
  This resolves the caveat on this map's own `sf-datacenter-moratorium-
  vote-0915` expectation: what was a single unconfirmed Google News
  redirect as of 09-11 is now independently corroborated by five local
  outlets (Mission Local, SFist, SF Chronicle, CBS SF, Hoodline). As an
  urgency ordinance under California Gov. Code §65858 it needs a 4/5
  Board vote today and, if the Planning Commission separately finds the
  city's 15 existing data-center campuses pose a risk, could extend to
  22 months and 15 days. **The vote outcome itself was not yet available
  as of this window** — this would be the first major-city (rather than
  suburban/exurban) jurisdiction among the string of US data-center
  moratorium fights this map has tracked, and the first sited inside the
  city most associated with the AI buildout itself.
