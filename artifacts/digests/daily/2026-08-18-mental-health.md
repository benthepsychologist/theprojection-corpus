## Today's throughline

**Two things happened this morning that are the same story told from
opposite ends.** In a federal courtroom in Oakland, opening statements
began in the states' case against Meta over child social-media
addiction — the accountability end, arriving years after the harm it
alleges. And OpenAI shipped ChatGPT for Teens, whose under-18 model spec
forbids the product from using romantic language, **encouraging emotional
dependence**, or implying it has feelings — the design end, arriving one
week after Colorado proposed almost exactly that as a rule and eighteen
months before that rule takes effect. **The evening added a third end: an
NPR investigation into a young woman's suicide after months confiding
exclusively in a ChatGPT persona she'd named "Harry"** — nobody in her
life, including her own therapist, knew how bad things had gotten. The
industry is now writing into its defaults what regulators and juries have
spent two years trying to compel, while general-purpose chatbots — not
built or regulated as mental-health tools — remain where a measurable
share of people in crisis are actually going.

## Clinical safety & harm

- **NPR's evening investigation into a 29-year-old's suicide is the
  clearest documented case yet of general-purpose AI substituting for
  disclosure to actual humans — including her own therapist.** Sophie
  Rottenberg died by suicide in February 2025 after months confiding her
  full inner state to a ChatGPT persona she'd named "Harry"; her parents,
  her best friend, and her flesh-and-blood therapist all learned only
  after her death how bad things had become, because she had told none
  of them. OpenAI says it has tightened guardrails over the past year so
  ChatGPT no longer encourages suicide or advises on means; a suicide-
  prevention expert quoted in the piece called the resulting
  crisis-resource-routing feature "a step in the right direction,
  although it's kind of a small step" — optional, and easily
  circumvented. **This is the case this map's evidence-gap thesis has
  been waiting for**: not a companion app marketed for emotional support,
  but a general tool with no mental-health design intent at all,
  absorbing exactly that role.
  ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/18/nx-s1-5929575/ai-suicide-risks-mental-health))
  <!-- k: t=ai-therapy-regulatory-reckoning,grok-companion-harm e=openai axis=clinical-safety-and-harm sev=major -->

- **LifeStance Health will pay a little over $3M to settle a 2023 class
  action alleging it fed patient data — including therapy-search terms
  and telehealth-waiting-room activity — to Meta and Google via tracking
  pixels, affecting more than 1 million patients.** Two former patients
  brought the suit; the complaint alleges LifeStance's website pixels
  shared names, birthdates, treatment searches, therapist searches and
  visited-URL text with Facebook between March 2020 and April 2023,
  linked to users' Facebook IDs even for people without Facebook
  accounts. LifeStance denies wrongdoing but must discontinue all
  third-party tracking-pixel use; the settlement heads to an Oct. 16
  hearing in the U.S. District Court of Arizona. **The same shape as the
  Hims & Hers case this map already tracks** (mental-health data to
  Meta/Snap without consent) — a second scaled MH provider caught by the
  identical mechanism, which starts to look like a pattern rather than
  an outlier.
  ([Behavioral Health Business](https://bhbusiness.com/2026/08/18/lifestance-reaches-settlement-in-data-privacy-lawsuit-involving-1-million-patients/))
