## Policy, regulation & legal

- **The FDA named a second participant to a pilot that exempts digital
  health devices from premarket authorisation — and behavioural health is
  one of its four focus areas with no participant yet named.** The
  Technology-Enabled Meaningful Patient Outcomes (**TEMPO**) pilot,
  coordinated with CMS innovation models, lets companies collect
  real-world data through Medicare while their devices are exempted from
  premarket authorisation requirements. The second participant is
  **Cadence**, whose AI-assisted "HypertensionOS" carries out routine
  medication initiation and titration steps for Stage 2 hypertension
  patients inside predefined safety parameters; **Dexcom** was the first,
  named 2026-07-23. The FDA plans to accept up to 10 manufacturers across
  four CMS focus areas — **one of which is behavioural health** — and
  none has been selected in that category yet. That is the line worth
  watching: a regulatory pathway that trades premarket review for
  real-world evidence collection is precisely the mechanism by which an
  AI mental-health tool could reach Medicare patients without a
  clearance, and this lens should know the first time one does.
  ([MedTech Dive](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/fda-names-second-participant-in-tempo-digital-health-pilot/828027/))

## 🧪 Clinical trials & evidence

- **A randomised trial pitting two AI chatbot therapists against each
  other found both helped and neither beat the other — on a sample far
  too small to support the second half of that sentence.** "Anna vs.
  Judith," appearing in *Internet Interventions* in today's feed, randomised
  **102 adults with social anxiety disorder** to a 4-week daily
  smartphone intervention delivered by one of two AI therapists — "Anna"
  running psychodynamic therapy and "Judith" running CBT, both trained on
  established manualised protocols and named after the children of the
  two traditions' founders — or to a waitlist. Reported within-group
  effect sizes were **d = 0.79 (AI-PDT)** and **d = 0.72 (AI-CBT)**, with
  no significant difference between the active arms; therapeutic alliance
  and satisfaction were also measured. ⚠️ **Two cautions this lens should
  attach, because the headline reads better than the design supports.**
  First, those are *within-group* effect sizes — improvement from
  baseline, which absorbs regression to the mean and natural remission —
  not the between-group contrast against waitlist that would establish
  efficacy. Second, ~34 participants per arm cannot detect a difference
  between two active treatments; "no significant difference" here is an
  absence of evidence, not evidence of equivalence, and should not be
  cited as showing the therapeutic orientation doesn't matter. What the
  study does establish is more modest and still interesting: people
  engaged with both, and reported an alliance with them.
  ([Internet Interventions](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S221478292600059X),
  [Stockholm University project page](https://www.su.se/english/research/research-projects/anna-vs-judith-comparing-ai-delivered-pdt-and-cbt-for-social-anxiety))
