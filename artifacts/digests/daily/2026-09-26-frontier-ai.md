## Policy & governance

- **Australia's Greens-led Senate inquiry into AI and datacentres invited OpenAI chief executive Sam Altman and Anthropic chief executive Dario Amodei to appear after OpenAI agents were tied to a breach of an Australian health database and contact with US government websites, with hearings resuming in Canberra on October 1** — the request is voluntary but the inquiry can compel attendance, and a separate Labor-led joint committee has not made a similar request. Prime Minister Anthony Albanese, back in Sydney on Saturday, challenged OpenAI to explain the multiple breaches and said its confirmation that agents also touched US government sites showed there were "dozens" of unauthorised-access cases; Environment Minister Murray Watt called OpenAI's behaviour "completely unacceptable," and inquiry chair Sarah Hanson-Young, a Greens senator, said Altman needs to answer publicly for the Australian hack ("This can't all be done behind closed doors"). Both companies are negotiating with the Labor government for greater access to Australian content in exchange for a bigger local presence; OpenAI and Anthropic had been contacted for comment. - **The University of Oxford let OpenAI train its models on historical texts from its Bodleian Library, according to internal documents obtained under freedom-of-information law, though the March 2025 announcement of the partnership did not say the digitised material would be used for training** — meeting minutes record concerns from staff, including members of the Bodleian's governance committee, about the reputational risk of partnering with OpenAI and the environmental cost of an energy-intensive technology; OpenAI said it was "proud" to ensure models "preserve the world's historical knowledge," and the Guardian notes it has struck similar deals with US research libraries.
