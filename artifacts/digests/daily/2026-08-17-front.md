## Today's throughline

**The largest contingent liability in the AI buildout got a number today,
and it had been sitting in a public filing before this map reported it
missing.** NVIDIA's 8-K — accepted at **08:41 ET** (corrected 08-18 from
04:41; see 🔄 Map changes) filed alongside the Ohio press release that
this morning's pass read instead —
caps the company's obligation at **$105 billion** and describes an
instrument materially different from the "guarantee" the coverage implied.
It is a **residual-value guarantee** on OpenAI's 20-year leases: NVIDIA
pays the shortfall between a guaranteed minimum lease value and whatever
the site fetches on re-lease or sale, triggered by OpenAI's insolvency or
non-payment, attaching only from 2028. And OpenAI has "agreed to reimburse
and indemnify NVIDIA for any and all amounts actually paid" — the recourse
runs back to the party whose insolvency is the trigger, which means the
indemnity is worth least exactly when it is called. The four-number
progression this map has tracked since 07-27 closes here: $250B → up to
$750B → under $120B (press) → **$105B, filed**.

**The day's actual repricing happened in the long bond, and it was about
debt supply rather than the Gulf.** The 30-year Treasury yield reached
**5.31%** at 2:14pm ET, its highest since June 2007, with the 2-year
easing to ~4.17% — a bear steepener, not a parallel shift. Equities faded
(S&P 500 −0.39%, Dow −0.46%, Nasdaq −0.24%). What makes the attribution
readable is what did *not* move: the US–Iran framework formally expired
the same day and the president threatened to bomb Oman, yet Brent gave up
its early gains and held near $89–90. A geopolitical supply shock lifts
crude and yields together; a fiscal repricing lifts long yields alone. The
mechanism surfaced in the same session — **global bond issuance crossed $5
trillion Monday**, more than a month faster than last year's record, with
US investment-grade at $136B in a fortnight and $1.4T year-to-date, and
investors pulling roughly **36% of their initial orders** after pricing was
squeezed, twice the prior week's rate.

**An independent AI-silicon competitor was converted into a customer, and
the conversion has a price.** Groq raised $350M at **$3.5B — half its
$6.9B September valuation** — led by Disruptive with Nvidia participating.
Nvidia's $20B licensing deal last December took founder Jonathan Ross and
the senior silicon team; Groq has since abandoned its own LPU architecture
and become a neocloud running Nvidia systems. It is the first rung on
Nvidia's stake ladder (Nebius, Naver, Intel, Groq) whose outcome is
legible, and the outcome is that the rival stopped being one.

**The largest US payer came under scrutiny from three directions at once,
and this map had no term for it.** An amended investor complaint accuses
UnitedHealth of "corporate governance failures on a historic scale" —
including shutting down an internal audit that had found at least **$200M
in unsupportable diagnosis codes**, $29B of buybacks, and known security
gaps preceding a breach that has cost over $2B. Separately, the IRS is
seeking to significantly increase its taxable income for 2017–2020 over
transfer pricing with a foreign subsidiary, per notices received in March
and unreported until today. Both reached this map through a generic
finance feed, because UnitedHealth — named in the map's own claim-denial
thread — had **no watchlist entry at all**. That gap and Centene's are now
closed.

**Maximal language, motionless markets, in the Gulf.** Trump demanded Iran
"put up the white flag of surrender," said he has "no time schedule,"
claimed a direct Revolutionary Guard backchannel, and threatened to bomb
**Oman** — the mediator whose confirmation the Hormuz arrangement depends
on. The IRGC called the backchannel claim a "delusion" within hours and
Iran's foreign ministry dismissed the underlying report as psychological
operations. Nothing moved. ⛔ **The Lebanon item this paragraph originally
carried — Aoun "breaking with Hezbollah" in front of von der Leyen and
Costa — is RETRACTED: its source is dated 2026-03-09, a five-month-old
story written up as current.** What Aoun actually did on 08-17 runs the
other way: he defended UNIFIL's continued presence against Israeli
pressure to end it, saying "our first choice is to extend UNIFIL's
mandate" and noting that "Israel rejects the presence of UNIFIL and has
been exerting pressure in this direction for nearly a year." Continuity
under pressure, not rupture — and the day had no real diplomatic shift.
Russia jailed the deputy chair of its only openly anti-war party for 11
years and barred the party from next month's ballot on the same day.

**Flash: none, checked twice.** Verbal escalation on a five-month
stalemate is not a front-page-anywhere event, and Brent's flat close is
the cleanest evidence the market agrees. Writing no flash is the correct
outcome.
