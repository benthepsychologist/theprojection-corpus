## Capital in my markets

- **China's Foreign Ministry and Embassy in Washington said Saturday that Beijing and Washington agreed a $30 billion reciprocal tariff reduction, a Board of Trade and an extension of the Kuala Lumpur trade outcomes among eight results of Xi Jinping's state visit.** The Chinese text says the two leaders "endorse the outcomes of the consultations between the two economic and trade teams" and instruct that they be implemented; it gives no product list, start date or truce length, and does not repeat the January 10 date or the two-month extension Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent named on Wednesday (Reuters puts the truce's prior expiry at November 10). The other outcomes: a China-US AI dialogue whose next exchange is in November 2026 plus a communication channel for AI-related incidents; agreement that Iran should honour its pledge not to develop nuclear weapons and that no country may impose tolls on international waterways; mutual support for hosting APEC and the G20, with each leader intending to attend the other's; and a military crisis-communication memorandum to be concluded "as soon as possible". Foreign Minister Wang Yi called the visit one that "opened a new chapter"; Xi has landed back in Beijing (Xinhua). This is China's account, and the US Embassy in Beijing did not immediately respond to Reuters. The White House's own fact sheet, dated September 25 and published at 11:29pm ET Friday, tells a different version: consensus on "more favorable tariff treatment" for $30 billion of non-sensitive goods in each direction (US farm products, fish and seafood, wood products, cosmetics and medical devices one way; small appliances, toys, holiday decorations and children's car seats the other), a Chinese commitment to import at least 10 million metric tons of US coal in each of 2027 and 2028, a new Board of Investment and continuing work on rare-earth shortages. Beijing's text has none of the coal, the product categories or the Board of Investment and calls the $30 billion a tariff reduction rather than a value of goods covered, and neither document gives tariff rates, start dates or a truce length; Greer said Friday morning that fuller US details come Monday 09-28.
