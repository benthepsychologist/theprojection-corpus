## Today's throughline

Three elections and a negotiation defined the day, and in each case the
result was either an entrenchment or a stalemate. Russia's three-day Duma
election closed with the Central Election Commission's early count
putting United Russia near 57.5% on turnout of roughly 56.7%, the highest
of any post-Soviet parliamentary vote — figures that exist only as the
state's own account, in an election its sole openly anti-war party was
barred from entering and where opposition observers say they were turned
away. Germany's two state elections went the other way for its governing
centre: projections put the AfD narrowly ahead of the SPD in
Mecklenburg-Vorpommern, with Chancellor Friedrich Merz's CDU at 5.0% in a
state it once led, while Die Linke became Berlin's strongest party for
the first time. Treasury Secretary Bessent and Trade Representative Greer
opened a day-long negotiation with China's Vice Premier He Lifeng in
Manhattan covering the November tariff truce, rare-earth flows and AI
guardrails — Bessent saying the AI discussion would cover "both open- and
closed-weight models" — but the session ran past the afternoon with no
readout of any kind. Saudi Arabia broke its silence on the Houthi strike
claims, its coalition saying the attempts on Yanbu, Taif, Baysh and
Farasan were thwarted and a ballistic missile aimed at Riyadh
intercepted, even as regional officials told the Associated Press the
kingdom is running short of interceptors and has asked four governments
for air-defence help. On AI, Nvidia's Jensen Huang rejected the case for
slowing development outright and put the odds AI ends the world by 2030
at zero, while Anthropic published three proposed public measurements of
that same pace and disclosed that Claude now leads 26% of its own AI
research and development work.
