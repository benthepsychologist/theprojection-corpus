## Today's throughline

**A federal judge permanently barred the Pentagon from blacklisting
Anthropic over two safety rules the company refused to drop, the market
repriced the Fed hawkishly while the one inflation print of the day came
in softer, and Nvidia quietly pulled back from the financing structure
holding up much of the AI buildout.** Three large things, in three
different lenses, on one Friday. **Two of the three had already happened
before this map looked**, and the recovery is the day's real story about
itself: the Anthropic ruling came down **Thursday evening**, the Nvidia
pullback was reported **Thursday evening**, and the 08-27 finalize pass
that ran at 10:00 this morning — three coverage critics, `coverage:
done` — **caught neither.** The 08-27 digest had predicted, in writing,
that its own coverage gap would be the uncurated evening. **It was right,
and the pass built to close that gap still missed both stories in it.**

**The ruling is the most consequential single item.** Judge Rita F. Lin
(N.D. Cal.) granted Anthropic summary judgment and converted March's
preliminary injunction into a permanent one, finding that Defense
Secretary Pete Hegseth's designation of the company as a national-security
supply-chain risk was **retaliation for Anthropic publicly criticising
the administration**. The underlying dispute is unusually clean: Anthropic
refused, in contract renegotiation, to lift two restrictions — **no use of
Claude for mass domestic surveillance of Americans, and no fully
autonomous lethal weapons** — and the government responded by directing
every federal agency to stop using its technology. **A frontier lab held
a safety red line against its own government, was punished for it, and
has now won in court.** ⚠️ One designation survives: a separate FASCSA
challenge is still pending in the D.C. Circuit.

**The market's answer to Warsh, which the morning run explicitly declined
to guess at, was hawkish and oddly shaped.** By 15:04 ET every point on
the curve had risen and the belly had risen hardest — the five-year up
about 9 basis points, **the thirty-year up barely 2** — with the dollar
up 0.56%, gold down 3.28%, and **the VIX essentially unmoved at 14.58**.
That is the signature of a rate-path repricing, not a fiscal scare, which
is a point of evidence running *against* the intervention thesis in
`treasury-long-end-intervention`. **And it happened on a day the
University of Michigan's final August survey showed year-ahead inflation
expectations falling to 4.0% from 4.2%** — the market moved on rhetoric,
against the data.

**The chairman of the Federal Reserve gave his first Jackson Hole keynote
this morning, spent a substantial part of it on artificial intelligence,
and said nothing whatsoever about the fight over the Fed's
independence.** Kevin Warsh's "In Our Time," delivered at 10:00 ET on his
hundredth day in the job, was read here directly from the Fed's own
released text. He declines to give a reaction function at all — *"I wish
our understanding of the economy were so precise... that some simple
function like a Taylor rule could be rigorously relied upon"* — argues
that forward guidance "has overstayed its welcome" as routine practice,
and makes the case for "a quieter Fed." On the mandate he is direct:
12-month PCE inflation at **3.7%**, six-month at **4.1%**, "running above
our 2 percent target," with the 2% goal treated as firm and fixed and the
phrase "average inflation" absent entirely.

**Then he spent a section of it on this map's own subject matter.** He
called AI "a new variable — potentially a new factor of production," said
progress "has been faster even than its evangelists predicted," described
"a hinge point in history" and "a kind of hyper–Moore's law," and put
**annualised token sales for the two leading labs alone at more than $100
billion, up over 500% in a year** — a figure he attributes to "reports,"
not to Fed data. The questions he then set out are the ones this map
exists to ask: whether AI produces a sustained productivity rise and
when, whether tokens complement or substitute for labour, whether the
next model generation needs more capital or less, and — *"it's not
obvious where the returns on capital will land or on what timescale"* —
how much of the surplus accrues to "AI labs, chipmakers, energy producers,
and cloud providers" versus to businesses and consumers.

**What he did not say is a finding, and it is checkable rather than
inferred.** The words "independence," "administration," "buyback," "long
end" and "average inflation" appear nowhere in the released text; the
single occurrence of "Treasury" refers to Treasury securities as one
market signal among several. Going in, previews framed the Cook removal
fight and the buyback/fiscal-dominance pressure as the market's central
question, and `treasury-long-end-intervention` carried an explicit watch
item asking whether the keynote would address the TGA mechanism. **It did
not. That is a resolved negative on a watch line, not a gap in the
research.**
