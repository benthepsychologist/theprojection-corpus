## Today's throughline

The envoys went. **Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner arrived in Moscow on
Saturday for talks with Putin and are still scheduled to fly to Kyiv on
Sunday** — a leg reported "in doubt" on Friday, now with a concrete
security condition attached: Kremlin spokesman Peskov said Putin has ordered
**no strikes on Kyiv for three days from midnight tonight**, explicitly
"connected with the preparation for and holding of meetings involving the
Americans in Kyiv." The pause is prospective. The night before it, Russia
hit Kyiv's own airports for the first time in a long while — Zelensky's
word was "deliberate," tied to discussion of where the envoys might land —
after rejecting Ukraine's unilateral offer to halt hostilities for the
visit. A ceasefire offered and refused, a pause announced for the visitors,
and the airports struck in between: that is the shape of the weekend.

The Iran war produced its first direct exchange between Iranian missiles
and US warships. **The IRGC fired ballistic missiles at a US aircraft
carrier and a guided-missile destroyer on Saturday; both evaded, and
CENTCOM answered by disabling two IRGC crude carriers off Kharg Island and
Jask and destroying a third in the Gulf of Oman.** Six months into a war
fought so far against Gulf bases, shipping lanes and Iranian territory,
this is a different target class in both directions — the US Navy at sea,
and Iran's export terminal waters — and it is answered on oil
infrastructure rather than on land. Israel and Hezbollah traded fire in
south Lebanon overnight, three civilians killed, two days after the
detainee exchange. And Yemen's own civil war, distinct from the Red Sea
campaign, is in its heaviest fighting since the 2022 truce — a Houthi
offensive toward Mokha and the heights over Bab al-Mandab, more than 120
dead by Friday on wire counts and sixty more on Saturday — and this run
opened a thread for it under the standing rule.

None of it is a flash. Two escalations inside running wars and a
