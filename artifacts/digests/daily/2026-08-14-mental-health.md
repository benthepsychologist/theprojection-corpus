## Research & evidence

- **The VA disclosed it's now running its 20th active clinical trial of
  psychedelic-assisted therapy, this one evaluating psilocybin for
  veterans with treatment-resistant depression — a federal research push
  independent of, and running in parallel to, the Compass Pathways NDA
  track this lens already tracks toward FDA approval.** The trial is the
  latest under VA Chief of Staff Curt Cashour's push (since early 2026)
  to prioritize emerging treatments — also citing MDMA for PTSD and
  GLP-1s for alcohol-use disorder — and sits inside more than $23 million
  in external funding the VA's Office of Research and Development has
  distributed across its psychedelic-trial portfolio. VA Secretary Doug
  Collins framed it as unrelated to Compass specifically: "VA is pursuing
  all avenues to evaluate new treatments." A separate MDMA-assisted-
  therapy trial for PTSD in young adults with childhood trauma also
  newly posted to the registry today (below) — the same federal
  psychedelic-acceleration pattern showing up in both a VA program
  announcement and a fresh academic registration on the same day.
  ([Behavioral Health Business](https://bhbusiness.com/2026/08/14/the-va-is-now-evaluating-mdma-glp-1s-and-psilocybin-for-behavioral-health/))

## 🧪 Clinical trials

Verified against the live registry's actual `StudyFirstPostDate`
(2026-08-14 only). Two stand out against this lens's active threads: an
MDMA-assisted-therapy trial for PTSD in young adults (18-26) with
childhood trauma, testing safety/feasibility and symptom change over up
to three MDMA sessions
([NCT07765693](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07765693)) — the same
federal psychedelic-research push the VA item above describes, this one
independently funded and academic — and a Singapore-based trial mapping
the neural circuits behind treatment-resistant depression and anxiety
using personalized, accelerated TMS (theta-burst stimulation) with dense
fMRI phenotyping
([NCT07766291](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07766291)), relevant
to this lens's neuromodulation-evidence thread on TMS/SAINT's evidence
base. Also newly posted: a University of Utah pilot testing an
AI-augmented maternal-mental-health-risk tool ("Lōvu") alongside usual
prenatal care
([NCT07766174](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07766174)) and a
University of Michigan trial on a circadian-focused postpartum-depression
intervention
([NCT07766161](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07766161)) — both
routine pilot/feasibility registrations, not results, on a
perinatal/postpartum angle this lens has surfaced before as a
not-yet-promoted thread candidate but which doesn't clear "genuinely new
development" today on registrations alone.
