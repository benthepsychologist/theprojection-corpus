## Today's throughline

A large, patient trial answered a question this lens has been circling for
months, and the answer is about format rather than therapy. A Swedish
factorial RCT of 2,477 people found unguided internet-delivered CBT and
unguided internet-delivered psychodynamic therapy equally effective against
waitlist — and found that doubling the treatment window from eight weeks to
sixteen added nothing, that peer forums added nothing, and that participants
completed fewer than half the modules on average. Run beside a German review
finding that the five depression apps Germany's statutory insurers reimburse
range from 22% to 72% guideline conformity while carrying identical
reimbursement status, the shape of the day is a challenge to the payment
model rather than to the treatments: what is being purchased is a delivery
format whose binding constraint is that people stop using it, and the
approval regimes are not measuring that.

## Research & evidence

- **A Swedish RCT of 2,477 people found unguided internet-delivered CBT and
  unguided internet-delivered psychodynamic therapy equally effective for
  depression and anxiety (d = 0.38–0.65 against waitlist), with gains holding
  at 24 months — but participants completed fewer than half the treatment
  modules on average, and extending treatment from 8 to 16 weeks added
  nothing.** Lindqvist, Mechler, Hlynsson, Andersson and Carlbring, in
  *Internet Interventions*, a 3×2×2 factorial design crossing treatment arm
  (Unified Protocol CBT, affect-focused psychodynamic therapy, waitlist) with
  duration (8 vs 16 weeks) and peer-forum access. Both active arms beat
  waitlist with small-to-moderate effects; head to head, the two modalities
  did not differ; peer-forum access added no benefit. The adherence numbers
  are the clinically load-bearing part — this is evidence that the unguided
  self-help *format*, not the treatment model inside it, is the constraint.
  ([PubMed, PMID 42633284](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42633284/),
  DOI 10.1016/j.invent.2026.100980, trial NCT05016843)
  <!-- k: t=mh-evidence-watch,dtx-payment-paradox axis=research sev=major -->

- **A German systematic review found the five DiGA-approved depression apps —
  all carrying identical statutory-insurance reimbursement — range from 22%
  to 72% guideline conformity, mean 62%.** Schmitz et al. (University of
  Bonn), *Internet Interventions*, published online 2026-08-25. Three of the
  five implemented all five core internet-CBT components the review's
  framework checked for; two implemented only some. App quality on the Mobile
  Application Rating Scale was moderate and uneven (mean 3.8/5, range
  3.2–4.2). This is the `dtx-payment-paradox` question stated with numbers:
  Germany's DiGA fast-track grants reimbursement on a basis that does not
  track guideline adherence.
  ([PubMed, PMID 42676895](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42676895/),
  DOI 10.1016/j.invent.2026.100991)
  <!-- k: t=dtx-payment-paradox axis=research -->

- **A blinded three-way benchmark on late-life-depression questions found
  ChatGPT (GPT-5.5) ahead of Gemini and Doubao on clinical acceptability
  (78.9% vs 72.2% vs 60.0%) and major-safety-error rate (5.6% vs 10.0% vs
  16.7%) — but all three degraded sharply on high-risk questions, with
  under-triage in up to 36.7% of Doubao's high-risk responses.** *Frontiers in
  Psychiatry*: 90 questions across six geriatric-psychiatry domains and three
  risk strata, each blind-rated by a psychiatrist on accuracy, safety,
  geriatric appropriateness, warning-sign recognition and triage; a
  30-question subset resubmitted for test–retest consistency (ChatGPT 90.0%,
  Gemini 83.3%, Doubao 73.3%). ⚠️ **Two reasons to hold this loosely.** The
  article's own metadata gives an online-first date of 2026-08-18 against its
  09-11 cover date, so this is a republication artifact rather than new
  research. And the author list's institutional affiliations — plastic and
  aesthetic surgery, a tourism college, general clinical medicine schools —
  have no visible connection to geriatric psychiatry or AI evaluation. That
  is a flag on editorial quality at a fast-turnaround open-access journal, not
  proof of a problem, but it is reason to treat these numbers as unreplicated.
  ([Frontiers in Psychiatry](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry))

A Swedish trial of nearly 2,500 people found it did not matter whether
unguided internet therapy was cognitive-behavioural or psychodynamic, did not
matter whether it ran eight weeks or sixteen, and did not matter whether
there was a peer forum — what mattered was that people completed fewer than
half of it. A German review found the five depression apps statutory insurers
reimburse range from 22% to 72% guideline conformity on identical
reimbursement terms. Both point the same way: the approval and payment
regimes are not measuring the thing that decides whether these treatments
work.
