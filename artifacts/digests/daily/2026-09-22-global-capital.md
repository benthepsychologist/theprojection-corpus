## Today's throughline

Wall Street ended Tuesday with the Nasdaq at a second straight record close
and the S&P 500 flat, but under the flat index bank, insurer and broker
stocks fell about 2% to 3% on fear that Meta's Muse agent will disrupt
businesses built on customer inertia. Brent settled at $99.25 a barrel
after a fifth straight losing session and the 10-year Treasury yield held
at 4.96%. Meanwhile AI financing kept getting pricier and more crowded:
SoftBank's roughly $11bn bond for its next OpenAI payment drew more than
$20bn of early orders at the highest yields it has ever offered on dollar
debt (8.75% to 9.875%), CoreWeave closed a $4.2bn convertible, Reuters
reported that investors are demanding wider spreads on AI-related
investment-grade bonds than on everything else, Nvidia's $1.5bn SB Energy
purchase executed at 90% of the IPO price, and Nscale's own filing carried
"going concern" language. Rate risk did not fade: two regional Fed
presidents argued for further hikes, and the administration said it is
examining a diesel export ban with US diesel at a record $6.53 a gallon.

Morning read: Nvidia's SB Energy vendor-financing commitment moved from
disclosed to executed (the IPO's roadshow has since been held back, see
