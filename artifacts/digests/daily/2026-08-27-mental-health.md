## Platform design & youth

  and the teen-design terms this map logged as a proposal yesterday are
  now binding for ten years.** ⚠️ **Stated plainly because it would
  otherwise read as a second event: this is the same agreement as the
  ~$17bn Oakland settlement filed to 08-26**, which ended the
  consolidated multistate trial before a jury verdict and which this map

  received the document, not signed off. **The approval is what is new**,
  and Meta's own post carries an "Updated on August 27, 2026 at 10:30 AM
  PT to reflect the judge's approval" stamp.

  **What Meta's own account adds past the attorney-general press
  release:** the protections apply **automatically** to under-18
  Instagram and Facebook accounts in participating jurisdictions; the
  **two-hour daily limit is cumulative across both apps** and counted
  across multiple accounts where Meta detects them; **Night Mode** blocks
  midnight to 6am across Feed, Stories, Explore and Reels; **School Mode**
  mutes notifications 8am-3pm; prompts fire every 15 minutes of
  continuous use and at 60 and 90 minutes cumulative; the
  **non-algorithmic feed** is teen-selectable and **parents can set it as
  required**; autoplay off; likes hidden; cosmetic-surgery and
  extreme-makeup filters disabled. Meta simultaneously published an open
  letter urging TikTok and YouTube to adopt the same standard.

  **Why this lens cares about the approval and not just the money:**
  `social-media-causality-fight` tracks a research argument that policy
  keeps outrunning — Australia's under-16 ban being the standing example.
  **A settlement received is a proposal; a settlement approved is a
  ten-year, jurisdiction-split design change on a named population that
  can actually be measured.** That is the natural experiment the
  causality literature has never been able to run.

  while **Meta's own newsroom index card still says 51.** Same coalition,

## Policy, regulation & legal

- **KFF published a state-by-state comparison of how US states are
  regulating AI mental-health tools, and the headline finding is that
  they are not converging** — Illinois and Nevada restrict use directly,
  while Utah and Rhode Island regulate disclosure and data handling
  instead, leaving open whether these tools count as clinical support, a
  consumer product, or care requiring licensure. **That unresolved
  classification is the whole fight** — every downstream question
  (liability, reimbursement, who may deploy one) follows from it, and
  a patchwork answer means the deploying organisation picks its
  jurisdiction. A policy synthesis rather than a new event, filed
  because it is the clearest current map of the terrain
  `state-therapy-chatbot-bans` tracks.
  ([KFF](https://www.kff.org/mental-health/different-state-regulatory-approaches-reflect-open-questions-about-ai-mental-health-tools/))
