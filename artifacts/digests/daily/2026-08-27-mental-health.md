the same question from opposite ends: what happens when triage stops
being a clinical act.** An American Prospect investigation reports that
one Kaiser Permanente clinic's mental-health triage staffing fell from

Utah and Rhode Island regulating disclosure and data instead, with the

**The Kaiser reporting is what the unresolved question looks like on the

## Clinical safety & harm

- **An American Prospect investigation reports Kaiser Permanente's Walnut
  Creek clinic cut mental-health triage staffing from nine clinicians to
  three over three years while leaning on automated and algorithmic

## Policy, regulation & legal

- **KFF published a state-by-state comparison of how US states are
  regulating AI mental-health tools, and the headline finding is that
  they are not converging** — Illinois and Nevada restrict use directly,
  while Utah and Rhode Island regulate disclosure and data handling
  instead, leaving open whether these tools count as clinical support, a
  consumer product, or care requiring licensure. **That unresolved
  classification is the whole fight** — every downstream question
  (liability, reimbursement, who may deploy one) follows from it, and
  a patchwork answer means the deploying organisation picks its
  jurisdiction. A policy synthesis rather than a new event, filed
  because it is the clearest current map of the terrain
  `state-therapy-chatbot-bans` tracks.
  ([KFF](https://www.kff.org/mental-health/different-state-regulatory-approaches-reflect-open-questions-about-ai-mental-health-tools/))
