## Today's throughline

The Iran war stopped being a war about shipping lanes and became a war
about refineries. Saudi Aramco facilities were struck again on Monday,
with the 400,000-barrel-a-day Jizan refinery hit for the third time since
the summer — and Jizan is on the **Red Sea**, which is the whole point:
Saudi Arabia has been routing crude west precisely to avoid the Strait of
Hormuz, so the strike lands on the workaround rather than the chokepoint.
No group had claimed it and Aramco had not commented at this cut. Around
it, the declaratory war escalated on both sides in a single day: Defense
Secretary Pete Hegseth said the United States "will destroy (and sink)"
Iranian oil tankers if Iran fires on US vessels — a step past CENTCOM's
own 09-05 strikes, which it said deliberately disabled three tankers
rather than sinking them to limit spill risk — and Iranian parliament
speaker Ghalibaf answered on X, "Strike our assets and you get struck."
Iran's Supreme National Security Council secretary Mohsen Rezaei
separately said Tehran will declare a "restricted zone" running from the
US naval blockade line through Hormuz into the Gulf, with any ship
entering it added to Iran's sanctions list. He published no boundaries and
gave no date beyond "coming days and weeks," and that gap is the item's
real content — a zone that is announced but never drawn says something
different about Iranian capability than one that is.

The deadliest thing that happened today was in Lebanon, not the Gulf.
Overnight Israeli strikes on **Kfar Roummane** destroyed a residential
building and killed 11 people, two of them children and four women, with a
paramedic killed in a second strike on a vehicle — twelve dead, and a
three-day south-Lebanon toll of at least 27 per Lebanon's health ministry.
That is a sharp escalation past the four deaths this map logged for 09-06,
and it is the item this run would most likely have missed: the first
world-news sweep ran out of time before reaching this thread and said so,
which is the only reason a second sweep was dispatched to find it.

Elsewhere the direction was better. Ukrainian technicians **restored
external power to the Zaporizhzhia nuclear plant** at 19:02 Kyiv time,
reconnecting it through the repaired 330kV Ferosplavna-1 line and ending
an 18-day outage during which diesel generators were the only thing
cooling six reactors and the spent-fuel pools; the plant is drawing 11 MW
from the line. The repair was possible only because of the IAEA-brokered
local ceasefire that took effect 09-05. The Kremlin's pause on strikes
against Kyiv held for a **second** night — 92 drones launched nationwide,
71 downed, hits at twelve locations and none in the city — leaving one
night to run on a three-night commitment. And in Germany, the returning
officer's provisional final count put the AfD at 43.8% in Saxony-Anhalt,
1.8 points below the partial count this map recorded on Sunday, with the
BSW clearing the 5% threshold at 5.3% and thereby keeping the AfD short of
governing alone.
