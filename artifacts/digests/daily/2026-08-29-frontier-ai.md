## Policy & governance

- **The Commerce Department's reported "cut-down AI diffusion rule" now
  has named routes, a named legal problem, and a timeline — none of which
  it had yesterday.** Tech Times' 08-29 synthesis reports the rule targets
  Chinese firms' remote GPU access routed through **Thailand and
  Singapore**, with **Malaysia, Indonesia and Japan** as secondary routes
  already in use, and quotes a Baker McKenzie export-control attorney that
  it is "widely acknowledged" in the bar that **"Commerce cannot enforce a
  regulation on remote access to chips under existing law"** without new
  statutory authority — which is what the pending Remote Access Security
  Act would supply. The reported trigger chain: BIS offshore-subsidiary
  guidance on 05-31, Moonshot's Kimi K3 launch on 07-16, OSTP Director
  Michael Kratsios publicly accusing Chinese firms of the practice on
  07-22, a draft sent to OIRA in February and withdrawn in March, and
  Commerce's own regulatory agenda now listing it first among planned
  FY2026 actions (the year ends 09-30). ⚠️ **A single outlet's
  legal-analysis synthesis of earlier reporting** (Tom's Hardware, The
  Information among them), reached via a reader proxy; no rule text
  exists and nothing here says the rule will issue by the deadline.
  ([Tech Times](https://www.techtimes.com/))

## China

  92% short of demand today to 34% short by 2035 — and names SMIC's yield
  and lithography capability as the binding constraint on how fast the
  gap closes.** Published by SCMP on 08-24 and corroborated across three

  this map on 08-29. **It is the first outside quantification of the

  a decade, with the tool this thread exists to test named as the reason
