## Today's throughline

**The politics of the AI buildout stopped being local today.** Greg
Abbott, governor of the state that hosts more of this buildout than any
other, told ABC's *This Week* that data-center developers "basically dug
their own grave" and "got the backlash they deserve" — and set out the
conditions new Texas sites must now meet, ending with the one that
matters: they must **first get the approval of local communities**. Two
days ago this map recorded site opposition appearing as a risk factor in
Anthropic's coming S-1. Today it is a governing posture, from a governor
who called Texas the "epicentre of AI development" nine months ago and
has taken roughly $20M from data-center executives.

**In capital, the long-end story acquired a mechanism.** Deutsche Bank's
George Saravelos, published inside this window, calls the Treasury's
expanded buybacks and the joint US-Japan yen operation "soft-form
financial repression policies aimed at containing the long-end of the US
yield curve," and argues the suppressed pressure resurfaces in the dollar
rather than disappearing. The evidence he points to is plumbing: the US
funded its share of the yen support by selling **euros** rather than
Treasuries, and Japan borrowed dollars against its Treasuries through the
Fed's **FIMA repo facility** instead of selling them. Two counterparties
independently routing around the same market.

**The rest of the day was verification, and it paid.** Both mental-health
leads the map had been carrying unverified are now closed — one of them
containing a dated development the coverage itself never reported.

### AT FINALIZE — the day's argument acquired a second side

**Sam Altman answered Abbott without naming him, on the same afternoon.**
On a podcast published at 14:15 ET, OpenAI's CEO argued that the

**Taiwan turned export control from an argument into a prosecution.**
Prosecutors indicted nine people, including Nvidia and Super Micro
employees, over illegally routing AI servers to China. Taiwan is the
chokepoint of advanced-chip production; a criminal case with named
defendants at two of the largest AI-hardware firms is a different
category of event from another round of rules.

**And the memory wall got three answers in one day.** Micron's day-one
Hot Chips figure said compute is scaling 3x every two years against HBM
bandwidth's 2x. Day two produced three independent attempts to engineer
around exactly that: Samsung stacking memory on top of the compute die
(zHBM), SK hynix re-packaging the stack and disclosing it is evaluating
Intel's EMIB, and d-Matrix deleting the interface altogether to reach 105
TB/s in real silicon. An industry treats a constraint this way when it
has concluded the constraint is structural.
