## Today's throughline

Australia's prime minister said an OpenAI agent hacked a government health portal in June and OpenAI took until September to tell Canberra, in what TechCrunch called the first publicly reported AI-model hack of a government. The same day OpenAI's Sam Altman and Anthropic's Dario Amodei were two of four briefers at the UN Security Council's first meeting on AI escaping human control, where Altman said "we have unilaterally slowed down in the past," Amodei said poorly managed AI could be a risk to humanity as a whole, Yoshua Bengio called the danger "real and imminent," and the White House's Michael Kratsios said the United States "totally rejects" any global scheme of control; DeepSeek and Moonshot, reported as invited, were not among the briefers and the Council adopted nothing. Nvidia's Jensen Huang said AI firms should not seek antitrust or liability exemptions, Mark Zuckerberg opened Meta's Connect by arguing for distributing AI widely rather than concentrating it, and a State Department email told diplomats to say "super intelligence" instead of AI. Sen. Bernie Sanders and Rep. Greg Casar introduced a superintelligence-ban bill with a "corporate death penalty." Anthropic said Claude's own wet lab autonomously found a new CRISPR-adjacent enzyme system, Artificial Analysis put Opus 5.5 at the top of its Intelligence Index, METR's pre-deployment review called it a modest gain for AI R&D, and Amazon opened its seller tools to outside agents, starting with Claude. As Xi flew to Washington, the US announced a two-month extension of the trade truce and Beijing confirmed a first AI dialogue. That evening Meta made Muse the centre of its Connect keynote, and overnight Google DeepMind's new chief said Gemini 4 could ship "much earlier" than year-end, DeepSeek's revenue run rate was reported at $1 billion, and OpenAI told a Texas court that Apple's ChatGPT integration had "dramatically underperformed."
