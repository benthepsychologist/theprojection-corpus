## Today's throughline

Wednesday's FOMC hike is still the story two ways at once this morning.
The 10-year Treasury yield climbed back above the psychological 5% level
to 5.016%, extending Wednesday's post-hike move rather than reversing
it — the second consecutive day this map's own "fiscal dominance" test
(can Treasury's buyback campaign hold the long end against a hiking Fed)
has resolved against the intervention thesis. At the same time, equities
are attempting a rebound this morning, and CNBC is explicit about why:
it's being driven by falling oil, not by anything Treasury is doing.
That oil move is real and large — Brent fell roughly 3.6% to about $102,
its sharpest single-day drop in this map's recent record, as Saudi
Arabia moved from "no restart timeline disclosed" to a concrete plan to
resume half the shut East-West pipeline's capacity within days and full
capacity within about six weeks, while also routing crude to Asian
refiners via ship-to-ship transfers near Oman. On the deals side,
CoreWeave filed to raise $3.0bn (plus a $500M option) in convertible
notes explicitly framed around reaching investment-grade credit — and
the stock sold off rather than rallied on the news, a second instance of
this map's own AI-credit-repricing watch line catching the market
reading fresh AI-infrastructure debt as a risk signal rather than a
confidence builder. **By the afternoon the oil move had become a real
equity rally rather than an attempted one** — the S&P 500 and Nasdaq
both up more than 1% intraday, chipmakers leading — and it pulled the
10-year Treasury yield back down with it, easing to roughly 4.95-4.97%
and snapping the eight-session rising streak that had carried it above
5% this morning; CNBC's framing held up through the session: this is
oil and a calmer post-Fed read driving the move, not Treasury's buyback
campaign. SoftBank's $10-20bn dollar-bond roadshow, due to conclude
today, was still unpriced as of this pass, and the Bank of Japan's
Thursday-Friday meeting heads into its final day with hike odds firmed
further (a Reuters poll of economists at roughly 97%) but no fresh
substantive news.
