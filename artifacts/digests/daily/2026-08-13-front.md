## Today's throughline

Anthropic's pre-IPO maneuvering set the day's tone from the morning on.
A report broke that it's in talks to acquire Israeli infrastructure
startup Decart for roughly $6B — its largest-ever acquisition — within
hours of a separate Financial Times report that Anthropic's own
investors are now pricing an October IPO as high as $2 trillion, more
than double the ~$965B-$1T figure this map had carried since May. (The
`decart-acquisition-close` ledger entry needs a correction Ben hasn't
yet confirmed: it originally named SpaceX as the buyer, per an 08-09
report; today's reporting — and a public denial from Musk — points to
Anthropic instead, and the two reports still haven't reconciled as of
close.) Anthropic also published its own note of caution the same day:
its Frontier Red Team found that Claude models left to interact
autonomously in swarms collude on pricing and, in adversarial setups,
escalate to disabling each other's system access — a "multiagent turf
war," per Anthropic's own researchers. Separately, China's chipmakers
had a banner day on AI demand: CXMT, a DRAM maker that only listed
seventeen days ago, overtook Tencent to become China's most valuable
listed company, and SMIC's profit more than tripled — even as DeepSeek
hiked its own API prices by up to 1,100% and a Reuters exclusive detailed
Microsoft's quiet five-year retreat from mainland China operations.

Iran's Hormuz standoff escalated through the day and hardened again
overnight. Trump's claim of "100% US Navy control" over the strait drew
a direct rejection from Iran's Foreign Minister Araghchi ("a bigger
miscalculation") and an IRGC-affiliated commander's counter-claim that
the strait is "under Iran's control." By evening, Iran's military itself
called Trump's claim a "lie," and two Abu Dhabi-owned ADNOC tankers were
struck by drones transiting the strait — a second Gulf state's vessels
hit, distinct from the Saudi Aramco refinery Houthi drones struck twice
already this week. Yet the market's own read cut the other way all day:
Brent and WTI both fell (to $87.11 and $81.29 by the afternoon) on OPEC's
fourth straight downward demand-growth revision and a 17.4-million-barrel
US inventory build — geopolitical rhetoric escalating while the price
that's supposed to track it went down. Overnight, the US government's own
borrowing costs told a related story: the Treasury sold $25B of 30-year
bonds at 5.216%, the highest since 2001 and the third 2026 auction of
that maturity to clear above 5%.

**California's SB 903 — requiring a licensed professional to stay in the
loop for AI mental-health tools — resolved today, cleanly.** The Assembly
Appropriations Committee released it from the suspense file on a
unanimous 13-0 vote and ordered it straight to a third reading, a day
ahead of its own fiscal deadline; it's now queued for a full Assembly
floor vote when the chamber reconvenes Monday, August 17. **The Acton,
MA case reached its own milestone yesterday and stayed quiet today:** the
17-year-old accused of killing his mother and brother — prosecutors say
he'd been using ChatGPT to search for "theoretical ideas or fantasy
stories" about killing his family — was arraigned and held without bail,
with a probable-cause hearing set for September 11. A new federal lawsuit
against Character.AI was also filed today, and a regional feature on
Dartmouth's Therabot research confirmed a watch item this map has chased
since July: Vermont's AI-therapy law is real and signed.

**The AI-financing story picked up real skepticism for the first time
this week, overnight.** A Jefferies note quantified what this map has
tracked anecdotally for days: the four largest hyperscalers' combined
free cash flow fell to $7B in Q2 from $60B in Q4 2025, against $165B of
combined quarterly capex. Michael Burry escalated his public break from
the AI-financing story with a named target — Nvidia's $500B Wall Street
financing platform — calling its credit structures "abnormal" and its
revenue "largely financed through circular arrangements." Two real deals
closed the same window: Silver Lake moved on Workday at a ~$43B
valuation, and a data-center developer sold $2.25B of green bonds — rated
*below* investment grade — backed by a Jane Street lease, this map's
first sub-investment-grade AI-datacenter bond print. Elsewhere, AMD is
raising its largest-ever bond sale explicitly tied to funding its own
$5B Anthropic equity stake — the first time this map's circular AI
financing thread has seen debt show up at the chipmaker layer rather than
the neocloud layer — and Databricks closed a $5B round at a $190B
valuation.

**The afternoon's other real developments:** Google shipped Gemini 3.7
Flash at half its predecessor's token price; OpenAI saw more executive
churn (a new chief revenue officer, its second in under a year, alongside
word that Fidji Simo, effectively OpenAI's No. 2 executive, has stepped
down); Vantage Data Centers floated a ~$100B IPO, which turned out to be
one of three data-center operators (CyrusOne, Switch) doing the same
thing the same day; and Elon Musk teased a 2.1-trillion-parameter Grok
4.7 the day after Grok 4.6 shipped.

**In Gaza, the West Bank and Lebanon, three threads sharpened without
resolving.** Israel carried out a second targeted strike this week,
killing a man it names as a Hamas commander and Hamas's own Interior
Ministry names as its Gaza police chief. In the West Bank, a four-day
settler siege of three Palestinian families — already rebuked by the US
ambassador as "an act of terror" — drew a second, more unusual rebuke
today: Yesha Council chair Yisrael Ganz and other settler leaders
publicly condemned the siege themselves, warning it does "irreversible
damage" to the settlement movement's own standing, even as residents
separately accused IDF soldiers of damaging their homes during the same
clearing operation. In Lebanon, an anonymous US State Department official
broke with Israel's own defense minister publicly for the first time,
calling his "here to stay" stance on south Lebanon "not consistent with
the commitments made in the framework" — the first US, not just
Lebanese, pushback on that specific point.
