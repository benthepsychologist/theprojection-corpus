## Today's throughline

**The afternoon's finding is not in California. It is that a law this
lens has tracked as live for three weeks was struck down before it ever
took effect, and the map kept carrying it.** France's Conseil
constitutionnel voided **Article 1** of the law protecting minors from
social-media risks — the under-15 ban itself — on **14 August 2026**,
four days after this map logged the ban as a hard-identity-verification
regime taking effect 1 September. The decision was public the whole time.
**This morning's own run flagged the uncertainty and aimed it at the
wrong document**, calling for a direct read of the enacted text on
legifrance.gouv.fr; no reading of the enacted text would have shown a
constitutional-review decision published elsewhere. The dispositif is
one sentence: *"L'article 1er … est contraire à la Constitution."*

**The reasoning matters more than the outcome, because it answers a
question this lens has been asking all month in a way neither side of it
anticipated.** The entry existed to test hard identity verification
against Australia's self-declaration model — a natural experiment in
whether real age-gating changes behaviour or just moves the workaround.
The Council never reached that question. It held the ban an interdiction
of general scope, imposed without regard to the individual minor or to
the risks specific to each service, and then made the point that ends
the entry: barring under-15s necessarily implies **every** user, adults
included, must prove their age — and *"le législateur n'a pas prévu les
garanties légales"* defining the conditions or limits of that check.
**The ban did not fall because France chose the wrong verification
method. It fell because the verification it implied was left legally
undefined.** Any replacement statute must define those safeguards to
survive review, which would make the eventual French model a stronger
test than the one just lost — whenever it arrives.

**The California picture did not move, and the reason turned out to be
simpler than this morning's explanation for it.** The morning digest read
the absent floor votes as leginfo lagging same-day action by hours. The
Assembly's own adjournment notice says the chamber's next floor session
was **14:00 PT / 17:00 ET today** — after this run's window closes.
**There was no vote to lag, because the chamber had not reconvened.**
Right observation, wrong explanation. SB 903, SB 1119 and AB 2575 all
remain exactly where they were at 10:00 ET, and every one of them still
has to clear before tomorrow's constitutional adjournment.

## Regulation & legislation

- **France's Constitutional Council struck down the under-15 social-media
  ban as unconstitutional on 14 August, sixteen days before it was due to
  take effect.** Décision n° 2026-911 DC declares Article 1 of the law
  protecting minors from social-media risks *contraire à la
  Constitution*, on the grounds that it was a prohibition of general
  scope taken without regard to the individual minor or to per-service
  risk, and that it implied a universal age-verification duty for which
  the legislature *"n'a pas prévu les garanties légales"* — no defined
  conditions, no limits, no safeguards. **The natural experiment this
  lens was set up to observe — hard-ID France against declaration-based
  Australia and Malaysia — cannot be run, because the French leg no
  longer exists.** ✏️ This also corrects this map's 08-10 entry, which
  described the ban as live law requiring passport, licence or national
  ID; that entry rested on a single advocacy-outlet source for a claim
  about what a statute requires, while the statute and its constitutional
  review were both published and free to read.
  ([Conseil constitutionnel, Décision n° 2026-911 DC du 14 août 2026](https://www.conseil-constitutionnel.fr/decision/2026/2026911DC.htm),
