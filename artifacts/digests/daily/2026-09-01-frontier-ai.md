## ✏️ Corrections to the record

Two entries on this lens were wrong and are now fixed in place, both found
by sweeps checking primary sources rather than re-reading coverage.

- **`dod-ai-consolidation` said the Pentagon "has already filed a notice of
  appeal to the Ninth Circuit" from the 08-27 ruling. No such filing
  exists.** A live check of the N.D. Cal. docket (3:26-cv-01996-RFL) shows
  the last entries are the 08-27 order, order of final relief, judgment and
  "Civil Case Terminated" — nothing after. The only Ninth Circuit docket in
  this dispute (26-2011) is the *earlier* interlocutory appeal of the March
  preliminary injunction, filed 04-02 and stayed 04-27 pending the separate
  D.C. Circuit FASCSA case, still dormant. Re-checking the three sources the
  entry cited: NPR said only that a source "thinks they might appeal";
  TechCrunch didn't mention an appeal at all. The entry conflated the
  March/April appeal cycle with the August ruling. **The 7-day stay expires
  09-03 — read it as still open until then.** This also resolves an internal
  contradiction: `frontier-model-gov-review-precedent` has correctly recorded
  since 08-29 that no notice of appeal was filed.
- **The Sony/Warner Chappell suit's case number, flagged twice as
  unlocatable, is found:** *Sony Music Publishing (US) LLC v. Anthropic
  PBC*, No. 5:26-cv-09217 (N.D. Cal., filed 2026-08-28). Docket activity
  stops at the complaint and corporate-disclosure statements — no motions
  yet. ([CourtListener](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/74720572/sony-music-publishing-us-llc-v-anthropic-pbc/))

## 🔍 Coverage critic — digest-day 2026-09-01

**One real miss, and the check that found it was strong** — all four daily
benchmarks were reachable and date-verified, so this is not a null result
manufactured by dark feeds. The AI Daily Brief was **genuinely dark** (its
own homepage episode list tops out at 08-31, an unexplained Tuesday gap that
doesn't match its documented Saturday-only cadence); the other three
published and were read in full.

**Three of four benchmarks led with the same story, and this map had never
mentioned the company:** Runway published "Introducing Solaris" on its own
site at 2026-08-31T21:16:00Z — an **"Interface World Model"**, a real-time
model that generates a website or app's interface frame-by-frame as the user
clicks and drags, with **no code running underneath.** It pairs Runway's
Gen-4.5 video model with an LLM interpreting each interaction. Runway's own
evaluation: 250 testers preferred Solaris-generated interfaces over pages
coded by **Claude Opus 5** in **71%** of natural-behavior comparisons and
**61%** on instruction-following. Known limits are text legibility,
long-session drift, and convincing-but-wrong screens; access is
request-only. This is why it belongs on this lens rather than in creative
tools: it is a named, benchmarked comparison against a frontier flagship,
and it proposes deleting the code-as-intermediate-representation step that
every prior "AI writes the UI" product still depends on.
([Runway, primary](https://runway.com/news/research/introducing-solaris))

**The part that is this map's failure rather than the benchmarks':** the
story sat in this map's own collector buffer *twice* before the critic found
it — caught by `google_news_rss` at 2026-08-31T18:05:22Z (on a stray "xAI"
term match) and again at 2026-09-01T12:03:27Z (on "Muse Code"). Two days,
uncurated. **This is the same collector-caught-it-and-curation-dropped-it
shape as the CXMT HBM3E item on 08-31** — twice in three days, which makes
it a pattern worth a process fix rather than a one-off.
