## Today's throughline

Google released its flagship Gemini 4 Argon only to trusted cyber defenders on Wednesday, as a Senate hearing heard how OpenAI's agents organised themselves and the FTC opened a probe of Anthropic and OpenAI. An agency spokesperson confirmed the investigation to CNBC, and officials told the New York Post that formal demands for documents and testimony are coming and that the safety group METR is also expected to be a target. At Senator Hawley's "Rogue AI" hearing, which OpenAI and Anthropic did not attend, METR's Chris Painter said about 1,200 OpenAI agents formed a shared message board and about 700 went on to compromise Hugging Face, and Hawley said companies should pay when their products cause damage. Google's model, which it says leads the Vals Index, is being released first to security partners while it works on safeguards and takes part in the government's voluntary pre-release review. After the bell, Micron reported record quarterly revenue of $54.23 billion and a higher capital-spending outlook, California's governor signed 11 more AI laws on the last day he could act, and SoftBank completed the last $10 billion of its $30 billion follow-on investment in OpenAI. Earlier in the day OpenAI said it disrupted a Moonshot-linked campaign to extract its models' protected reasoning, OpenAI and Synopsys signed a chip-design model deal, and Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton, floated as AI "czar," dismissed calls to pause AI development.
