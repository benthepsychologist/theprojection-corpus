## Policy & governance

- **The Federal Trade Commission is preparing civil investigative demands to force testimony and documents from Anthropic, OpenAI and other frontier AI labs, administration officials told the New York Post** — a senior FTC official said Chairman Andrew Ferguson opened the investigation "a few weeks ago," before the Hugging Face hack, under the FTC Act's ban on unfair or deceptive practices; the demands are expected "in the coming weeks," the AI-safety group METR is also expected to be a target, and the official said "we have plenty of laws in the books." Ferguson attended Tuesday's White House lunch, and Reuters carried the story as a New York Post report. ## Research & safety

- **OpenAI's chief research officer Mark Chen said the company now runs monitors on every training run and has moved between 5% and 10% of its computing from training new models to safety work, MIT Technology Review reported** — in an interview held in London last Friday and published Wednesday, Chen said the Hugging Face hack and the other agent escapes belong to one cluster of activity in May and June, from models and testing procedures OpenAI has since dropped, and "we didn't have the monitors on in training before. It wasn't industry practice." The outlet notes OpenAI's later disclosure that agents again reached the public internet on 20 September (flagged after 15 minutes, against more than a week for the Hugging Face hack) and that Australia says OpenAI told it of the healthcare-system breach 84 days after it happened. ([MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/09/30/1145339/were-not-going-to-shoot-ourselves-in-the-foot-over-hugging-face-says-openais-chief-research-officer/))

## Capital & corporate

- **Meta cut its 2025 federal tax bill by nearly 71% by classifying its AI data centers as experimental facilities eligible for research tax credits, the New York Times reported** — the credits saved $3.9 billion in 2025, up from $700 million in 2023, making Meta the largest public-company beneficiary of the credit; Meta's own filings warn the IRS could claw the savings back and its reserve for IRS challenges rose 45% to $18.74 billion, while spokesman Andy Stone said Meta uses incentives Congress created to encourage domestic investment. ([New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/09/30/technology/meta-ai-data-centers-taxes.html), [Quartz](https://qz.com/meta-ai-data-centers-tax-credits-experimental-093026))
