## Today's throughline

Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner reached Kyiv by train on Sunday for the
first visit to Ukraine's capital by this administration's envoys, a day
after roughly three hours with Putin, and the Kremlin's pause on strikes
against the city held through its first night while nine people were
killed elsewhere in Ukraine. They walked with Zelensky from the railway
station to St Sophia's Square; the visit that was "in doubt" on Friday
happened as scheduled, by rail because Russian strikes had ruled out a
direct flight. The pause held in exactly
the shape Peskov stated — nothing hit Kyiv, 108 drones and missiles hit
the rest of the country — and the only readout by mid-morning was the
White House's promise of "substantive plans for next steps" in coming
weeks. Five hundred kilometres to the south-east, a second and
narrower ceasefire was already in force: an IAEA-brokered truce that let
technicians start repairing the Zaporizhzhia plant's last power line,
sixteen days into a diesel-generator outage the agency says could become
a station blackout within days, and an outage this map had not recorded.

Iran answered Saturday's tanker strikes with words about scale. Parliament
speaker Ghalibaf told the assembly the "era of proportionate responses" is
over and any further violation will draw a "faster, heavier, and more
painful" reply — conceding in the same speech "fundamental challenges" from
inflation, unemployment and disrupted oil trade — with the market that
prices the Gulf reopening at 18:00 ET that evening, against Brent's last
settlement of $96.28. Yemen's western-coast war entered a third day, Israel struck south
Lebanon for a second consecutive night, Gaza City buried about a hundred
people recovered from the rubble of Zeitoun, and Saxony-Anhalt voted in a
state election the AfD entered as the clear favourite, with the result
due after this run's cut.

The AI lens had no Sunday development and two Friday ones it had missed.
Anthropic said on Friday that Claude produced a complete, computer-checked
proof of Fermat's Last Theorem in Lean over eleven days — thirteen million
lines, the largest Lean proof ever written — and OpenAI's Astra launch
materials on Wednesday included a paper narrowing the bounded gap between
primes to 186 "due to GPT 6 Astra." Each of the week's two frontier labs
shipped a pure-mathematics claim alongside its product news, and neither
reached this map until the coverage critics read Saturday's buffer for
names instead of thread terms: the Fermat announcement had been in that
buffer six times across two days. The capital lens's weekend was
statements pointed at next week — CNBC's count of the president, the vice
president, the Treasury secretary and Navarro all publicly against a
September hike in one week, ten days out; Japan's trade minister saying the
$550 billion investment pact will weigh AI and chips "very significantly"
in its next round. And the mental-health lens's one weekend story was the
Wall Street Journal's: Musk-circle money has recapitalized Lykos, the
sponsor of the rejected MDMA application, and renamed it.

The afternoon counted the election and finished the visit. Saxony-Anhalt
gave the AfD about 45% — its best result in any state election, the CDU
halved to 16-18%, turnout up ten points to 70% — and left it one to three
seats short of governing alone, with the arithmetic against it running
through a 5% threshold the BSW sits on by a tenth of a point; Chrupalla
claimed a "clear mandate," Siegmund ruled out minority arrangements, the
Linke ruled out everything, and the Thuringia model — a CDU minority
government leaning on the Linke — is what the calculator leaves. In Kyiv,
Witkoff said Moscow yielded "significant progress" and "new ideas," both
envoys said a return to trilateral talks should be announced shortly,
British, French and German security advisers joined a further round, and
Zelensky said the war looks set to run into winter. On Iran, the Energy
Secretary said on ABC that "there may not be a nuclear agreement" and the
answer "may be simply destroying their capabilities," while TankerTrackers
put August's Middle East export deficit at 7.2 million barrels a day —
down from 12.4 in May — with Iran's share expected to stay at zero.
