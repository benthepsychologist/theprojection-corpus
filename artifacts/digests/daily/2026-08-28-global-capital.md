## Today's throughline

**The Fed chair used his first Jackson Hole keynote to talk about
artificial intelligence and to refuse, at length and on principle, to say
anything about interest rates — and said nothing at all about the fight
over the Fed's independence.** Kevin Warsh's "In Our Time," delivered at
10:00 ET on his hundredth day as chairman, opens by calling its own
outline "a trail map . . . just don't call it forward guidance," and it
means it: he explicitly declines to offer a reaction function, argues
that forward guidance "has overstayed its welcome" as a routine practice,
and makes the case for "a quieter Fed." On inflation he is direct — 12-month
PCE at **3.7%**, the six-month at **4.1%**, "running above our 2 percent
target," and "otherwise, we have work to do" — while calling labour
markets consistent with full employment. **What the market was actually
positioned for, he did not touch.** The words "independence,"
"administration," "buyback" and "average inflation" do not appear in the
speech at all; the single use of "Treasury" refers to Treasury securities
as a market signal. **This map went into the day with a specific,
logged question — whether the keynote would address the TGA and
long-end mechanism — and the answer is a clean no.** That is a resolved
negative, not a research gap.

**The market has now answered him, and the answer was hawkish — but the
long end barely participated.** By 15:04 ET, with the session still open,
every point on the curve this map reads had risen and the belly had risen
hardest: the five-year up about **9 basis points to 4.488%**, the
ten-year up about **6 to 4.728%**, the thirteen-week bill up about **5 to
3.730%** — and the **thirty-year up barely 2, to 5.213%**. Equities gave
up their pre-keynote gains (S&P 500 **-0.32%**, Nasdaq Composite
**-0.60%**), the dollar index rose **0.56%**, and gold fell hard. **That
shape is the finding, not the direction.** A repricing driven by fiscal
or term-premium fear shows up in the long end first; this one showed up
in the part of the curve that prices the next several policy meetings and
left the thirty-year almost untouched. **The market read a chairman who
refused to give a path as more likely to hold or hike, not as a fiscal
risk** — which is a point of evidence for `treasury-long-end-intervention`
running against the intervention thesis on this particular day.

⚠️ **And it repriced hawkishly on a day the only inflation-expectations
print available came in softer.** The University of Michigan's final
August survey, released this morning, showed year-ahead expectations
**falling** to 4.0% from July's 4.2%. **Rhetoric moved this market, not
data.**

**The close, read from Treasury's own curve the next day, moves the hump
and settles the number this map said it did not have.** Close-to-close
the **two-year rose 14 basis points to 4.34%** — the largest move on the
whole curve — with the three-year up 11, the five-year up 10, the ten-year
up 6, and the twenty- and thirty-year up 3 each. The repricing sat in the
two-to-three-year sector, not the five-year the intraday read had named,
and **the long end really did not move**, which is the point that matters
for `treasury-long-end-intervention` and now rests on the primary source.
A second Fed voice from the same mountain agreed with the chairman's
method and went further on the substance: Chicago Fed President Austan
Goolsbee told Odd Lots he is worried the economy is overheating and that
GDP is growing "mostly due to AI and the data center buildout." **And the
evening window, swept as its own pass for the first time, held the
mechanism behind the day's biggest single-name move**: Nvidia's paused
programme is **$36bn** of take-or-pay and revenue-share commitments, and
the clause that drew the antitrust concern let Nvidia decide which
customers a cloud provider could rent guaranteed capacity to.
