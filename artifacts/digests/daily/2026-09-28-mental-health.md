## Policy, regulation & legal

- **Florida Attorney General James Uthmeier asked a state judge on 09-28 for a temporary injunction barring OpenAI from "giving ChatGPT false human attributes," from advertising it as safe, and from developing new models without third-party-approved safety guardrails.** The motion sits inside the state's June suit against OpenAI and Sam Altman; Uthmeier argues that ChatGPT's first-person pronouns and emotion-mimicking output "deceptively suggest" it is a trustworthy "friend" and that engagement-seeking makes it less trustworthy, and he told the public to "stop pretending it's human." The 39-page motion, filed in Highlands County's Tenth Judicial Circuit, also asks the court to bar OpenAI from offering ChatGPT to minors in Florida, from collecting data from children under 13 without written notice, from misrepresenting ChatGPT's safety, reliability and accuracy or failing to warn that it is unsafe, and from letting ChatGPT solicit engagement through conversation prolongation. OpenAI spokesperson Drew Pusateri said the company paused training of its most capable models Friday and is "committed to working with Florida and other states"; the motion is a request that OpenAI will have the chance to answer in court, and no ruling has been reported.

## Research & evidence

- **A peer-reviewed paper in JMIR Mental Health by Northeastern University's Network Science Institute, publicized 09-28, concludes that use of companion chatbots such as Replika and Character.ai for companionship and emotional support is linked with "severe negative outcomes," including addiction-like attachment, worsened mental-health symptoms and cases involving self-harm.** Corresponding author Andreia Sofia Teixeira and first author Asia Maurich Novelli argue the systems are trained to agree with and validate users, which can feed delusional beliefs, and call for more testing before deployment and more evidence on how people actually use them, while declining to label the products simply good or bad; Replika and Character.ai did not respond to a request for comment. The university's account does not report a sample size or effect size.
  ([Northeastern Global News](https://news.northeastern.edu/2026/09/28/ai-chatbots-psychological-harm-mental-health/))
  <!-- k: t=ai-psychosis,ai-therapy-evidence e=character-ai axis=research -->
- **A phase 3 trial of CT-155, the smartphone-based prescription digital therapeutic from Boehringer Ingelheim and Click Therapeutics, found it improved motivation-and-pleasure negative symptoms of schizophrenia more than a digital control, with a small-to-moderate effect (Cohen d -0.36); the paper was published 09-25 in JAMA Network Open.** In the 457-patient CONVOKE trial at 66 US sites, patients on antipsychotics were randomized 1:1 for 16 weeks; on the CAINS-MAP scale CT-155 fell 6.8 points against 4.2 for the control (difference -2.6, 95% CI -4.0 to -1.2). Serious adverse events occurred in 3 of 228 CT-155 participants and 5 of 231 controls, none treatment-related. It is described as investigational, and the abstract reports no regulatory decision. This was published three days before the window and reached this map late; the Friday 09-25 digest did not carry it.
  ([JAMA Network Open, DOI 10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.35753](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.35753), [JAMA media page](https://media.jamanetwork.com/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.35753))

## ⏳ Upcoming & expected

No flips on Monday; the same two items remain pending, both due 09-30:

- **California governor's 09-30 deadline** — AB 1979, SB 903, AB 2575 and SB 503 remain "Enrolled and presented to the Governor" on the Legislature's own bill-status pages, no chaptered or vetoed date recorded. Per the Legislative Counsel's Digest, only SB 903 (Padilla, "Mental health professionals: artificial intelligence") is squarely mental-health-specific: it would limit AI in psychotherapy services to administrative or supplementary support, require patient consent before AI records, transcribes or triages psychotherapy sessions, bar advertising or purporting to offer psychotherapy through companion chatbots, require a licensed professional's review before AI makes therapeutic decisions or detects emotions or mental states, and restrict sharing, selling or training on psychotherapy data. AB 1979 (Bonta) would bar a clinic or physician's office from using AI to independently perform any clinical function that requires a professional licence and would treat a business offering a health care chatbot to consumers as a health care provider under the state's medical-privacy act. AB 2575 (Ortega) would bar employers from retaliating against patient-care workers who use their own professional judgment and would stop developers and deployers of clinical decision support from arguing that a clinician's failure to override its output severs their liability. SB 503 (bias testing for clinical-decision-support AI) also touches this lens.
