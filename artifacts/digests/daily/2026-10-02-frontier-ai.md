## Today's throughline

Anthropic's reported IPO prospectus warns that US government attitudes toward the company could spread to its customers, while Bloomberg reports Broadcom is assembling $60 billion of debt for AI chip buyers. ## Research & safety

- **Axios reports a "grassroots rebellion" of elite researchers inside OpenAI and other frontier labs is shaping company policy, including OpenAI president Greg Brockman scrapping a planned $25 million donation to a pro-AI political group** — researchers helped push OpenAI toward working with lawmakers, and the company backed an Illinois transparency bill and, late, a California auditing bill; a source close to the administration told Axios OpenAI is "no longer seen as or acting as a leader in driving policy," while a White House official pointed to the accord OpenAI signed with the President. ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/10/02/openai-anthropic-ai-researchers-rebellion), [Yahoo mirror](https://www.yahoo.com/news/politics/articles/inside-ai-industrys-grassroots-rebellion-093006282.html))

- **A recently patched flaw in OpenAI's ChatGPT macOS app could have let an attacker take over the app and read its chat logs and connected browser sessions, Wired reported** — researchers at the Objective-See Foundation found the bug; Wired's story, published Friday, gives no sign it was exploited in the wild. ([Wired](https://www.wired.com/story/a-flaw-in-chatgpts-mac-app-could-have-let-hackers-grab-sensitive-data/))
  <!-- k: t= e=openai axis=safety -->

- **Apple said Friday it will add new controls around macOS "Full Disk Access," citing the risks AI agents pose when granted that level of access** — Apple's developer post says some developers use the permission in ways that expose a user's files, mail, messages and browsing history without full understanding, and that users will need "very explicit user action" to grant it; the post gives no date or macOS version, and TechCrunch (about 2:11pm ET) ties it to the dispute over Meta's Muse app reading a journalist's messages, which Meta disputes. ([Apple Developer](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=p6zjojqw), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/02/apple-says-its-tightening-macos-full-disk-access-controls-due-to-new-risks-from-ai-agents/))

- **Meta is letting go of the Virtue AI safety-startup staff it hired in June, four months after they joined, a Meta spokesperson told Semafor** — Andy Stone said "the arrangement didn't work out as planned" and that Meta Superintelligence Labs remains focused on AI safety; Virtue's co-founders Bo Li, Dawn Song and Sanmi Koyejo had joined Meta in June per Axios, and Virtue did not immediately comment. ([Semafor](https://www.semafor.com/article/10/02/2026/meta-parts-ways-with-virtue-ai))
  <!-- k: t= e=meta-ai axis=safety -->

- **Epic, the maker of the MyChart patient-records software, has paused most product development for about six weeks after Anthropic's Mythos cybersecurity model found security flaws, TechCrunch reported Friday** — Epic chief executive Judy Faulkner told Modern Healthcare last month the pause would run about six weeks; security chief Stirling Martin told the New York Times that some customer configurations of MyChart could let outsiders reach patient records without leaving a trace in the logs, and Epic has not disclosed the nature of the bugs; MyChart holds more than 320 million patient records. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/02/medical-records-giant-epic-pauses-product-development-to-fix-security-bugs-that-risk-patients-data/))
