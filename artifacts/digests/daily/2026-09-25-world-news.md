## Yemen / Iran

- **Saudi Arabia, Turkiye and Pakistan are arranging an urgent meeting of their military chiefs of staff in Riyadh under the Mecca Joint Defence Agreement after weeks of Houthi missile and drone attacks on Saudi territory, Al Jazeera and Reuters reported Friday — a step short of a formal invocation: Turkey's parliament has not yet ratified the pact, and Pakistan's and Turkiye's own statements have been markedly more cautious than Riyadh's.** Turkiye's Defence Ministry confirmed General Selcuk Bayraktaroglu is travelling to Saudi Arabia; Pakistan's Field Marshal Asim Munir is also expected, though Pakistan's military media wing did not confirm his departure. The pact, signed 08-07, treats an attack on any of Saudi Arabia, Turkiye or Pakistan as an attack on all three; Turkiye's parliament speaker said Friday it has not yet been submitted for ratification, expected 10-01, and Reuters reports any Turkish support "at this stage would be largely bilateral" rather than under the pact itself. The Houthis, who seized Yemen's Red Sea coast and Bab al-Mandeb strait islands earlier this month (already on this thread), have since pushed toward Yemen's highlands and made escalating claims against Saudi Arabia; Saudi Arabia's Grand Mufti called Friday for forces to "stand united" against the Houthis, and King Salman separately accused them of attempting to strike Mecca again. Analysts told Al Jazeera Pakistan and Turkiye's own public statements on the New York foreign-ministers' meeting that preceded this (Pakistan's Dar and Turkiye's Fidan, both Thursday) were notably more cautious than Saudi Arabia's, naming no Houthi attacks; a Houthi political-bureau member had threatened both countries with an "iron fist" if they entered the war, and analysts cited Pakistan's own strained forces (Afghan border fighting, India tensions, domestic attacks) as a real constraint on any troop deployment. **The meeting itself took place in Riyadh Friday afternoon**, Turkiye's Defence Ministry and Daily Sabah reported: General Bayraktaroglu met Field Marshal Munir and Saudi chief of general staff General Fayyadh bin Hamed Al-Ruwaili in person, discussing regional air and maritime security, intelligence sharing and common deterrence measures against ballistic-missile and drone threats. Neither the Saudi, Turkish nor Pakistani side issued a joint statement or announced concrete commitments; Al Jazeera separately reported Saudi air defences shot down six ballistic missiles aimed at Taif and Yanbu on Thursday, and that fighting inside Yemen killed at least 150 fighters — 98 government-aligned, 52 Houthi — in the 24 hours to Friday, per an AFP tally, with more than 230,000 people newly displaced since the start of September, UNHCR said. France has separately offered its own defensive support: President Emmanuel Macron told TF1 Thursday that Paris "will send military assets, meaning soldiers, radars and defence systems," to protect the Yanbu terminal specifically — support outside the Mecca pact and not yet reflected as a formal deployment.

## Horn of Africa

- **Tigray's ruling party, the Tigray People's Liberation Front, ordered private and state-owned telecom operators to cut phone and internet access across the region late Thursday, four sources including a diplomatic source told Reuters — a shutdown the TPLF itself denies responsibility for, blaming the federal government instead.** One source said the restrictions may be a TPLF tactic to blunt the government's advantage in using battlefield drones, which rely on the mobile network; NetBlocks confirmed the disruption, and calls to Ethio telecom numbers were not going through Friday. The shutdown has revived fears of the 2020-2022 war, when a federal-government-imposed blackout accompanied famine-like conditions in Tigray; residents rushed to withdraw cash and stock supplies, and one Mekelle resident described people being "forced to join the war effort" by TPLF fighters going house to house. Tigrayan forces and allies have seized three local airports and pushed into Afar and Amhara in what they call a defensive war; Ethiopia's federal government has accused Eritrea of financially backing the TPLF and its allies, which Asmara denies. The UN and EU have called for restraint and a return to the African Union-brokered peace process.
  ([Reuters via the Straits Times](https://www.straitstimes.com/world/internet-and-phone-network-disrupted-in-ethiopias-war-hit-tigray-region))
  <!-- k: t=horn-of-africa-war axis=ethiopia -->
- **Ethiopian forces battled overnight to repel a Tigrayan-rebel assault near the Afar town of Abala, using at least one drone, a humanitarian source told AFP Friday; heavy clashes had also hit North Wollo zone in Amhara on Thursday, including around the UNESCO-listed rock churches at Lalibela.** Analysts told AFP the TPLF's seven-group armed alliance appears to be repeating its 2020-22 strategy — pushing south through Amhara toward Addis Ababa while a separate push through Afar aims to cut the supply corridor from Djibouti, which handles almost all of Ethiopia's trade cargo including fuel. The federal government, which has roughly 500,000 troops, a large drone arsenal and UAE backing, only this month had US sanctions tied to the 2020-22 war's atrocities lifted; the US embassy in Addis Ababa has sided with the government, saying it was "disappointed" by the TPLF's offensive. Rights groups have separately accused the TPLF of a forced-recruitment campaign this year.

## Russia / Ukraine

- **The United States has proposed a new trilateral meeting of Ukraine, Russia and the US in the United Arab Emirates, President Volodymyr Zelensky said Friday, with both Kyiv and Moscow confirming the plan to relaunch talks; the Kremlin said a meeting "could" happen soon but gave no date.** Zelensky said Washington has not yet proposed a date. Putin's envoy Kirill Dmitriev met Witkoff and Kushner in New York on Thursday (already on 09-24's digest); Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said those specific talks were about "potential economic cooperation," a narrower framing than the Ukrainian and US side's characterisation of talks covering a peace settlement too. Zelensky separately said Trump told him Ukraine will be allowed to produce Patriot air-defence missiles under licence, though any new production would take more than a year to reach service; Zelensky said Russia "will only escalate as winter approaches unless there is pressure" and called for stronger US and European sanctions enforcement. Overnight, Russian strikes killed a 14-year-old boy and injured at least seven others when a 16-storey building was hit in Kyiv's Pechersk district, and killed one person and injured another in "massive" strikes on a residential area and a postal depot in Odesa, Governor Oleh Kiper said; Russia's Rostov region reported about 50 Ukrainian drones shot down and a drone-damaged Novoshakhtinsk refinery forced to suspend operations, and Zelensky said Ukraine had also struck refineries in Bashkortostan and Samara in the past 24 hours.

## Gaza & Israel at the UN

- **The US-created Board of Peace has met UNRWA officials at least three times since Gaza's ceasefire despite publicly saying the UN Palestinian refugee agency has no role in the strip's future, former UNRWA chief Philippe Lazzarini told the Associated Press in his first interview since leaving the post, corroborated by two senior UN officials and a Board of Peace official.** The Board official confirmed the meetings but said they were about making UNRWA's operations "far more efficient and accountable," insisting "UNRWA's days are over" even as its roughly 11,000 Gaza staff (more than any other agency) run about half of Gaza's public health system and 14,000 daily medical appointments. Israel wants UNRWA out of Gaza over unproven Hamas-infiltration allegations the International Court of Justice says have not been substantiated; reconstruction has not begun almost a year after the ceasefire because Israel still bars entry to the Palestinian technocratic committee pending Hamas disarmament. Jordan's foreign minister told a 50-country ministerial meeting Thursday that UNRWA remains "indispensable."
  ([AP via ClickOnDetroit](https://www.clickondetroit.com/news/world/2026/09/25/board-of-peace-has-met-with-unrwa-despite-saying-it-has-no-gaza-role-agencys-ex-chief-says/))

- **Congo's North Kivu province has become the new epicentre of the country's Ebola outbreak, with all 29 beds full at one Butembo treatment centre this week and two new centres opened to cope, Reuters reported Friday from Butembo; confirmed cases nationally reached 7,820 and deaths 3,779 as of Tuesday, per government data, up from 7,773/3,759 reported 09-24.** North Kivu now accounts for about a third of new infections nationally and has a fatality rate above 60%, against a 48% national average; WHO said transmission is slowing in the worst-hit province, Ituri, but a seventh province, South Ubangi, was newly affected last week, and MSF's emergency coordinator said "the outbreak is not shrinking; it is moving." Community mistrust is hampering the response: residents in Butembo have attacked decontamination and burial teams, a health post was burned after residents accused it of collaborating with responders, and some deny Ebola exists because no vaccine or treatment exists yet for this outbreak's rarer Bundibugyo strain (unlike the more familiar Zaire strain).
