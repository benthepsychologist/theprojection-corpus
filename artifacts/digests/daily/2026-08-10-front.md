# The Projection — 2026-08-10

## Today's throughline

**2026-08-10 is the day the AI buildout's financing moved off the
principals' balance sheets and onto everyone else's.** Two unrelated
structures landed within hours. Nvidia signed memorandums of
understanding — not final agreements — with Apollo, BlackRock,
Blackstone, Brookfield, KKR and Goldman Sachs to mobilize over $500
billion of third-party capital, treating GPU compute as a
collateralizable real asset to be lent against like commercial real
estate rather than as depreciating hardware. Separately, Anthropic,
Macquarie Asset Management and Singapore's sovereign fund GIC launched
Theseus Infrastructure, which will own data centres outright and lease
them back to Anthropic as anchor tenant, with Macquarie and GIC funding
the majority of equity per project. One structure lends against the
chips; the other simply buys the buildings. Anthropic also signed a
$9.1B compute deal with a bitcoin-miner-turned-AI-landlord, and
Microsoft was reported in talks for 300,000+ custom chips from TSMC.
Three smaller markers pointed the same way: Bloomberg Opinion launched a
dedicated AI-credit-risk tracker, a JPMorgan-led $441M debt facility
closed for a two-year-old AI-infrastructure firm, and this map's own
CDS-widening entry now has company — AI-buildout debt is starting to be
underwritten and watched as its own risk category.

**The counter-pressure arrived the same day, and it is about who pays
for the electricity.** Anthropic's Theseus commitment includes covering
consumer power-price increases arising from its sites; OpenAI and Meta
both signed onto Texas's data-centre consumer-protection standards; and
NPR's campaign reporting established data-centre siting as a defining
bipartisan issue in the 2026 midterms, with primary results already
turning on it. A lab contractually absorbing ratepayer costs is a
concession that only makes sense against organised opposition. The
financing structure and the political structure are now visibly
responding to each other — that is the day's real story, not either half
alone. Congress pressed from both chambers too, the House pushing OpenAI
and Anthropic directly on the rogue-agent incidents and Sanders
threatening Senate action absent a voluntary pause, while Zuckerberg's
6,500-word case against concentrating frontier AI met a press reception
that ran skeptical rather than celebratory.

**Elsewhere.** Iran's war moved capital and territory at once: Trump
countered Iran's own compensation demand with one of his own, Brent
posted its steepest one-day jump of the war (+4.95% to $87.69), and Gulf
shipping data showed the Strait of Hormuz effectively shut — six vessels
transited Monday against a pre-war norm near 130-140. Russia hit
Zaporizhzhia and Kyiv with one of the war's larger single-night
barrages, killing at least six, with Zelenskyy alleging North Korean
missiles. In mental health, three jurisdictions handled AI-companion and
social-media risk three different ways in one week — China ordered
ByteDance, Alibaba and Tencent to pull companion apps outright, France
moved to hard government-ID age verification rather than
self-declaration, and a US think tank warned the ~100-bill state chatbot
patchwork risks repeating social media regulation's failures. Also
resolved: the Barcelona port stake held back yesterday for
single-sourcing — MSC and BlackRock's TiL unit formally withdrew their
EU merger request, now confirmed by four outlets.

**What the finalize pass added.** Running the coverage critics against
the day's benchmarks caught four frontier-AI stories the original sweep
missed. The largest: Anthropic's Claude improved a century-old bound
tied to the Riemann Hypothesis, lifting the proven share of
zeta-function zeros on the critical line from 41.6% to 67.2% — the work
of roughly 60 coordinated subagents over a day and a half, after 650
failed approaches, with the result machine-verified in Lean. Anthropic
is explicit that this is incremental progress on an existing line of
mathematics, not movement toward proving the hypothesis. Alongside it:
Amazon has bought an ~8,000-acre Texas site and contracted for power
from a private gas plant permitted to emit over 30 million tons of CO2 a
year; Google quietly open-sourced TPU Raiden, the first concrete step
toward externalising its TPU stack the way Nvidia externalised CUDA; and
North Korea's Kimsuky group was found running local, self-hosted AI
tooling to scale intrusion work without sending anything to a
commercial provider. On the capital side, Moody's warned that banks'
own AI adoption is concentrating dependence on a handful of
foundation-model vendors, and CoreWeave closed a $2.6B term loan at
SOFR+550 — a 10.44% yield, flexed wider from initial guidance of
S+425-450, which is the first confirmed transaction-level coupon in
this map's record of AI-buildout debt.
