## Today's throughline

Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner reached Kyiv by train on Sunday for the
first visit to Ukraine's capital by this administration's envoys, a day
after roughly three hours with Putin in Moscow, and the Kremlin's pause on
strikes against the city held through its first night while nine people
were killed elsewhere in Ukraine. **The visit that was "in doubt" on
Friday happened as scheduled**, by rail from Rzeszów because, Zelensky
said, Russian strikes had ruled out a direct flight; the two walked with
him from the station to St Sophia's Square, and the only readout by
mid-morning was the White House's promise of "substantive plans for next
steps" to be announced in coming weeks. The pause did what Peskov said it
would and no more: nothing hit Kyiv, and 108 drones and missiles hit the
rest of the country. Five hundred kilometres to the south-east a second,
narrower ceasefire was already running — the IAEA-brokered truce that let
technicians start repairing the Zaporizhzhia plant's last power line,
sixteen days into a diesel-generator outage the agency says could become a
station blackout within days.

**Iran answered Saturday's tanker strikes with words, and the words were
about scale.** Parliament speaker Ghalibaf told the assembly that the "era
of proportionate responses" to US attacks is over and that any further
violation of Iran's "interests and security" would draw a "faster, heavier,
and more painful" reply; Iranian forces separately claimed to have hit a US
unmanned vessel near Hormuz, unconfirmed by Washington. It is a statement,
not an action, and it arrived with the market that prices the Gulf closed
until Tuesday. Yemen's western-coast war entered a third day with the
government claiming Hais district and four civilians killed by shelling on
the Taiz–Aden road; Israel struck south Lebanon for a second consecutive
night after two more Hezbollah drones, four killed; and Gaza City buried
about a hundred people whose remains had been recovered from the rubble of
Zeitoun in one of the largest single funerals in Palestinian history.

**The afternoon brought the result.** Saxony-Anhalt gave the AfD about
45% of the vote — its best showing in any state election, the CDU halved
to 16-18%, turnout up ten points — and left it one to three seats short
of governing alone, with the arithmetic against it running through a
5% threshold the BSW sits on by a tenth of a point. AfD co-leader
Chrupalla claimed a "clear mandate" and named Ulrich Siegmund the next
premier; Siegmund ruled out minority arrangements and the BSW; the Linke
ruled out everything; the alternative tagesschau's calculator leaves is a
CDU-led minority government reliant on the Linke, the Thuringia model.
The Kyiv visit produced its readout after the morning's cut: Witkoff said
Moscow yielded "significant progress" and "new ideas," both envoys said
a return to trilateral talks should be announced shortly, British, French
and German security advisers joined a further round, and Zelensky said
territory is a leaders-level question and the war looks set to run into
winter. And Washington's Sunday answer on Iran came from the Energy
Secretary rather than the Pentagon: "there may not be a nuclear
agreement... it may be simply destroying their capabilities to do it,"
while the IRGC said the US "has no way but to leave the region."

None of it is a flash — including the election. A scheduled vote whose
leader had polled at 40% for months produced the result the polls
predicted, larger; that it may seat the first far-right state government
in Germany since 1945 makes it the day's biggest story and a `major`
development on the record, not a 9/11, an invasion or a market halt. The
Kyiv readout and the Wright statement are running states inside running
wars.
