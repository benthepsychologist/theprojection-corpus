## Today's throughline

**The rate story went global overnight, then reversed at home by
midday.** Japan's 10-year government bond yield pushed toward its highest
since 1996 on fiscal-policy jitters around the Takaichi government's
FY2027 budget, and Tokyo's own AI-proxy stocks broke the same way
Nvidia/AMD/Micron did Monday in New York: **Nikkei −2.5%, SoftBank
−8%+, Kioxia −9-13%, Arm −6% in US trading, Tokyo Electron −4.4%** — a
Daiwa strategist named the mechanism explicitly: higher yields raise
hyperscaler borrowing costs and revive doubts about AI capex payback
periods, the identical logic this map has been tracking domestically all
week. Hours later the US side of the same story reversed: the Treasury
**doubled the size of its long-bond buyback operations**, and the 30-year
yield that hit 5.32-5.34% Tuesday fell to **5.196%**, with US equity
futures and the SK Hynix-led chip complex rallying on the news. In
between, a second echo of this map's own NVIDIA-13F finding turned up in
SoftBank's own 13F: **67% of its $18B disclosed US equity book is a
single Intel position, and its growth since Q1 is entirely mark-to-market
— no new shares bought**, the same pattern this map found in NVIDIA's
Intel stake a week ago. Google, meanwhile, bought its way into the custom-
chip supply chain with a warrant rather than a check: a right to buy up
to $12.2B of Marvell stock, vesting against future chip revenue rather
than paid upfront.
