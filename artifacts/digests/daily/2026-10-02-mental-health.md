## Policy, regulation & legal

- **Oklahoma began issuing Counseling Compact privileges at 10am ET on Thursday, 10-01, becoming the tenth state to let licensed professional counselors from other member states practice there, according to the compact commission.** The American Counseling Association's release, dated 10-01 at 6:45pm ET, says Oklahoma joins nine states already issuing privileges (Arizona, Minnesota, Ohio, Louisiana, Georgia, Indiana, Arkansas, Wyoming and Tennessee) and that 40 states and jurisdictions have passed the compact's legislation; the compact lets a counselor licensed in one member state practice in person or by telehealth in the others. The commission's notice and the association's release are the only sources, and both are advocacy-side. This happened Thursday and was found Friday afternoon.
  ([Counseling Compact commission](https://counselingcompact.gov/10-states-now-offering-privileges/), [American Counseling Association via Newswise](https://www.newswise.com/articles/history-is-made-oklahoma-allows-counselors-to-practice-across-state-lines))

- **HHS Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. announced in Houston on 10-02 that eight communities will receive $96 million over four years, $3 million a year each, under the new STREETS program for people who are homeless and have serious mental illness, addiction or both.** The department's release names Montgomery County (Ohio), the Cherokee Nation, Detroit, Houston, Huntington (West Virginia), Miami-Dade County, Robeson County (North Carolina) and Tangipahoa Parish (Louisiana); the Substance Abuse and Mental Health Services Administration administers the program, which HHS says puts its "Treatment First" approach into action by pairing street-based engagement and treatment with recovery supports and housing. SAMHSA's chief of staff, Timothy Westlake, said "we are organizing systems around recovery instead of asking people in crisis to organize themselves around systems"; the figures and the description are HHS's own.
  ([HHS](https://www.hhs.gov/press-room/secretary-kennedy-announces-96-million-streets-program-homelessness-addiction.html))

The counts and rates are VA's own. This happened Wednesday 09-30 and was found Saturday.

## Product & market

- **Talkiatry, which calls itself the largest private employer of psychiatrists in the United States, said on 10-02 that its therapy services are now available in all 50 states, with more than 400 directly employed therapists, 85% licensed in more than one state.** The New York company's release says it is the in-network psychiatry partner for more than 70 health systems with more than 170 million covered lives, that the expansion follows a $210 million Series D round earlier this year and a partnership with the American Medical Group Association, and that patients who see both a Talkiatry therapist and psychiatrist stay in care twice as long as those receiving psychiatric care alone; the retention figure and the coverage numbers are the company's own.
  ([Talkiatry via PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/talkiatry-expands-therapy-nationwide-now-available-alongside-psychiatric-care-in-all-50-states-302896750.html))
  <!-- k: axis=product -->

- **Headway, the clinician-credentialing platform valued at $2.3 billion in 2024, will charge clinicians $9.99 a session for patients who find them through its new opt-in marketplace, starting 10-08 in five states, and clinicians pushed back.** Behavioral Health Business reported on 10-02 that clinicians on and off the platform objected on social media and to the outlet that the fee squeezes practices already pressed by reimbursement rates; Headway says the fee does not apply to clients who book through a provider's own link or to existing caseloads, that providers can opt out of the marketplace without penalty, that it has invested $150 million in the marketplace and patient app this year, and that it has 90,000 providers. Headway's help center says the fee starts 10-08 for providers whose primary practice state is Virginia, Washington, Georgia, Ohio or Illinois and later in 2026 elsewhere, and applies to every session with a marketplace client for as long as the provider sees them; the fee terms and the investment figures are the company's own. Behavioral Health Business posted the story at about 4:17pm ET Friday; Headway announced the changes on Thursday 10-01.
  ([Behavioral Health Business](https://bhbusiness.com/2026/10/02/headway-faces-backlash-after-unveiling-new-opt-in-patient-marketplace-with-10-per-session-fee/), [Headway help center](https://help.headway.co/hc/en-us/articles/54117060370068-Understanding-the-marketplace-service-fee), [Upheal](https://www.upheal.io/blog/headway-marketplace-fee))
