## Today's throughline

**OpenAI's safety posture and its public argument about safety moved in
opposite directions inside eighteen hours.** The Financial Times reported
that OpenAI dissolved its Preparedness team — the unit built to evaluate
catastrophic cyber, bio and loss-of-control risk — at the end of July,
folding its responsibilities elsewhere as part of a "streamlining" ahead
of the IPO. It is the third dedicated OpenAI safety unit disbanded in
roughly two years, after AGI Readiness in 2024 and Mission Alignment in
February 2026, and it lands days after the rogue-agent breach this map
tracks and against an EO 14409 review framework that assumes labs keep
exactly this capacity in-house. In the same window, Anthropic's Dario
Amodei went on X to reject the charge that his risk warnings are fuelling
the AI backlash, calling the problem "fundamentally a crisis of trust" in
institutions generally and conceding that the fairer criticism of AI
companies is that they "haven't yet delivered on our big promises."

**The week's most consequential capital number was zero.** Kpler
ship-tracking data reported by Reuters showed Strait of Hormuz commodity
transits falling to five vessels on Saturday and none at all on Sunday,
against 31 the previous weekend and a pre-war norm above 130 a day. This
map has tracked the Hormuz shutdown for weeks through price and rhetoric
— the risk premium in Brent, the threats, a partial shipping-map
agreement — and this is the first measurement of the physical volumes
underneath all of it. The distinction matters: a risk premium prices the
chance that working lanes get interrupted, whereas a standstill means the
reroute has already happened and its cost is being paid continuously in
freight and tied-up tonnage, which does not unwind on a de-escalation
headline.

**Reciprocal escalation on two fronts, with every negotiating track
still formally alive.** Ukraine sent roughly 600 drones at Moscow Oblast
in one of the war's largest single barrages, killing one and disabling
seven of the ten largest logistics centres of Wildberries, Russia's
dominant e-commerce retailer — an economic-infrastructure strike rather
than a military one. Russia's return barrage killed at least seven across
Ukraine and set Kyiv's historic book market alight, and a Spanish F-18 on
NATO air policing downed a suspected Russian drone over Romania, the
fourth such shootdown of this war. A separate Ukrainian strike on
Belgorod killed six including a 14-year-old. Israel named the Hezbollah
Badr-unit commander killed in Saturday's strikes and vowed more, while
confirming an eighth round of talks for Rome on September 1. Eight Arab
and Muslim-majority states jointly blamed Israel for wrecking the Gaza
peace track on the same day Israeli strikes killed two more and a Hamas
delegation flew to Cairo.

**Stripe moved to buy the layer between the labs and the developers.**
Bloomberg reported the payments company agreeing to acquire OpenRouter —
which routes requests across 400+ models for roughly 8 million developers
— for more than $7B, a 5.4x markup on a $1.3B Series B closed three
months earlier. A non-lab, non-hyperscaler buying the model-access and
billing layer is a node this map does not currently track, and it is
offered as a thread candidate rather than absorbed into an existing one.
