## Iran

- **Iran's Revolutionary Guard Corps warned Monday that any new US attack
  would bring "significant changes" to its "defense and counteroffensive,"
  including a shift in "the geography of the war" and the use of weapons
  it has not yet deployed.** IRGC spokesman Brigadier General Hossein
  Mohebbi said Iran has "new goals that have not yet been targeted" and
  that the US "lacks the courage to admit" what Tehran characterises as a
  historic defeat in the war so far. This directly follows Sunday's claim that Iran's military command had been told the US is
  preparing to resume strikes; as of this morning, no US or allied
  government has confirmed or denied any strike preparation, and roughly
  seven months into the stated ceasefire neither side has moved to
  full-scale resumption. ⚠️ This is IRGC rhetoric, not a confirmed
  operational move by either side — carried because it escalates the
  specific threat register (new weapons, new geography) beyond Sunday's
  more general warning.

## Russia

- **Russia's Central Election Commission now reports United Russia has
  won 355 of the State Duma's 450 seats, a supermajority exceeding the
  party's own 2016 record of 343, with electoral chief Ella Pamfilova
  saying more than 95% of votes have been counted and turnout exceeded
  59%.** The Communist Party takes 40 seats, the LDPR 25, and New People
  20 on the same count. Final results remain due for certification on Friday. ⚠️ Still the Russian state's own account of its own election —
  the standing caveat on every figure this vote has produced.

## Germany — post-election

- **Chancellor Friedrich Merz is holding his reform course with no
  personnel or policy change, and today the public pressure widened
  beyond his own party: Manuela Schwesig, the SPD's incumbent state
  premier of Mecklenburg-Vorpommern — the state the CDU was just shut out
  of — said she was "very irritated" by Merz's defiant remarks and that
  "more humility is actually called for on the part of the Chancellor."**
  Sunday's pressure came only from within Merz's own CDU (Hamburg chief Dennis Thering's "deliver" line, former
  general secretary Mario Czaja's criticism); a coalition partner's own
  sitting state premier criticising him by name is a wider and more
  consequential source of pressure. No resignation, no confirmed
  Bundesrat consequence.
  ([PBS News, AP](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/world/merz-vows-to-press-ahead-with-reforms-after-partys-historic-defeat-in-2-german-state-elections))

## Gaza

- **Two more Palestinians were killed by Israeli forces this morning:
  Ismail al-Bardawil, a child, died of gunshot wounds in the al-Mawasi
  area of Rafah, and Muhsen Ahmad Mustafa Bahja was shot in the head by an
  Israeli quadcopter drone in northern Gaza.** The Palestinian Health
  Ministry's cumulative post-ceasefire toll now stands at 1,395 killed and
  more than 4,830 injured, with 831 bodies recovered from rubble. Neither
  incident breaks the low-boil pattern of recent weeks. ⚠️ Figures are
  from Gaza's Health Ministry and the families.

## Japan — Typhoon Dujuan

- **Typhoon Dujuan put more than 1.9 million people in six prefectures — Ibaraki, Chiba, Tokyo, Kanagawa, Shizuoka and Saitama — under evacuation orders as it battered the greater Tokyo region on Monday, with sustained winds of 143 km/h and gusts to 198 km/h.** Firefighters in Kanagawa feared a woman had been buried when a landslide crushed a house, a man in Chiba was hospitalised after being trapped while clearing debris, and more than 50,000 households lost power. Japan's evacuation orders are advisory rather than mandatory.
  ([BBC via Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/weather-news/articles/japan-warns-flood-landslides-powerful-055350378.html))
