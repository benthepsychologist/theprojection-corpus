## Yemen

- **After Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman pressed him by phone to
  intervene against the Houthis' rapid advance in Yemen, Donald Trump
  ordered the Pentagon to prepare airstrikes on the group, then called them
  off at the last minute with bombs already being loaded onto aircraft, the
  New York Times reported Monday.** Houthi fighters are now moving to seize highlands
  that would cut the coast off from Saudi-backed government forces; the UN
  says the fighting has displaced at least 125,000 people since the start
  of September, with Taiz city alone taking in up to 86,000. Trump has
  otherwise stayed out of the fight directly, telling reporters this month
  the Houthis "don't want to fight us" and were "allowing ships to pass."
  The bombs-loaded detail is the New York Times's reporting, relayed by
  the Guardian; neither the Pentagon nor the White House has confirmed it
  on the record, though CNBC separately cites administration officials
  telling the Times that Trump has decided against bombing the Houthis
  for now.
  ([Guardian, citing NYT](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/sep/21/houthis-seize-strategic-terrain-yemen-trump-calls-off-airstrikes-last-minute))

## Russia

- **Russia's Central Election Commission now reports United Russia has
  won 355 of the State Duma's 450 seats, a supermajority exceeding the
  party's own 2016 record of 343, with electoral chief Ella Pamfilova
  saying more than 95% of votes have been counted and turnout exceeded
  59%.** The Communist Party takes 40 seats, the LDPR 25, and New People
  20 on the same count. Final results remain due for certification on Friday. ⚠️ Still the Russian state's own account of its own election —
  the standing caveat on every figure this vote has produced.
  ([Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/2026/09/21/russians-vote-in-final-day-of-parliamentary-elections))

  fought in the Ukraine war, party leadership said Monday, and the EU's
  top diplomat Kaja Kallas called the vote "another major setback for
  Russian democracy," saying "the Kremlin silenced dissent and shut out
  international election monitors."** Russia's election chief, Ella
  Pamfilova, put United Russia's final vote share at 57.83%, up from 49.8%
  in the 2021 election, with 95% of ballots counted.

## Germany — post-election

- **Chancellor Friedrich Merz is holding his reform course with no
  personnel or policy change, and today the public pressure widened
  beyond his own party: Manuela Schwesig, the SPD's incumbent state
  premier of Mecklenburg-Vorpommern — the state the CDU was just shut out
  of — said she was "very irritated" by Merz's defiant remarks and that
  "more humility is actually called for on the part of the Chancellor."**
  Sunday's pressure came only from within Merz's own CDU (Hamburg chief Dennis Thering's "deliver" line, former
  general secretary Mario Czaja's criticism); a coalition partner's own
  sitting state premier criticising him by name is a wider and more
  consequential source of pressure. No resignation, no confirmed
  Bundesrat consequence.
  ([PBS News, AP](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/world/merz-vows-to-press-ahead-with-reforms-after-partys-historic-defeat-in-2-german-state-elections))
  <!-- k: axis=politics -->
- **Merz rejected the idea that the election defeats were "a personal
  rebuke" on Monday, framing them instead as part of "a profound
  transformation of the political party system in Germany as well as in
  many other countries around the world," and said keeping his coalition
  together was "about nothing less, and nothing more, than the democratic
  future of our country."** Leif-Erik Holm, the AfD's top candidate in
  Mecklenburg-Vorpommern, predicted Merz "certainly won't make it to the
  end of the year." No CDU figure has yet called publicly for a leadership
  change.
  ([Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/sep/21/germany-chancellor-freidrich-merz-cdu-christian-democratic-union-state-election-defeats-mecklenburg-western-pomerania-berlin))

## Japan — Typhoon Dujuan

- **Typhoon Dujuan put more than 1.9 million people in six prefectures — Ibaraki, Chiba, Tokyo, Kanagawa, Shizuoka and Saitama — under evacuation orders as it battered the greater Tokyo region on Monday, with sustained winds of 143 km/h and gusts to 198 km/h.** Firefighters in Kanagawa feared a woman had been buried when a landslide crushed a house, a man in Chiba was hospitalised after being trapped while clearing debris, and more than 50,000 households lost power. Japan's evacuation orders are advisory rather than mandatory.
  ([BBC via Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/weather-news/articles/japan-warns-flood-landslides-powerful-055350378.html))
  <!-- k: axis=disaster -->
- **Typhoon Dujuan killed at least two people as it passed the Boso
  Peninsula into Monday evening: a 51-year-old man died in hospital in
  Sodegaura, Chiba, after a landslide engulfed the excavator he was
  operating on road-repair work, and a woman died in Yokosuka, Kanagawa,
  after a landslide sent mud into her house; one
  resident remains unaccounted for in neighbouring Miura, and Kanagawa
  prefecture requested Self-Defense Force deployment to help with
  rescues.** Oshima in the Izu Islands recorded 600mm of rain in the 24
  hours to 2pm local time — a September record for that observation site —
  prompting Japan's Meteorological Agency to issue its highest-level
  (Level 5) landslide special warning for the town.

## Americas

- **A UN special rapporteur concluded that US military strikes on
  alleged drug-trafficking boats in the Caribbean and Pacific may
  constitute crimes against humanity, finding "reasonable grounds to
  believe that the reported 68 attacks that killed 223 people constituted
  crimes against humanity of murder."** Ben Saul's report says the
  targets, even if members of designated terrorist groups, had not staged
  an armed attack on the US that would give a right of self-defence, and
  calls the strikes "serial extrajudicial killings." It came two days
  after the US military said it killed four people in a strike on a
  vessel in the Caribbean; the White House has not responded. The
  operation, "Southern Spear," has run since September 2025.
