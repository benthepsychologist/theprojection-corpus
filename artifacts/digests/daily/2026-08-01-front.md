# The Projection — 2026-08-01

## Today's throughline

The day's defining event is an absence. Two federal AI-governance
deadlines came due this morning and neither was met: Executive Order
14409's two 60-day deliverables — a classified NSA-led threshold defining
what counts as a "covered frontier model," and the design of the
framework giving government up to 30 days of pre-release access — and the
separately-tracked White House announcement of that same voluntary
framework, which turns out to be the same deliverable counted twice. As
of Wednesday it was still a draft in dispute with OpenAI, Anthropic and
Google over the definition of "frontier model" and the treatment of
open-source. Nothing was published, and for the classified half there is
not even an acknowledgment that it was delivered. Both entries flip to
passed-silent, which in this ledger is the loud outcome. The contrast
arrives tomorrow: the EU AI Act's Code of Practice obligations bind on
Sunday, a hard non-discretionary date that this map was not tracking at
all until an OpenAI compliance post surfaced it — logged now.

Where a deadline did bite, it bit a company. Minnesota's "nudify" ban
took effect this morning and xAI failed to stop it: a federal judge
denied its restraining order on Friday, holding that a firm which waited
nearly three months after the law was signed before suing had not shown
immediate harm. It is the first of the 2026 state AI-harm statutes to
survive a pre-enforcement federal challenge and actually commence, though
the merits fight is still ahead on August 19. That was xAI's second
adverse ruling of the day in a second federal court.

Both wars escalated after Friday morning and neither is on the schedule
the flash rail assumed. Russia struck Kyiv overnight with 35 missiles and
185 drones, killing at least nine — far deadlier than the NATO-airspace
incursion the Russia flash actually describes. Trump ordered strikes on
Iran "as soon as this weekend," which is now, and they have not happened
yet; the IRGC struck two tankers in the Strait of Hormuz, the first time
the blockade has hit vessels actually transiting, and that — not the
43-nation Saudi coalition — is what moved oil on Friday. Both flash
entries expire within three days while both conflicts are still
intensifying.

Markets are closed today. Friday's close, recovered this morning from a
digest that had missed it entirely, showed July ending with the AI
complex carrying nearly all the month's damage: the Nasdaq down 3.2%
against a flat S&P 500.

And the largest event of the day was one this map had entirely missed
until it was specifically asked to look. Roughly 50,000 people crossed
from Morocco into Spain's Ceuta enclave over 07-30 and 07-31 — the
biggest such episode since 2021. Reported deaths rose across the window
from at least 18 to 57, Spain deployed its armed forces, and seven EU
states reimposed or threatened Schengen border controls. It reached this
record only as an unlabelled statistical cluster nobody had examined. The
reason it could not surface on its own turned out to be a single missing
entry in a country-code table: the pipeline's matcher cannot connect a
country it has no name for, so both this crisis and the Gaza war — the
two largest unmatched signals of the day — were structurally invisible to
it. Gaza is now tracked as its own thread, opened on Ben's steer, and the
matcher gap has been sent to the engine's maintainers.
