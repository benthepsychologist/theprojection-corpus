## Power & lobbying

- **An executive order put the grid's own hardware under emergency trade
  authority, and it reaches every datacenter build this map tracks.**
  Executive Order 14421, signed 08-26 and published in today's Federal
  Register, declares foreign supply of bulk-power system electric
  equipment an "unusual and extraordinary threat" under IEEPA and the
  National Emergencies Act. **The capital-relevant part is the scope**:
  generation turbines, large and backup generators, substation
  transformers, grid-connected inverters, battery energy storage and
  high-voltage circuit breakers — the equipment classes whose multi-year
  lead times are already the buildout's binding constraint. Prohibitions
  require a case-by-case determination by the Energy Secretary, who has
  **120 days (to 2026-12-24)** to publish implementing rules that will
  designate which countries and firms count as "Covered Foreign Entities."
  Sec. 2(b) also permits conditions on equipment **already installed**,
  up to replacement or removal. The Edison Electric Institute said
  utilities are "committed to working with DOE on the implementation," and
  an industry representative told Utility Dive there is "a need for a lot
  more clarity" about what counts as foreign-designed, particularly
  software. ⚠️ **Nothing is banned automatically and no determination has
  been published.**
  ([Federal Register, full text](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/text/2026/08/31/2026-17843.txt),
  [Utility Dive](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/trump-declares-emergency-moves-to-block-some-foreign-made-equipment-from-g/828959/),
  [White House fact sheet](https://www.whitehouse.gov/fact-sheets/2026/08/fact-sheet-president-donald-j-trump-declares-a-national-emergency-to-secure-americas-bulk-power-system/))
