**The afternoon's finding is that a law this map has carried as live for
twenty days was struck down before it ever took effect — and that the
morning run had already sensed something was wrong and pointed at the
wrong document.** France's Conseil constitutionnel voided **Article 1**
of the law protecting minors from social media, the under-15 ban itself,
on **14 August 2026** — four days after this map logged the ban as a
hard-identity-verification regime commencing 1 September. This morning's
expectations ledger flagged the claim as unresolvable by further
searching and called for "a direct read of the enacted text on
legifrance.gouv.fr." **No reading of the enacted text would have found
it**, because the dispositive act was a constitutional-review decision
published on a different site. The lesson generalises past this entry:
when a legal claim is contested, the question is *which body last acted
on this*, not *where is the statute*.

**And the reasoning refuses both sides of the question the map was
asking.** The entry existed to test hard identity verification against
Australia's self-declaration model — a natural experiment in whether real
age-gating changes behaviour or just relocates the workaround. The
Council never reached it. It held the ban an interdiction of general
scope imposed without regard to the individual minor or per-service
risk, then made the point that ends the entry: barring under-15s implies
**every** user must prove their age, and the legislature *"n'a pas prévu
les garanties légales"* defining how. **The ban fell because the
verification it implied was left legally undefined — not because France
picked the wrong method.** The comparison cannot be run; the French leg
no longer exists.
