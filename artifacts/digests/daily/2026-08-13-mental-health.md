## Today's throughline

California's SB 903 — the bill requiring AI mental-health tools to stay
under a licensed professional's supervision — resolved today, and it
resolved cleanly: the Assembly Appropriations Committee released it from

immediately ordered to a third reading, a day ahead of its own fiscal
report-out deadline. It's now queued for a full Assembly floor vote when

- **California's SB 903 cleared the Assembly Appropriations Committee
  today on a unanimous 13-0 "do pass" vote — released from the suspense
  file, not held — and was immediately ordered to a third reading on the
  Assembly floor.** Leginfo's own bill-history log shows the sequence in

## Research & evidence

- **A regional feature on Dartmouth's Therabot research — the only
  generative-AI mental-health chatbot with a published clinical
  trial — surfaced a real update this lens's watchlist has been chasing:
  Vermont's AI-therapy law is confirmed signed, not still pending.**
  Dartmouth psychiatrist Michael Heinz, first author on the Therabot
  trial, told the paper the tool remains research-only seven years in
  and that he doubts it will ever seek FDA approval — "trying to fit a
  square peg into a round hole," since the agency is built to evaluate
  things that hold still, not a model that keeps changing. He
  acknowledged users forming real attachment to the bot during the trial
  and that its underlying models "can be sycophantic ... overly
  agreeable." The reporting confirms Vermont's H.816, signed in June,
  requires a licensed professional to review AI-generated mental-health
  advice before it reaches a patient — resolving this lens's "VT
  confirmation" watch item — and notes New Hampshire's own attempt (Sen.
  Howard Pearl's unlicensed-practice bill) stalled into a study
  committee rather than passing.
  ([Concord Monitor / Valley News](https://www.concordmonitor.com/2026/08/13/dartmouth-ai-therapy-chatbot/))
