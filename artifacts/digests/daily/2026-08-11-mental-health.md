## Policy, regulation & legal

- **A UK government mental-health digital-innovation programme formally
  launched today, backed by up to £50M over five years.** MHDI (Mental
  Health Digital Innovation) — the digital workstream of the
  government's Mental Health Goals programme, funded by the Office for
  Life Sciences and delivered through the Medical Research Council, led
  by the University of Manchester — aims to build shared infrastructure
  (data, digital technology, genomics, clinical trials, lived-experience
  partnerships) so digital MH tools can move from pilot to routine NHS
  care, naming fragmented procurement and unclear reimbursement pathways
  as the actual blockers to adoption. A rare funding-*increase*
  counterpoint to this lens's recent run of US evidence-infrastructure
  funding-cut coverage (NIMH, NICE).
  ([MHDI](https://www.mhdi.uk/))

- **A dedicated check of the legislative record found no delay,
  amendment, or legal challenge to Colorado's HB 26-1195 ahead of its
  effective date tomorrow.** The bill — the country's first law requiring
  a real, licensed therapist to remain in the loop for AI use in
  psychotherapy — was signed June 3 and, per the state's own bill page,
  takes effect August 12 by mechanical operation of law (any bill enacted

  unrelated fight: xAI's federal preliminary-injunction challenge targets
  Colorado's broader AI Act (SB 26-189), not this healthcare-specific
  bill or its companion HB 26-1139 — legal trackers confirm both
  healthcare bills proceed unaffected.
  ([Colorado General Assembly](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/HB26-1195))

## Research & evidence

- **A randomised controlled trial of a guided digital app for young
  adults with a history of adverse childhood experiences found its
  primary well-being outcomes null but its targeted mechanisms moved in
  the right direction.** The FACE app — a 10-week, two-component online
  intervention (self/emotion regulation, then social-skills/social
  information processing) — was tested against care-as-usual in 167
  emerging adults (18-24) recruited from a population-based cohort of
  1,514 eligible people (11% enrollment). Resilience and well-being, the
  study's primary outcomes, showed no group difference (both arms
  improved, ending near normative baseline); secondary, more proximal
  outcomes — self-efficacy for managing emotions, fear of negative
  evaluation, negative problem orientation — favored the app,
  particularly its emotion-regulation component. The authors flag low
  uptake (11% of those eligible enrolled) as the study's central
  practical limitation. Published in *Internet Interventions*, online
  ahead of its September 2026 print issue since 08-03; this pipeline's
  buffer only surfaced it this window.
  ([DOI: 10.1016/j.invent.2026.100987](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.invent.2026.100987))
