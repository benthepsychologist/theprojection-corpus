## Today's throughline

The revision mattered more than the print. August payrolls rose **162,000**
against a consensus near 50,000-55,000, and the two prior months were
revised **up** a combined 55,000 — July flipping outright from a reported
-23,000 to +21,000, which unwinds the weak print that framed the whole
argument. Unemployment held at 4.1%; earnings were in line at 0.3% monthly
and 3.1% annually. Governor Waller had staked out a public case for holding
in September less than twenty-four hours earlier, on the explicit condition
that disinflation continue, and named this print as the data he was waiting
for. It came in hot. September-hike odds retraced from about 54.6% back to
roughly 58-60%, the 2-year yield jumped 8bp through 4.416% — its highest
since January 2025 — the 10-year rose to about 4.77%, and the dollar
recovered off its weekly low. **Equities did not sell off**, which is the
part worth holding onto: the S&P was roughly flat, the Nasdaq slightly
higher. The rate path repriced; the risk trade did not.

Two financings priced the same appetite in opposite directions. SoftBank's
record **¥1tn ($6.3bn) seven-year retail bond came at 4.75% — the top of
its 4.3-4.9% range**, more than double the ~2.3% average for comparable yen
retail paper. Banks had declined the risk and left it to households, and
households charged for it. **ByteDance, meanwhile, upsized a syndicated
loan from $20bn to $29.6bn on more than $30bn of demand — unsecured, no
collateral pledged**, nearly 30 banks, Chinese lenders taking over 60%.
Two of the largest AI-buildout financings of the year, one paying the top
of its range to retail and one oversubscribed without security.

Underneath, a demand-side signal on the long end that has nothing to do
with the Fed: **Norway's $2.3 trillion sovereign wealth fund proposed
cutting its government-bond benchmark weight from 70% to 50%**, which would
take roughly **$80 billion out of its ~$215 billion of US Treasuries** —
its single largest holding.

**The afternoon added a political leg and a breadth signal, and took away
nothing.** Within minutes of a print that argued for tightening, the
President demanded the opposite: at 9:56am ET Trump posted on Truth Social
that the Fed "must get smart - BE PATRIOTS for a change," attaching a trade
threat — "LOWER THE RATE OR I'LL STOP TRADING WITH COUNTRIES WITH WHICH WE
HAVE A DEFICIT." The morning run missed it. The detail worth keeping is
who was *not* attacked: Chair Kevin Warsh is named approvingly as "its
great new leader," a week after telling Jackson Hole the Fed had "work to
do" on inflation — a markedly different register from the treatment Powell
drew, on the same demand. And underneath the flat index, the market sorted
itself: **chip stocks ran hard while the broader Nasdaq sat still, in the
same session that pushed September-hike odds through 60%.** As of an
11:12am ET read Intel was +4%, AMD +3%, Nvidia +2% and the SOXX
semiconductor ETF +3%, against QQQ at +0.02%. Rate-sensitivity logic says
high-multiple chip names should have been hit hardest by a hawkish
surprise. They led instead. The morning's "equities did not sell off" holds,
and now has a sharper edge: this was not broad-market indifference, it was
AI-specific bid.

## Deals & financing
