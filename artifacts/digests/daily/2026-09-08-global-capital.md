## Today's throughline

Two shocks arrived in the same overnight and they are not the same shock.
Houthi drones and ballistic missiles hit Saudi energy infrastructure and
four Saudi cities, wounding 73 — striking the Red Sea coast that was
itself the workaround for a closed Strait of Hormuz — and OPEC+ chose the
same week to leave October output flat rather than lean against it. Brent
traded to about $98.6, measured against Monday's close rather than
Friday's. Separately and for unrelated reasons, the yen ran to a
seven-month high on bets the Bank of Japan hikes, unwinding carry
positions and taking the Nikkei down 1.7%. The US market opened into both
at once and did something quieter than either implies: the S&P and Nasdaq
each off about half a percent, the Dow off 1.17%, the long end unmoved.

What makes the day legible is that the bond market did nothing. A 10-year
at 4.780% against Thursday's 4.784% is not a market repricing an inflation
shock — and an oil-driven inflation scare arriving while the White House
presses for looser policy is precisely the collision the Fed-independence
thread has been waiting for.
