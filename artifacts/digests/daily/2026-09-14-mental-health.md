## Clinical safety & harm

- **NPR ran its own users through ChatGPT's "Trusted Contact" feature — the
  opt-in safety tool OpenAI quietly launched in May to notify a named
  contact when the bot detects serious suicide/self-harm risk — and found
  neither a first-time user nor two existing regular users, including the
  reporter, were ever prompted to set it up.** Asked directly, ChatGPT
  itself first denied having the feature before confirming it, findable
  only by digging into Settings; OpenAI told NPR it has seen "good
  adoption" but declined to give numbers. Suicide-prevention researchers
  were split on the record — one wanted it offered proactively to all
  users before any crisis, another doubted a bot can replicate a
  clinician's judgment about who those contacts should be. This is the
  evidence-gap thesis this thread exists to track, in the most concrete
  form yet: a genuine safety feature whose real-world reach is both
  unmeasured and, by direct test, effectively invisible.
  ([NPR/Houston Public Media](https://www.houstonpublicmedia.org/npr/2026/09/14/nx-s1-5943118/npr-looked-for-chatgpts-new-crisis-feature-its-hard-to-find/))
