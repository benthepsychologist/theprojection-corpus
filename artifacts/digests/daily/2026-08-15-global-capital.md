Nvidia's own OpenAI Ohio data-center guarantee got smaller (from up to
$250B in talk down to under $120B, covering only the first phase of the

## Capital in my markets

- **Bond traders start pricing $70 billion in off-balance-sheet AI
  "shadow credit backstops," and multiple named credit-desk sources
  converge on the same concern Michael Burry raised alone two days
  earlier.** Bloomberg reports the $70B figure spans contingent
  guarantees that let a chipmaker or hyperscaler backstop its own
  customers' financing without booking it as a liability: Nvidia can
  cover up to 25% of its $500B compute-financing platform (per CEO
  Jensen Huang directly), Broadcom is backstopping Anthropic's $35B
  "Big Sky" chip-financing deal, and Meta's earlier "Beignet" ($27B) and
  "Sopaipilla" ($13B) data-center debt deals are cited as the template.
  Moody's warned "multiple such transactions over a short period" pose a
  primary risk to a guarantor's own credit profile; DoubleLine's Mariya
  Entina called the ratings treatment "gaming the system"; CreditSights
  called the structure "pro-cyclical" — costless in the boom, dangerous
  in a downturn.
  ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-15/bond-traders-agonize-over-ai-companies-70-billion-of-shadow-credit-backstops), [Yahoo Finance, mirror](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/bond-traders-agonizing-over-70-190000845.html))
