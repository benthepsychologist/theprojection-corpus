## Today's throughline

Overnight Asia carried forward both of last night's big stories. The
Bank of Japan's 25bp hike to 1.25% (already logged on 09-17's finalized
digest, since the decision itself landed inside that digest-day's
window) weakened the yen further into the morning — continuing to soften
toward ¥157 — while Asian equities extended Wall Street's Thursday rally
rather than selling off on the hike: the Nikkei rose roughly 1.5-2%, the
Hang Seng +0.6%, the Shanghai Composite +1%, with chipmakers leading on
Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang's comments that the company will double the
number of chips it sells next year and that production capacity, not
customer demand, is now the binding constraint. Oil kept falling: Brent
eased a further 2.2% to about $102.57 and WTI 1.9% to about $100.05,
a fourth consecutive down session as Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline
restart plan continues to remove the supply-side premium this map has
tracked since the 09-10 strike. And SoftBank added a fourth financing
lever to the stack this map has been tracking all week (the still-
passed-silent dollar-bond roadshow, the Apollo/Vision-Fund-2 NAV-loan
talks, both logged 09-17): Bloomberg reports SoftBank increased its
Arm-backed margin loan from $20bn to $25bn, renegotiated with creditors
this month, with lenders offering roughly $7bn of demand against a
$3-5bn target — a sign banks remain comfortable taking Arm shares as
collateral at current prices even as the company raises money by every
other available channel simultaneously.
