- **Hezbollah Secretary-General Naim Qassem rejected the Lebanese
  government's Rome-talks negotiations with Israel and the US in a
  Friday evening speech, saying Beirut "is signing Israeli-imposed terms
  with Israeli ink" and calling for the framework — which pairs Lebanese
  army deployment in the south with Hezbollah disarmament — to be
  reversed.** The rejection lands the same day Lebanese and Israeli
  reporting converged on a sharper fact: the IDF has spent recent days
  tightening a siege on roughly 40 Hezbollah fighters trapped in an
  extensive tunnel network beneath the Ali al-Taher ridge near Nabatieh —

  "awaiting a green light from the United States" for a "precise,

  need this achievement and will not back down from it." This is not a
  new front — Israel's first attempt to take Ali al-Taher earlier this
  year cost four IDF soldiers' lives, and the underground complex has
  been described as Hezbollah's south-Lebanon "nerve center" — but the
  siege tightening plus a reported US-approval request together mark the
  most concrete step toward renewed ground combat this thread has logged
  since it opened. Separately, talks are said to be aiming to resume in
  "early September."
