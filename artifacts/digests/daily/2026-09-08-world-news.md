## Today's throughline

Two of the day's front pages lead with a trade war and the third with a
strike. Canada's counter-tariffs on US goods took effect this morning at
15, 25 and 50 per cent, the first Canadian retaliation to reach the border
rather than the podium. The Houthis hit Saudi Aramco facilities and four
Saudi cities overnight, wounding 73 — an escalation that pulls Yemen, the
Red Sea and the Iran war into one file, since the Red Sea route being
struck exists precisely because Hormuz is closed. And Russia ended its
three-day pause on Kyiv hours after the American envoys who prompted it
left the city.

The pattern across all three is the same and worth naming: each is a party
demonstrating that a workaround does not work. Canada is showing that
tariff pressure runs both ways, the Houthis that the western export route
is not a sanctuary, and Moscow that a pause bought by a visit expires with
the visit.

## Trade & sanctions

- **Canada's counter-tariffs on US goods took effect this morning at 15,
  25 or 50 per cent of value for duty, and are the lead story on the BBC.**
  Confirmed against the government's own text: CBSA Customs Notice 26-23,
  dated Ottawa 09-07, implements the United States Surtax Order (2026) as
  a response to US Section 338 tariffs, with each product's rate generally
  mirroring the US rate. It reaches shipments below de minimis thresholds
  including postal and courier imports, and does not stack with the
  existing steel-derivative surtax. ⚠️ Scope is unreconciled — coverage
  gives either 629 items or C$27.6bn against this map's logged ~700
  products and ~C$20bn, and the notice itself states neither.
  ([CBSA](https://www.cbsa-asfc.gc.ca/publications/cn-ad/cn26-23-eng.html),
  [AP](https://apnews.com/article/canada-trump-carney-trade-war-tariffs-4620ffb5ecc029322217207fa6758431),
  [BBC](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c8jdev0422jo))
  <!-- k: t=north-american-trade-policy e=canada axis=policy sev=major -->

- **The UK, France and ten other countries announced sanctions and import
  bans on goods from Israeli settlements in the West Bank.** Foreign
  Secretary Ed Miliband told Parliament Britain would introduce an import
  ban plus a "comprehensive sanctions regime," saying Britain "refuses to
  be bystanders" to what he called ethnic cleansing in parts of the West
  Bank. The US ambassador to Israel criticised the move and two Israeli
  ministers called for retaliation. Practical effect on the roughly £6bn
  UK-Israel trade relationship is expected to be small, since
  settlement-origin goods are hard to distinguish — the significance is the
  twelve-country bloc, not the trade volume.
  ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/8/uk-announces-sanctions-on-illegal-israeli-settlements))
