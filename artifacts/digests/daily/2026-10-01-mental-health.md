## Policy, regulation & legal

- **Connecticut's omnibus AI law began taking effect on 10-01, adding anonymous-reporting protections for employees of frontier AI developers and consent rules for subscription renewals, while its AI-companion duties to detect suicide and self-harm risk start on 2027-01-01.** The Daily Campus reports the companion rules require operators to detect when users are "clearly showing a risk of suicide, self-harm, or imminent physical violence," refer them to resources such as 988, never claim to be human, and offer companions to minors only with parental controls; violations are prosecuted under the state's unfair-trade-practices act. The law is one of more than 90 Connecticut laws taking effect this week.
  ([The Daily Campus](https://dailycampus.com/2026/09/30/new-ai-regulation-law-goes-into-effect-oct-1/))

- **New York's Medicaid cut to autism-therapy (applied behavior analysis) payments for behavior technicians took effect on 10-01, which the Council of Autism Service Providers says leaves providers about half of what the state paid for the same services in early 2025.** The Times Union reports the technician rate falls to about $38 an hour from about $77, the cuts were authorized by the state's 2026-27 budget, and state officials say the change "ensures providers of ABA services are compensated equitably for their training and experience"; the Council's chief executive called it a "fundamental misunderstanding" of technicians' roles.
  ([Times Union](https://www.timesunion.com/health/article/medicaid-autism-therapy-cuts-applied-behavior-22452876.php))
  <!-- k: t=mh-clinical-infra-funding axis=policy -->
- **The bipartisan Congressional Psychedelics Advancing Therapies (PATH) Caucus published a report on 10-01 saying stakeholders see broad consensus that psychedelic-assisted therapy could treat a range of treatment-resistant conditions, and urging coordination among federal agencies, states and tribes.** The report, built on responses to a 2024 request for information, recommends federal guidelines for psychedelic therapy, multiple credentialing pathways for providers, a national adverse-event monitoring system and work on insurance coverage; co-chairs Reps. Jack Bergman and Lou Correa said President Trump's executive order on psychedelic research had pushed colleagues to take the issue seriously.
  ([Marijuana Moment](https://www.marijuanamoment.net/bipartisan-congressional-psychedelics-caucus-lays-out-plan-for-federal-and-state-cooperation-to-increase-therapeutic-access/))
  <!-- k: t=psychedelic-regulatory-sprint axis=policy -->
- **California Governor Gavin Newsom said on 10-01 that the number of people with CARE Court treatment plans or agreements had more than doubled to 1,470 as of June 2026, from 706 in October 2025, still far below the 7,000 to 12,000 his office first projected.** The San Francisco Chronicle reports more than 5,400 petitions have been filed, many rejected for not meeting diagnosis requirements or because the person declined to take part, and that the announcement came with an updated state dashboard of county behavioral-health programs; San Francisco's petitions in the first half of 2026 (40) are on track to beat 2025's total (50) by about 50%. The governor's own release says CARE pathways have reached more than 10,000 people through June 2026, that the 1,470 is 1,414 voluntary agreements plus 56 court-ordered plans, and that nearly 4,900 people who first engaged through CARE were referred to services outside the formal process.
  ([San Francisco Chronicle](https://www.sfchronicle.com/politics/article/care-court-data-22449316.php), [Governor of California](https://www.gov.ca.gov/2026/10/01/care_court_bh_county_profile/))
  <!-- k: axis=policy -->
- **HHS said on 10-01 that the restored 988 LGBTQ+ youth line is "fully aligned" with the administration's executive orders on "gender ideology," without saying whether counselors may use transgender callers' chosen names and pronouns; The Trevor Project, which helped build the service, said its proposal to take part was rejected.** The Advocate reports an HHS spokesperson confirmed the specialized "Press 3" service resumed as Congress directed, but did not answer whether the policies restrict counselors from affirming a young person's gender identity, nor name the organizations running the line or provide training materials. The Trevor Project's communications director said its proposal to join the restored services "was rejected"; Congress required SAMHSA to spend $33.1 million on the restoration, and the program was handling nearly 70,000 contacts a month before it ended on 2025-07-17, per the Government Accountability Office.
  ([The Advocate](https://www.advocate.com/health/mental-health/988-returns-questions-remain))
  <!-- k: axis=policy -->
- **The Trump administration's replacement proposal for the federal mental-health parity rule is now expected in 2027, according to a government legal filing reported by Bloomberg Law on 10-01.** Bloomberg Law's reporter writes that the forthcoming proposal would overhaul how the federal government regulates employers' mental-health coverage, which the 2024 rule had tied to comparative tests under the 2008 parity law, that enforcement of the 2024 rule was paused in May 2025, and that the departments released narrower enforcement-priority guidance last month; the full article is paywalled and only this opening was readable, and the filing itself was not located.
  ([Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/health-law-and-business/expected-timeline-for-mental-health-coverage-rule-slips-to-2027))

- **Four House members from both parties announced the Protecting Kids from Human-Like Chatbots Act, which would bar chatbots from communicating with minors in a human-like way by default, including claiming to be human, simulating emotions or using relationship-building language.** Reps. George Whitesides and Doris Matsui (Democrats, California), Mike Kennedy (Republican, Utah) and Mariannette Miller-Meeks (Republican, Iowa) announced the bill in a press release dated 10-01, which cites Common Sense Media's 2026 census finding that 28% of 13- to 15-year-olds use AI every day; the release does not give a bill number or the introduction date, and the text was not read.
  ([SCVNews, reproducing Whitesides' release](https://scvnews.com/whitesides-introduces-bipartisan-legislation-to-protect-children-from-chatbots/))

- **Two Minnesota state senators, Zaynab Mohamed and Scott Dibble, announced on 10-01 that they will seek laws requiring transparency about who owns autism-therapy centers and giving the state power to weed out providers whose profit-driven models compromise children's care.** The announcement cites The 74's investigation of private equity in applied behavior analysis, which reports that Medicaid payments for the therapy grew from $400 million in 2019 to nearly $2 billion in 2024 and that private-equity firms own almost half of the 50 companies with the highest claims, accounting for $3.1 billion of the almost $7 billion billed over six years; no bill text was reported, and the figures are The 74's own analysis of federal Medicaid records.
  ([The 74](https://www.the74million.org/article/after-74-investigation-minnesota-senators-seek-law-regulating-autism-therapy/))

## 🧪 Clinical trials

ClinicalTrials.gov's own API, queried for studies first posted 2026-10-01 by psychiatric condition and modality (a selection), returned the following on-lens records.

- **Attune Neurosciences registered a 300-participant dose-finding study of low-intensity focused ultrasound, with its ATTN201 device, across seven deep-brain targets in adults with subsyndromal-to-moderate depression, anxiety or obsessive-compulsive disorder (first posted 10-01; start planned 2026-10-05).** The double-blind, within-participant crossover design gives every session active stimulation, with no sham or placebo arm; the primary outcome is a patient-specific transdiagnostic score one week after stimulation. This is the sponsor's own registration.
  ([ClinicalTrials.gov NCT07851038](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07851038))

- **The University of Pittsburgh registered a 57-participant randomized, single-masked study of transcranial focused ultrasound aimed at the ventral striatum in adults with cocaine use disorder, measuring brain activity to cocaine cues before and after stimulation (first posted 10-01; start planned 2026-12-15).**
  ([ClinicalTrials.gov NCT07852923](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07852923))
  <!-- k: t=neuromodulation-evidence axis=clinical-trials -->
- **Egypt's Kafrelsheikh University registered a 182-patient randomized non-inferiority trial comparing 3-minute intermittent theta-burst stimulation with standard 37-minute 10 Hz repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation for treatment-resistant depression (first posted 10-01; start was 2026-06-30).** Participants receive 20 sessions over four weeks, with change on the 17-item Hamilton scale at week 4 as the primary outcome.
  ([ClinicalTrials.gov NCT07852065](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07852065))
  <!-- k: t=neuromodulation-evidence axis=clinical-trials -->
- **The University of British Columbia registered a three-participant open-label pilot of a single 25 mg psilocybin dose for nicotine craving, with repeated brain imaging (first posted 10-01; start planned 2027-01).** It follows participants from the group's earlier SCANS imaging study, and the registrant describes it as informing larger future studies.
  ([ClinicalTrials.gov NCT07852832](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07852832))
