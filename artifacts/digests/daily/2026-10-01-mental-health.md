- **Four House members from both parties introduced the Protecting Kids from Human-Like Chatbots Act (H.R. 10719) on 10-01, which would make a non-personified setting the default for any user a chatbot knows or has reason to know is a minor, barring claims to be human, simulated emotions and relationship-building language.** Reps. George Whitesides and Doris Matsui (Democrats, California), Mike Kennedy (Republican, Utah) and Mariannette Miller-Meeks (Republican, Iowa) introduced it; the Congressional Record lists it as H.R. 10719, referred to the Energy and Commerce Committee and, in addition, Science, Space, and Technology. The text Whitesides' office posted (a draft stamped 11:18 a.m. on 10-01, bill number still blank) makes it unlawful for a developer to offer a chatbot likely to be used by minors without the setting, lets only a parent or guardian turn it off (educators in school-managed deployments), bars nudging a minor to disable it, is enforced by the Federal Trade Commission and state attorneys general, preempts state law only where it conflicts, and takes effect one year after enactment; the member release cites Common Sense Media's 2026 census finding that 28% of 13- to 15-year-olds use AI every day. The draft's wording may differ from the introduced text.
