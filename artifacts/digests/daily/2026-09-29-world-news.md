## Russia / Ukraine & Europe

- **Estonia's foreign minister said "with certainty" that an August arson attack on the defence company Milrem Robotics in Tallinn was sabotage ordered by Russia's special services, and summoned the Russian ambassador; the Kremlin called the accusation "baseless."** Margus Tsahkna said the perpetrators had been apprehended and "no longer pose a threat," and called the attack part of a broader Russian campaign across Europe. NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte said Russia "continues its reckless campaign of hostile actions against NATO allies," and France, Lithuania, Latvia and Sweden condemned the attack.

## Iran

- **Iranians are stockpiling food and medicine as fears grow of new fighting with the US and of shortages under a tightening US naval blockade and embargo, Al Jazeera reported from Tehran on Tuesday.** Shops remain stocked, but social-media posts urge people to hold several days of supplies; the US has also announced a worldwide ban on Iranian airlines and is pressing other countries to cut trade with Iran. Trump rejected Iran's seven-day truce offer on Saturday, and the war is in its eighth month.
  ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/9/29/iranians-stock-up-on-food-and-medicine-as-fears-of-new-us-fighting-grow))

## Yemen

- **Yemen's UN envoy, Hans Grundberg, said Tuesday he met the Houthis' chief negotiator in Muscat over the weekend to try to head off further escalation, calling the Houthis' attacks on Saudi Arabia "unprecedented" and "dramatic"; the UN counts at least 838 killed and more than 3,640 wounded in the offensive.** He also held talks in Riyadh with the government and regional powers. The UN migration agency counts more than 145,000 people displaced inside Yemen and over 3,000 who fled to Djibouti; Grundberg said the war has become entangled with the US-Israeli war on Iran and urged that the conflicts be "disentangled" and addressed one by one.
  ([Naharnet, AP](https://www.naharnet.com/stories/322809-un-envoy-meets-houthi-negotiators-in-a-bid-to-halt-escalating-violence-in-yemen))

- **The US military's Africa Command called the growing ties between the Houthis and Somalia's al-Shabaab a "highly concerning, escalatory threat to regional stability," the Financial Times reported.** Security experts told the paper the Houthis have sold weapons to al-Shabaab and trained hundreds of its fighters in drone and explosive use; the Houthis seized the port of Mokha this month and advanced on the Bab al-Mandab strait, through which about a tenth of global trade passes.
  ([Financial Times, via Horseed Media](https://horseedmedia.net/us-warns-houthi-ties-with-al-shabaab-threaten-red-sea-trade/426023/))

## Horn of Africa

- **The governor of Ethiopia's Afar region told Al Jazeera on Tuesday that Eritrean forces are taking part in the fighting, control more than two-thirds of Afar and are moving to cut the road linking Djibouti and Eritrea; Eritrea has not responded, and Ethiopia's army says it has killed about 270 Tigrayan fighters and wounded more than 200 in North Wollo.** The governor's account is a claim by one side that Al Jazeera could not independently verify, and Eritrea and Sudan have denied the army chief's separate accusations of backing the rebel coalition; Egypt said its policy is based on "full respect for the sovereignty, unity and territorial integrity of states." The Afar push threatens the Djibouti corridor that carries most of Ethiopia's imports, including fuel.
  ([Al Jazeera, AP/Reuters](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/29/fighting-in-ethiopia-intensifies-whats-the-latest))
