## Today's throughline

Gulf equities gave the first market read on the Houthis' claim to have
struck Saudi Aramco's Yanbu export hub, and the read was mild: Saudi
Arabia's index opened 0.5% lower and closed down 0.3% at 10,750, while
Aramco's own stock reversed an early dip to finish 1.3% higher. Treasury
Secretary Scott Bessent and US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer were
due to meet China's Vice Premier He Lifeng at JPMorgan's Manhattan
headquarters from about 10:30am ET, on a day-long agenda covering the
tariff truce that expires 11-10, rare-earth export flows and AI
guardrails, four days before Trump hosts Xi in Washington.

## Markets

- **Saudi Arabia's Tadawul index closed 0.3% lower at 10,750 on the first
  trading day after the Houthis claimed strikes on Riyadh and on Aramco's
  Yanbu facilities, having opened down 0.5% — and Saudi Aramco's own
  shares reversed an early 0.6% fall to close 1.3% higher.** The steepest
  faller was ACWA Power, down 2.8%. Elsewhere in the Gulf, Qatar's index
  closed down 1.1% at 9,550 with Industries Qatar off 4.1%, Egypt fell
  0.2% to 55,371, Bahrain 0.2% to 1,920, Oman 0.3% to 7,580 and Kuwait
  0.7% to 9,182. The detail that carries the information is Aramco
  closing *up* on a day its own export infrastructure was publicly
  claimed as a target: the equity market nearest to the event, and the
  best-informed about it, priced the claim as largely immaterial. That is
  a data point on the claim's credibility, not proof either way, and CME
  crude has not reopened to offer a second opinion.
