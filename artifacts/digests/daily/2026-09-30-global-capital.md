- **Core PCE inflation cooled to 3.0% in August, below the 3.3% forecast, and traders cut the odds of an October Fed hike to about 35%.** The Commerce Department's August PCE price index rose 3.4% from a year earlier against a forecast of 3.7%, with headline up 0.3% on the month and core up 0.2% (forecast 0.3%); spending rose 0.9% against 0.8% expected and income 0.2% against 0.4%. The Bureau of Economic Analysis changed how it measures prices for legal services, software and computer accessories, and portfolio management, and CNBC said it was unclear how much that affected the readings. TradeStation's David Russell called it good news for investors worried about the bond surge but noted the data does not reflect September's surge in diesel prices; Navy Federal's Heather Long said the data show no progress on inflation and that September will be higher. Gasoline rose 4.4% in August. The CME FedWatch tool put a quarter-point hike in October at about 35%, down from about 51% a day earlier, with another hike still expected in December.

- **The Bank of England's Financial Policy Committee warned that AI-related borrowing, about $450 billion so far this year, and sovereign debt could run into trouble together, with government bond yields at levels not seen since 2008.** Its record said the Middle East conflict is "re-intensifying the risk that vulnerabilities in sovereign debt markets, risky asset valuations and risky credit markets crystalise at the same time," that private credit "remains vulnerable to a tightening in financial conditions," and that "the risk of a sharper correction persists" if expectations for AI earnings are shaken. It cited JPMorgan estimates that debt-financed AI capital spending could reach about $4.1 trillion in 2026 to 2030, Morgan Stanley's estimate that about $700 billion of data-center spending in 2026 to 2028 will be private-credit financed, and "circular arrangements" in AI financing. Governor Andrew Bailey wrote separately that regulation "is not, in my view, the right place to start."
