## Today's throughline

**This lens has been covering the Federal Reserve for two weeks without
knowing who runs it.** The 07-29 decision is recorded here in detail — held
at 3.50-3.75% on a 9-3 vote, all three dissenters named, their subsequent
speeches tracked — and the coverage critic found that the words "Fed chair"
attach to no name anywhere in this map. A benchmark ran two consecutive
issues about the chair's positioning while this record had no idea the
office had changed hands.

It has. **Kevin Warsh has chaired the Federal Reserve since 2026-05-22**,
confirmed 54-45 on 05-13 — the narrowest confirmation the position has ever
had — after a nomination in January and a hearing delayed by a blockade
tied to a federal investigation into his predecessor. Jerome Powell's
chairmanship ended after eight years; he did not leave, and still sits as a
governor. None of this was in the record.

The 9-3 hold reads differently once you know the chair is a hawk who spent
2008-2011 opposing quantitative easing and took office promising to be
"strictly independent" of the president who appointed him. Three dissents
for a *hike* under a hawkish chair who voted to hold is a different object
than three dissents under a dove. The vote counts we recorded were right;
the thing they were a fact *about* was missing.

## Power & lobbying

- **Kevin Warsh has been Fed chair since 2026-05-22**, for a four-year
  term ending 2030-05-21. Nominated 2026-01-30, Senate Banking hearing
  04-21 (delayed by a Tillis blockade tied to an investigation into
  Powell), confirmed **54-45** on 05-13 with Fetterman the lone crossover,
  sworn in 05-22 and selected FOMC chairman unanimously. A Fed governor
  from 2006-2011 and one of the "Four Musketeers" through the 2008 crisis,
  he opposed Bernanke's QE on hawkish grounds while voting for it, and told
  his confirmation hearing he would be a "strictly independent" chair who
  would not cut rates on the president's behalf.
  ([Federal Reserve, primary](https://www.federalreserve.gov/aboutthefed/bios/board/warsh.htm))

  and 106 days as chair — a detail that matters for reading FOMC
  composition, since a former chair sitting as a voting governor is an
  unusual configuration.
  ([Federal Reserve, primary](https://www.federalreserve.gov/aboutthefed/bios/board/default.htm))
