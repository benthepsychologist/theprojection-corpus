## The week's throughline

The week opened and closed on the same unresolved bet — an Iran/Hormuz
de-escalation nobody has actually signed — and in between, the AI-capex
financing story that's dominated this map since July found its two
sharpest single-name tests yet. Monday's broad risk-on rally was bought on
an Iran supply-relief claim Tehran itself denied within hours; by Sunday
morning, Iran's Revolutionary Guard had hardened its position further,
explicitly separating the Hormuz-reopening question from the Oman talks
and listing six conditions the US would have to meet first — the same
"agreed in principle" story that spent all week drifting between "final
drafting" (Thursday) and "getting close" (Friday) without ever
closing. Inside that frame, SpaceX and AMD's Thursday-night earnings set
the week's real pattern — both beat cleanly and both sold off, because
investors are now pricing AI capex as a cost rather than rewarding the
beat around it — and SoftBank's Wednesday print turned that pattern into a
hard, quantified number: Arm's post-earnings slide showed up directly on
SoftBank's balance sheet as a real ¥14 trillion (~19%) NAV cut, even as
the previously-stalled $10B OpenAI-collateral margin loan finally got
signed. Berkshire's Saturday earnings ran the same tension in reverse —
Greg Abel ended a 14-quarter net-selling streak to put $10B into an
explicitly AI-infrastructure-tied Alphabet stake, in the same week
Alphabet itself went to raise up to $25B more in debt to fund the
buildout that stake is betting on. And underneath all of it, Friday's job
report landed as the week's actual capstone: nonfarm payrolls fell
23,000 — an outright decline, not a soft beat, against a market expecting
roughly +84,000 — with a combined 103,000-job downward revision to May
and June on top of it, hitting a Fed committee that had just gone from a
unanimous hold in June to a 9-3 vote with three hawkish dissents in July.
Trump used the same week to revive his effort to remove Fed Governor Lisa
Cook on a hard three-week deadline — a fight over the Fed's *composition*,
not just Kevin Warsh's messaging regime, that this map had never tracked
before Friday. Sovereign and state capital kept concentrating in parallel:
Saudi PIF's $55B Electronic Arts buyout closed at a genuinely concentrated
93.4% direct ownership (Jared Kushner's Affinity Partners a small
co-investor), and the federal government's chipmaker-equity portfolio grew
to 30 companies worth roughly $26.7B on paper. One structural finding
about the map's own process, not the market: **Axios Pro Rata and FT
Unhedged were effectively unreachable on every day this week** this
digest could check — a genuine, recurring access gap in how this lens
benchmarks its own coverage, detailed in the near-miss audit below.

## By radar question

### Q7 — Where is capital and economic power concentrating — in my markets and above them?

**What moved, in order.** The vendor-financing structure this map has
tracked since late July got its sharpest quantified test yet: SoftBank's
Wednesday (08-06) Q1 print showed Arm's own post-earnings slide translating
directly into a same-release pro-forma NAV cut from a record ¥72.3T to
¥58.3T — a ¥14 trillion, ~19% mark-down, driven specifically by Arm's
contribution falling from ¥43.4T to ¥31.1T — while the separate,
previously-stalled $10B OpenAI-collateral margin loan (halted since
06-10) finally got signed the same week (Goldman/JPMorgan/Mizuho/Apollo/
SMBC), pushing SoftBank's total OpenAI-linked commitments past $60B.
That's the clearest single number this map has produced yet for "what
does the AI-financing risk actually cost when it lands," and it landed
the same week Thursday night's SpaceX and AMD earnings showed the market
pricing the mechanism in real time — both companies beat cleanly and both
sold off on the size of their AI capex, not the quality of their
results. Berkshire's Saturday print ran the identical tension from the
buy side: Greg Abel's first net-equity-buying quarter in 14 put $10B into
an Alphabet stake explicitly framed as an AI-infrastructure bet, landing
in the same week Alphabet itself raised up to $25B in fresh debt (its
third bond sale of 2026, ~$70B raised or in progress against ~$85B of
equity issuance already this year) — one of the best-capitalized
companies in the cohort choosing debt over its own enormous cash
generation to fund the same buildout Berkshire just bought equity in.
**The week's genuinely new axis was political, not financial**: Friday's
jobs report (payrolls -23,000, a 103,000-job combined downward revision,
against a committee that just split 9-3 hawkish) is the strongest data
point available to push back on July's hike-dissent bloc before the
09-16 meeting, and Trump used the same week to revive his effort to
remove Fed Governor Lisa Cook — a hard three-week deadline (08-26),
rebuilt to survive the Supreme Court's due-process ruling that blocked
his first attempt. That's pressure on the Fed's *composition*, a second
and distinct lever from Warsh's own low-guidance regime change, and
neither Cook nor a "Fed independence" frame has ever been tracked here
despite two straight weeks of detailed FOMC vote coverage — offered as a
thread candidate 08-07, still unpromoted. On the wealth-and-power side:
Saudi PIF's $55B Electronic Arts buyout closed 08-04 at a sharper detail
than the original announcement carried — PIF alone holds 93.4% direct
ownership, not a diversified three-way split, with Kushner's Affinity
Partners a small but direct 1.1% co-investor — and the federal
government's own chipmaker-equity program added six more firms
(GlobalFoundries plus five earlier-stage names), taking its total
position to 30 companies and roughly $26.7B on paper, still headlined by
the Intel stake this map has tracked since late July.

**Working-note candidate, updated:** the 08-04 note (hyperscaler
guarantees as the financing mechanism to watch, alongside the
guarantee-count and CDS/loan-spread leading indicators) holds and gets a
concrete extension this week — the guarantee/off-balance-sheet structure
is now joined by a straight debt-vs-equity split visible on a single
balance sheet (Alphabet borrowing while Berkshire buys the equity), and
the vendor-financing risk finally produced a real, dated, quantified loss
number (SoftBank's ¥14T NAV cut) rather than a spread or a CDS move. Track
next: whether other "smart money, discipline-branded" allocators follow
Berkshire's Alphabet bet (the Apple-2016 precedent this map's own
interpretation layer flagged), and whether the Fed-composition fight
(Cook's 08-26 deadline) becomes the dominant Q7 story going into
09-16 — it is currently a bigger open question than anything purely
financial on this map.

### Q2 — Where is the money going?

This week filled in the destination layer at the level of individual
balance sheets rather than a sector-wide claim. Alphabet's up-to-$25B bond
raise and Berkshire's $10B AI-linked equity stake in the same company, the
same week, is the cleanest single illustration yet of the two channels
this lens has been tracking separately since late July (debt-funded capex
vs. equity conviction) converging on one name. SoftBank's signed $10B
OpenAI-collateral loan is the same story from the lender side — real money
now actually moving, not a guarantee sitting contingent on a balance
sheet. And the federal chipmaker-equity program's growth to 30 companies
(~$26.7B) confirms last week's finding that state capital is accumulating
stakes with no centralized oversight layer, now at meaningfully larger
scale. No new destination category opened this week; the existing ones
(hyperscaler capex, vendor financing, sovereign/state equity) all got
harder numbers attached.
