# Global Capital — week of 2026-08-17

*Synthesized from 7 dailies (2026-08-17 through 2026-08-23) + a fresh

## The week's throughline

This was the week the long end of the US Treasury curve became this
lens's actual lead story, and the week this lens discovered it had no
place to put that story. The 30-year yield hit a nineteen-year high
(5.31–5.34%) Monday and Tuesday on record global bond issuance rather
than any Gulf escalation; Treasury Secretary Bessent doubled long-bond
buyback size Wednesday, which reversed the move for about a day before
it fully round-tripped by Friday; by Sunday, Deutsche Bank's George
Saravelos had named what was actually happening — "soft-form financial
repression," with both the buyback and a parallel US-Japan yen operation
structured specifically to avoid adding Treasury supply (funded by
selling euros and by Japan borrowing dollars through the Fed's FIMA
repo facility rather than selling its own Treasury holdings). The same
mechanism went global mid-week: Japan's 10-year JGB pushed toward its
highest yield since 1996 and took the Nikkei, SoftBank, Kioxia and Arm
down together, with a Daiwa strategist naming the identical
rate-into-AI-equities transmission this map had only tracked
domestically until then. Markets read the intervention as a currency
story rather than a rates story — gold hit a three-month high and
bitcoin rose 7–9% in a single session as the dollar slid. This lens's
own coverage-critic pass confirmed FT Unhedged, its single most
important benchmark, led with this exact story three consecutive
editions (08-20, 08-21, 08-22) while this map carried the substance as
loose digest bullets with nowhere to accumulate it — `fed-independence-fight`
covers the Fed's *composition* (the Lisa Cook removal fight), not the
Treasury's own balance-sheet mechanics. The candidate was offered four
times across the week and answered only today (2026-08-25, after this
week closed) when Ben opened `treasury-long-end-intervention`.

Underneath the rate story, the vendor-financing/circular-financing
structure this lens has tracked all summer kept compounding rather than
resolving: Nvidia's Ohio guarantee to OpenAI got an actual number from
its own 8-K ($105bn, capped, a residual-value guarantee OpenAI itself
indemnifies); Broadcom began assembling $70–80bn of SPV debt to build
the chips Anthropic's own $2 trillion IPO valuation depends on — the
same company being valued and financed on the same future revenue on
the same day (08-21); SoftBank's 13F showed the identical
mark-only-growth pattern this map found in Nvidia's Intel stake the
week before (67% of its US book in one Intel position, no new shares
bought, value up on price alone); and Alibaba raised $10.2bn in Hong
Kong's largest-ever follow-on placement, putting 100% of it into AI
while posting a 75% profit decline and a $6.6bn cash outflow — the
public-market, openly-diluted version of the same loop this map tracks
structured away in the US. The week closed with Nvidia raising AI
server prices more than 15% for 2027 delivery, handing pricing power to
three memory makers (Samsung, SK Hynix, Micron) this map still holds
only as unslugged search terms, and repricing the ~$610–650bn of
tracked hyperscaler capex against a cost base nobody planned around.

### Q7 — Where is capital and economic power concentrating — in my markets and above them?

**What moved.** The week supplied Q7's "macro backdrop" sub-scope (rates/
inflation/FX) with its first genuinely dominant story, and exposed that
this lens had never built a node for it. The sequence — a 19-year yield
high on debt-supply grounds, a policy intervention that worked for about
a day, a full round-trip, and finally a named transmission mechanism
(financial repression, pressure resurfacing in the dollar) — is a
complete arc that happened entirely inside one week, verified at primary
source (Treasury's own announcement, Fed data, Deutsche Bank's own
research note) rather than reconstructed from rumor. It is also the
clearest evidence yet that Q7's three sub-scopes are not equally
instrumented: "capital in my markets" (vendor financing, IPO chatter,
13Fs) has threads for everything it produces; "macro backdrop" had none
until today. On the "wealth & power" sub-scope, the week's sharpest data
point was structural rather than a single filing: SoftBank's 13F
replicating Nvidia's own mark-only-growth pattern on the same underlying
asset (Intel) is now a second independent sighting of the same
mechanism — a stake ladder's headline growth being priced appreciation,
not fresh buying — at two different institutions in eight days. Also
notable: this week is the first time the memory makers (Samsung, SK
Hynix, Micron) functioned as the *subject* of a capital story (Nvidia's
price hike) rather than incidental context, and this map still cannot
tag any of the three as an entity.

### Q2 — Where is the money going? (money lens extending)

**What moved.** Two data points this week sharpen Q2's "destination"
question rather than just restating Q7's credit-risk framing of the same
mechanism. First, Broadcom's $70–80bn SPV for Anthropic's chip capacity
is a new instrument shape at the "compute-financing platform" layer —
larger and more structured (a senior/junior tranche split, Apollo and
Blackstone as junior-capital providers) than anything this map logged at
that layer before this week. Second, Alibaba's $10.2bn Hong Kong
placement is the cleanest public-market illustration yet of the
debt/equity-funded-AI-buildout loop this map has mostly tracked as a US
phenomenon: a company burning cash on AI (a 75% profit decline, a $6.6bn
free-cash outflow, both attributed to AI project and compute costs)
funding more AI by selling discounted equity in the open market rather
than through a structured guarantee — the dilution taken openly instead
of engineered away. Money is visibly still landing at every layer this
note has tracked since 08-10 (compute-financing platforms, chipmaker
equity, lab valuations, landlord real estate) — this week's addition is
that debt is now visibly the connective tissue even in a market (Hong
Kong) that isn't the primary one this map watches.
