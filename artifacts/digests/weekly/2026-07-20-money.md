## The week's throughline

The week circular financing stopped being a thesis and showed up on the
tape. It escalated in four steps: a survey (BofA, 07-23: 48% of managers
call hyperscaler capex the top systemic risk), a live deal wobbling
(BlackRock's $12.3B Meta data-center bond sale met soft demand, 07-24), a
second chipmaker adopting the pattern (AMD investing up to $5B in Anthropic
while selling it "tens of billions" in servers, 07-22), and then the
capstone on Sunday night: **Nvidia in talks to guarantee $250B of financing
for OpenAI's 10GW Ohio buildout** — the vendor backstopping its biggest
customer's debt. Chanos: "Nvidia is effectively financing its own chip
sales." Meanwhile the DRAM squeeze got its capital-markets marker (CXMT's
466% STAR debut, Monday, briefly past Intel's market cap) and a Red
Sea/Hormuz oil shock (Brent > $100, then $97) ran straight into a hawkish
FOMC setup (Sept-hike odds 82–93%) under a Fed chair — Warsh — the macro
strip had never named.

## By radar question

### Q7 — Where is capital and economic power concentrating?

**What moved:** the vendor-financing loop tightened at every scale. Nvidia:
$250B OpenAI financing guarantee in talks (07-26, WSJ), a confirmed 9.3%
Nebius stake (07-20), $1B into Naver (07-27) — equity and credit flowing
*from the chip monopolist to its customers*. AMD copied the structure with
Anthropic (07-22). BlackRock's GIP closed the $40B Aligned Data Centers
acquisition (07-21, with MGX/AIP) — the largest single asset-manager
position in the buildout — while its Meta bond sale drew soft demand
(07-24), the first live-deal crack after Wednesday's Mag7 −$797B day. A BIS
"worse than 2008" warning circulated (07-22) but is thin — single
aggregator, unconfirmed against any primary BIS document; treat as
unverified until sourced. **Implication:** the buildout's capital structure
is now self-referential at the top (chip vendor ↔ model lab ↔ cloud), and
the first external check — the credit market — has started to blink.
**Working-note candidate:** track deal-level debt demand (cover ratios,
spreads) as the leading indicator; surveys lag, guarantees conceal.
