## The week's throughline

Seven days in, every one of the map's six standing conflict threads moved
on real, dated events, and three of them — Israel-Lebanon, Iran's
widening war, and Russia-Ukraine — hardened week over week into their
most consequential shape yet, without any of them resolving. Iran's
Hormuz standoff spent six straight days collapsing from negotiation into
open contradiction and then into grudging partial movement: Trump's
Monday compensation precondition (stacked on Iran's own reparations
demand) gave way to a Tuesday "50 years" escalation an outside analyst
dismissed as "banter," then to Wednesday's flat contradiction between
Trump's claimed "100%" US Navy control and Iran's own Strait Authority
insisting the waterway "remains blocked" — backed by shipping data
showing traffic near a three-month low. The `iran-oman-hormuz-deal-signing`
ledger expectation formally slipped Wednesday with no signature ever
appearing, re-set to 08-19 at low confidence. By Friday the fight had
widened on three fronts at once — the UAE blamed Iran's Revolutionary
Guard for a drone attack on two tankers ("acts of piracy"), Canada and
nine European countries added fresh sanctions, and Trump floated
declaring the strait "a territory of the United States" — and by
Saturday a third ADNOC tanker had been hit even as Iran and Oman
announced real, if partial, progress: a shipping route-map agreement
that Iran's own foreign ministry was explicit is "not negotiation" and
not the full interim deal this map is tracking. Underneath the
diplomacy, the blockade turned lethal for the first time since
February — a Houthi double-tap strike killed six aboard a cargo ship
Tuesday, and Houthi missiles killed civilians and marines at Yemen's
port of Mokha Saturday — and Iran's fight quietly widened into Iraq,
where its Quds Force commander lobbied against Baghdad's
militia-disarmament deadline. Then, for the first time all week, the
thread went quiet: Sunday's sweep found no new dated development on
Hormuz, Iraq, or the broader Iran front at all — the only day of the
seven this thread didn't move.

Israel-Lebanon supplied the week's sharpest single escalation and kept
sharpening every day it moved, including the last. What opened the week
as "talks unlikely to resume before end of summer" and a UNIFIL
peacekeeping phase-out moved by midweek to Israeli forces besieging
roughly 40 Hezbollah fighters trapped in a tunnel network beneath the Ali
al-Taher ridge, reportedly awaiting only a US "green light" to seize the
position by force, and reached the deadliest single day of Israeli
strikes in Lebanon since the ceasefire on Saturday: at least 11 dead
across two villages, after a Hezbollah drone attack wounded three IDF
soldiers and Israel struck what it called a Hezbollah command post,
hitting a family in the process. Hezbollah's leader had already rejected
the Rome-talks framework outright the day before, and an anonymous US
State Department official broke ranks mid-week to call Israeli Defense
Minister Katz's "here to stay" stance on south Lebanon "not consistent"
with the agreed framework — the first daylight gap this thread has
recorded between Washington and Israel's own defense ministry on this
specific point, not just Lebanese criticism. Sunday closed the loop on
Saturday's strikes rather than opening a new front: Israel named Abu
Hassan Alaa, a commander in Hezbollah's Badr unit, among the dead —
retroactively framing Saturday as a targeted decapitation strike rather
than the mis-strike Israel had first described — while Defense Minister
Katz vowed "no score will be left unsettled in any arena." Even with that
hardening, the diplomatic track held: a Lebanese official told Qatar's
news agency an eighth round of Rome talks is provisionally set for
September 1, a new dated expectation this map has since logged to the
ledger (see Expectations scorecard below).

Gaza saw operations resume after a seven-day pause (two, then three,
targeted strikes across the week) inside the same disarmament-sequencing
dispute — Hamas accepting "decommissioning" in principle against
Netanyahu's insistence on full disarmament first — closed midweek with
Jared Kushner, Nickolay Mladenov and Tony Blair announcing a trip to
Israel and Egypt specifically because Netanyahu rejected Trump's own
15-point plan, and by Saturday produced the ceasefire's first documented
armed exchange inside the Yellow Line (a Hamas-planted bomb under an IDF
tank; two Palestinians shot dead crossing the line). A West Bank
side-story (the Qusra settler siege, joined by a second site at Umm
al-Khair) drew a US ambassador's "act of terror" charge, condemnation
from inside the settler movement itself, and Katz moving enforcement
power over settlers from the army to the police — critics call it a step
toward de facto annexation. Sunday produced the week's sharpest
diplomatic rebuke of the process yet: eight Arab and Muslim-majority
states — Turkey, Egypt, Indonesia, Jordan, Pakistan, Qatar, Saudi Arabia
and the UAE — jointly blamed Israel for wrecking the peace track, on the
same day Israeli strikes on Islamic Jihad and Hamas targets in Khan
Younis and Nuseirat killed two more and a Hamas delegation led by Khalil
al-Hayya landed in Cairo ahead of Kushner's own meetings there —
condemnation, strikes and shuttle diplomacy inside a single day.

Russia-Ukraine produced two of the week's largest claims back to back
before closing with its heaviest exchange yet. Kyiv declared an
eight-month counteroffensive complete Friday, retaking 745 km² (Ukraine's
figure; the Institute for the Study of War's independent count is a more
conservative 627 km²) and stripping Russia of nearly all its
Dnipropetrovsk gains, then followed it Saturday with the deepest verified
strike this thread has logged — a Flamingo cruise-missile hit on the
Savasleyka air base 650-700km inside Russia (home to Kinzhal-carrying
MiG-31Ks), paired with a strike on the Progress Rocket Space Center in
Samara. Sunday topped even that for sheer scale: roughly 600 Ukrainian
drones hit Moscow Oblast, disabling seven of e-commerce giant
Wildberries' ten largest logistics hubs and killing one person, while
Russia's retaliatory barrage killed at least seven and burned Kyiv's
historic book market — a NATO-adjacent footnote attached itself the same
night, a Spanish F-18 flying NATO air-policing duty shooting down a
stray Russian drone over Romania, the fourth such shootdown of this war —
and a Ukrainian strike on Russia's Belgorod region, landing just inside
the digest-day's 05:00 ET boundary, killed six people including a
14-year-old. Read against a sustained week of barrages on both sides
(Zaporizhzhia/Kyiv's 12 dead Monday, an Odesa train crew killed
Wednesday, Saturday's retaliatory strike killing a three-month-old
infant), both militaries' strain stayed visible all week rather than
pointing to any lull, and Sunday's exchange was, in raw drone-count
terms, the largest of the seven days.

Horn of Africa stayed genuinely quiet the entire week — no new dated
development on the Tigray front since before this week began. Europe's
Schengen thread stayed quiet through Friday and then moved on the week's
final day: Morocco broke up a second attempted mass crossing into Ceuta
on Saturday, detaining roughly 294 people after social-media calls for a
repeat of the late-July crisis. That same Sunday report surfaced a live,
unresolved sourcing discrepancy this map is carrying openly rather than
papering over: this thread's own casualty figure from the original July
crisis is 72 dead, against NPR's 08-16 reporting of "more than 140" dead
and "over 70,000" crossings in the week of July 27 — a materially
different picture that no outlet checked has resolved either way.

Two events outside this lens's six tracked threads registered at scale
this week and are flagged rather than folded silently in: a
magnitude-7.4 earthquake in western Colombia (Monday, the country's
strongest this century) climbed from 224 to 281 confirmed dead by
Thursday with search operations entering a declared "final phase," and
the DRC's Ebola outbreak — first surfaced by this lens's own sweep on
08-12 — passed 2,000 deaths, the fastest-accelerating outbreak on
record. Both were offered once as thread candidates (08-10/08-12) and
not re-offered per this lens's own "once, then drop" convention. A
third, genuinely new disaster signal arrived Saturday: a magnitude-7.7
earthquake off Indonesia's Flores Island killed at least 47 and
triggered a since-lifted tsunami warning — offered once as a new
candidate on 08-15, then re-offered on 08-16 (an exception to "once,
then drop," made explicitly because the toll cleared the re-offer bar
that digest itself set: a materially revised death count, 47 up to 68).
None of the three cleared the flash-rail bar on any day this week (each
day's digest ran the flash-test explicitly and came back no), and
`attention/flash.yaml` sits empty for the entire week — confirmed
directly: `flashes: []`, with only cosmetic prunes of already-expired
prior entries noted in its comments. Zero flashes filed 08-10 through
08-16, despite three separate mega-events (a >280-dead earthquake, an
outbreak passing 2,000 deaths, and a second earthquake whose toll climbed
to 68) landing in the same seven days.
