## The week's throughline

Five days in, this was a week where every one of the map's six standing
conflict threads moved on real, dated events, and three of them —
Israel-Lebanon, Iran's widening war, and Russia-Ukraine — hardened into
their most consequential shape yet by Friday without any of them
resolving. Iran's Hormuz standoff spent the week collapsing from
negotiation into open contradiction: Trump's Monday compensation
precondition (stacked on Iran's own reparations demand) gave way to a
Tuesday "50 years" escalation an outside analyst dismissed as "banter,"
then to Wednesday's flat contradiction between Trump's claimed "100%"
US Navy control and Iran's own Strait Authority insisting the waterway
"remains blocked" — backed by shipping data showing traffic near a
three-month low. The `iran-oman-hormuz-deal-signing` ledger expectation
formally slipped Wednesday with no signature ever appearing, and by
Friday the fight had widened on three fronts at once: the UAE blamed
Iran's Revolutionary Guard for a drone attack on two tankers ("acts of
piracy"), Canada and nine European countries added fresh sanctions, and
Trump floated declaring the strait "a territory of the United States."
Underneath the diplomacy, the blockade turned lethal for the first time
since February — a Houthi double-tap strike killed six aboard a cargo
ship Tuesday — and Iran's fight quietly widened into Iraq, where its
Quds Force commander lobbied against Baghdad's militia-disarmament
deadline.

Israel-Lebanon supplied the week's sharpest single escalation. What
opened the week as "talks unlikely to resume before end of summer" and
a UNIFIL peacekeeping phase-out closed it with Israeli forces besieging
roughly 40 Hezbollah fighters trapped in a tunnel network beneath the Ali
al-Taher ridge, reportedly awaiting only a US "green light" to seize the
position by force — the most concrete step toward renewed ground combat
this thread has logged since it opened. Hezbollah's own leader rejected
the Rome-talks framework the same day. Notably, an anonymous US State
Department official broke ranks mid-week to call Israeli Defense
Minister Katz's "here to stay" stance on south Lebanon "not consistent"
with the agreed framework — the first daylight gap this thread has
recorded between Washington and Israel's own defense ministry on this
specific point, not just Lebanese criticism.

Gaza saw operations resume after a seven-day pause (two, then three,
targeted strikes across the week) inside the same disarmament-sequencing
dispute — Hamas accepting "decommissioning" in principle against
Netanyahu's insistence on full disarmament first — and closed the week
with Jared Kushner, Nickolay Mladenov and Tony Blair announcing a trip to
Israel and Egypt specifically because Netanyahu rejected Trump's own
15-point plan. A West Bank side-story (the Qusra settler siege) drew a US
ambassador's "act of terror" charge and then, unusually, condemnation
from inside the settler movement itself, while Katz moved enforcement
power over settlers from the army to the police — critics call it a step
toward de facto annexation. Russia-Ukraine produced the week's single
largest claim: Kyiv declared an eight-month counteroffensive complete
Friday, retaking 745 km² (Ukraine's figure; the Institute for the Study
of War's independent count is a more conservative 627 km²) and stripping
Russia of nearly all its Dnipropetrovsk gains — read against a sustained
week of barrages on both sides (Zaporizhzhia/Kyiv's 12 dead Monday, an
Odesa train crew killed Wednesday, 553 Russian-claimed drone intercepts
Friday) that kept both militaries' strain visible rather than pointing to
a lull. Horn of Africa and Europe/Schengen stayed genuinely quiet all
week — no new dated development on either front since before this week
began.

Two events outside this lens's six tracked threads registered at scale
this week and are flagged rather than folded silently in: a magnitude-7.4
earthquake in western Colombia (Monday, the country's strongest this
century) climbed from 224 to 281 confirmed dead by Thursday with search
operations entering a declared "final phase," and the DRC's Ebola
outbreak — first surfaced by this lens's own sweep on 08-12 — passed
2,000 deaths, the fastest-accelerating outbreak on record. Neither
resolves a lens-boundary question the dailies didn't already flag for
Ben (natural disaster/health emergency vs. this lens's conflict remit);
see the map-deltas section below.
