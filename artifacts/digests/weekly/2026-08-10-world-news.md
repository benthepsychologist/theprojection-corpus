## The week's throughline

Six days in, every one of the map's six standing conflict threads moved
on real, dated events, and three of them — Israel-Lebanon, Iran's
widening war, and Russia-Ukraine — hardened week over week into their
most consequential shape yet, closing Saturday sharper than they opened
Monday, without any of them resolving. Iran's Hormuz standoff spent the
week collapsing from negotiation into open contradiction and then into
grudging partial movement: Trump's Monday compensation precondition
(stacked on Iran's own reparations demand) gave way to a Tuesday "50
years" escalation an outside analyst dismissed as "banter," then to
Wednesday's flat contradiction between Trump's claimed "100%" US Navy
control and Iran's own Strait Authority insisting the waterway "remains
blocked" — backed by shipping data showing traffic near a three-month
low. The `iran-oman-hormuz-deal-signing` ledger expectation formally
slipped Wednesday with no signature ever appearing, re-set to 08-19 at
low confidence. By Friday the fight had widened on three fronts at once
— the UAE blamed Iran's Revolutionary Guard for a drone attack on two
tankers ("acts of piracy"), Canada and nine European countries added
fresh sanctions, and Trump floated declaring the strait "a territory of
the United States" — and by Saturday a third ADNOC tanker had been hit
even as Iran and Oman announced real, if partial, progress: a shipping
route-map agreement that Iran's own foreign ministry was explicit is
"not negotiation" and not the full interim deal this map is tracking.
Underneath the diplomacy, the blockade turned lethal for the first time
since February — a Houthi double-tap strike killed six aboard a cargo
ship Tuesday, and Houthi missiles killed civilians and marines at
Yemen's port of Mokha Saturday — and Iran's fight quietly widened into
Iraq, where its Quds Force commander lobbied against Baghdad's
militia-disarmament deadline.

Israel-Lebanon supplied the week's sharpest single escalation, and it
kept sharpening through Saturday. What opened the week as "talks
unlikely to resume before end of summer" and a UNIFIL peacekeeping
phase-out moved by midweek to Israeli forces besieging roughly 40
Hezbollah fighters trapped in a tunnel network beneath the Ali al-Taher
ridge, reportedly awaiting only a US "green light" to seize the position
by force, and closed the week with the deadliest single day of Israeli
strikes in Lebanon since the ceasefire: at least 11 dead across two
villages Saturday, after a Hezbollah drone attack wounded three IDF
soldiers and Israel struck what it called a Hezbollah command post,
hitting a family in the process. Hezbollah's leader had already rejected
the Rome-talks framework outright the day before. An anonymous US State
Department official broke ranks mid-week to call Israeli Defense
Minister Katz's "here to stay" stance on south Lebanon "not consistent"
with the agreed framework — the first daylight gap this thread has
recorded between Washington and Israel's own defense ministry on this
specific point, not just Lebanese criticism.

Gaza saw operations resume after a seven-day pause (two, then three,
targeted strikes across the week) inside the same disarmament-sequencing
dispute — Hamas accepting "decommissioning" in principle against
Netanyahu's insistence on full disarmament first — closed midweek with
Jared Kushner, Nickolay Mladenov and Tony Blair announcing a trip to
Israel and Egypt specifically because Netanyahu rejected Trump's own
15-point plan, and ended the week with the ceasefire's first documented
armed exchange inside the Yellow Line (a Hamas-planted bomb under an IDF
tank; two Palestinians shot dead crossing the line). A West Bank
side-story (the Qusra settler siege, now joined by a second site at Umm
al-Khair) drew a US ambassador's "act of terror" charge, condemnation
from inside the settler movement itself, and Katz moving enforcement
power over settlers from the army to the police — critics call it a
step toward de facto annexation. Russia-Ukraine produced two of the
week's largest claims back to back: Kyiv declared an eight-month
counteroffensive complete Friday, retaking 745 km² (Ukraine's figure;
the Institute for the Study of War's independent count is a more
conservative 627 km²) and stripping Russia of nearly all its
Dnipropetrovsk gains, then followed it Saturday with the deepest
verified strike this thread has logged — a Flamingo cruise-missile hit
on the Savasleyka air base 650-700km inside Russia (home to Kinzhal-
carrying MiG-31Ks), paired with a strike on the Progress Rocket Space
Center in Samara. Read against a sustained week of barrages on both
sides (Zaporizhzhia/Kyiv's 12 dead Monday, an Odesa train crew killed
Wednesday, Saturday's retaliatory strike killing a three-month-old
infant), both militaries' strain stayed visible all week rather than
pointing to any lull. Horn of Africa and Europe/Schengen stayed
genuinely quiet all week — no new dated development on either front
since before this week began.

Two events outside this lens's six tracked threads registered at scale
this week and are flagged rather than folded silently in: a
magnitude-7.4 earthquake in western Colombia (Monday, the country's
strongest this century) climbed from 224 to 281 confirmed dead by
Thursday with search operations entering a declared "final phase," and
the DRC's Ebola outbreak — first surfaced by this lens's own sweep on
08-12 — passed 2,000 deaths, the fastest-accelerating outbreak on
record. Both were offered once as thread candidates (08-10/08-12) and
not re-offered per this lens's own "once, then drop" convention. A
third, genuinely new disaster signal arrived Saturday: a magnitude-7.7
earthquake off Indonesia's Flores Island killed at least 47 and
triggered a since-lifted tsunami warning — offered once as a new
candidate, distinct from the closed Colombia/DRC offers. None of the
three cleared the flash-rail bar on any day this week (each day's
digest ran the flash-test explicitly and came back no), and
`attention/flash.yaml` sits empty for the entire week — confirmed
directly: `flashes: []`, with only cosmetic prunes of already-expired
prior entries noted in its comments. Zero flashes filed 08-10 through
08-15, despite three separate mega-events (a >280-dead earthquake, an
outbreak passing 2,000 deaths, and a second >45-dead earthquake) landing
in the same six days.
