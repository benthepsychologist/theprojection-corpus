## The week's throughline

This was the week evidence-based practice stopped being background
texture and became this lens's own organizing structure — not one story
among many but a reframe of how the lens itself is built. Ben promoted
EBP to a first-class strand ("What Works"), and a ten-agent research wave

primary literature, mostly confirming an uncomfortable middle: reach is
scaling far faster than outcomes (NHS Talking Therapies grew referrals

## By radar question

### Q3 — Is mental-health tech getting more rigorous, or is hype winning?

**What moved:** EBP became a first-class strand this week, and the
ten-question research wave gave it real evidentiary backing rather than
just a new organizing label. The field's one direct attempt to test
whether therapy's efficacy is climbing or falling over time — Johnsen &
Friborg's claim that CBT's effect on depression declines year over year —
was reanalyzed with 30 additional RCTs and came back a statistical
artifact, present only in US-only subsamples: the net verdict is **flat**,
not falling, not rising. Meanwhile reach has scaled roughly 5x (NHS
Talking Therapies volume, 2011→2024) with recovery rates parked in the
same band the whole time. Neuromodulation supplied this week's genuine
counter-case, and Ben ruled it a full member of the evidence family, not
a peripheral watch: SAINT/SNT went from a 30-patient trial underpinning
2022's FDA 510(k) clearance to a sham-controlled confirmatory RCT (*World
Psychiatry* 2026, n=48, remission 50.0% vs. 20.8% sham) that replicated
the *direction* of effect at a far more modest *magnitude* than the
marketed "80-90%" figure — real evidence catching up, four years late,
landing at roughly half the advertised size. On AI specifically: Consumer
Reports' own health-chatbot test found confident, wrong answers (Google
Gemini's mistaken carbon-monoxide-detector placement advice among them)
the same week Anthropic published the first frontier-lab safety numbers
showing real generation-over-generation improvement (multi-turn
self-harm handling appropriate-response rate 56%→86%, Opus 4.1→4.5) —
rigor is visibly improving inside at least one lab, but it is still
vendor-graded, not independently verified, and Therabot — the
highest-profile chatbot-therapy RCT this lens tracks — still has zero
independent replication anywhere in the trial registry.

**Implication:** the book's central thesis holds up under real testing
rather than getting cleanly vindicated or debunked. This is not hype
winning outright — it's a field where reach keeps outrunning evidence at
every scale examined, where the one clear evidence success story
(neuromodulation) took four years and landed at half its advertised
effect, and where AI-specific safety claims are improving on paper but
still ungraded by anyone outside the company shipping the model.

### Q4 — How is AI showing up in mental health — and is it safe and governed?

**What moved:** governance kept fragmenting rather than converging. This
week's dedicated governance research memo counted at least 7 US states
now banning AI from independently delivering therapy outright, nearly
100 chatbot-related bills introduced across states in 2026 alone, and —
checked across five independent sources including a direct search
against Illinois's own regulator — **zero enforcement actions under any
of these laws anywhere**, so the patchwork is untested as well as
uncoordinated. California's SB 903 threaded through a week of suspense-
file uncertainty to a confirmed Assembly Appropriations hearing date of
August 13; the CHATBOT Act (S.4407) and the Kids Online Safety Act both
cleared Senate Commerce markup 08-05. The federal tracks the book
expected to converge mostly haven't: MHPAEA's 2024 parity rule stays
non-enforced (a replacement targeted for December 2026), DEA is on its
fourth "temporary" telemedicine extension with no permanent rule, and
the FTC has brought zero mental-health-specific enforcement actions
since April 2024's Cerebral settlement — only the CMS Physician Fee
Schedule billing-code track is actually advancing on schedule. Litigation
is doing the accountability work regulation hasn't: *Raine v. OpenAI*'s
coordinated proceeding (JCCP No. 5431, five sibling chatbot-death suits)
got its first case-management order 08-04, next conference 09-23. On
harm itself, a New Mexico judge ordered Meta to pay $567M into a child
mental-health fund — on top of March's $375M jury penalty, $942M
combined — for algorithmic design a court called a "public nuisance."
OpenAI announced a teen-safety partnership with the American
Psychological Association (secondary-sourced only so far, no primary
release located — worth a follow-up check). And "AI psychosis" moved one
step past pure case reports: a Vanderbilt retrospective chart review is
the first attempt at population-level prevalence within a defined
clinical population, though it remains single-site and not true
epidemiology — OpenAI's own disclosed figure (~0.07% of weekly users,
~560,000 people, showing possible psychosis/mania signals) is still the
largest number on the record, and it is self-reported and unaudited.

**Implication:** the fragmentation read from the last `/week` held and
hardened rather than resolving. No federal convergence anywhere except
CMS billing codes; states racing ahead of each other and of Washington
with a zero-enforcement track record so far; and litigation, not
regulation, supplying the sharpest live test of what "safe" actually
means for chatbot-delivered mental health.

### Q6 — What's moving in the market around my work?

**What moved:** two comparable public mental-health platforms split hard
on the same week's earnings. LifeStance Health's Q2 adjusted EBITDA
nearly doubled (+94% YoY) with raised full-year guidance and resumed
tuck-in M&A; Talkspace missed both revenue ($61.67M vs. a $67.93M
consensus) and EPS estimates the same week — a real divergence between
two directly comparable outpatient-care platforms, not sector-wide
softness. CVS's Q2 call confirmed AI-driven claims automation
(processing time down over 20%, 1M pharmacist hours redirected to
clinical care) is now standard payer-side language, not just
UnitedHealth's talking point. This week's market-buyers research memo
put a hard number on where the money is actually flowing: H1 2026's two
largest mental-health venture deals were human-clinician marketplaces
(Talkiatry $210M, Grow Therapy $150M), together outraising the two
leading AI-safety-oversight startups roughly 14-to-1 — the venture money
is buying clinicians augmented by software, not autonomous chatbots.
Two buyer pockets that barely existed in 2021 are now the fastest-growing
in the category, and this lens has essentially zero watchlist coverage
of either: private-equity clinical due diligence (56 behavioral-health
deals closed in 2025, up 47% YoY) and AI-liability insurance underwriting
(a ~$6.8B global segment that functionally didn't exist five years ago,
with Chubb, Berkley, AIG and others writing explicit generative-AI
exclusions into policies starting January 2026). An Aflac survey supplied
the demand-side number underwriting every big-tech health bet this lens
tracks: 76% of Gen Z and 63% of millennials now go to AI before a doctor
for health guidance — though the same survey found Gen Z comparatively
*more* willing to see a mental-health therapist specifically than older
cohorts, so the AI-first substitution cuts deeper into primary/preventive
care than into therapy itself.

**Implication:** platform consolidation continues as last week's frame
said it would, but the sharper read this week is that venture capital's
own revealed preference (human clinicians over autonomous chatbots) sits
in real tension with every AI-health headline this lens otherwise tracks
— and this map has genuine structural blind spots on the buyer side
(PE clinical DD, AI-liability underwriters, benefits consultants,
litigation bar) worth naming to Ben directly rather than absorbing
quietly.

**Working-note candidate for radar.md:** *week 08-09 (/week, closing week
