## The week's throughline

The Kaiser fight became a civic event, and the governance layer above the
state-ban wave started to consolidate. Kaiser ran the full arc in one week:
clinician backlash (Mon) → NUHW's formal DMHC/DOL complaint (Wed) → the SF
Board of Supervisors hearing Kaiser *skipped*, agreeing to mediation in
absentia (Wed) → a third media wave Friday (CBS, KQED's "Terrible Three,"
Hoodline's "Kaiser Ghosts City Hall"). Above the individual state bans,
the aggregate view arrived: Transparency Coalition counts **84 new AI laws
in 27 states** this year, with **5 states now prohibiting AI chatbots from
replacing licensed therapists**, and Stanford HAI named the three
regulatory gaps — no consensus definition of "mental health AI," evaluation
frameworks that lag the field, and engagement-maximizing business models
structurally at odds with healthy use. Meanwhile OpenAI moved in both
directions at once: a new liability suit and ChatGPT Health going GA to
all US adults, while its Foundation funded Child Mind Institute research
into youth-MH markers in chatbot interactions.

## By radar question

### Q3 — Is MH tech getting more rigorous, or is hype winning?

**What moved:** Stanford HAI's convening (07-24) is the sharpest rigor
statement of the week — evaluation is point-in-time on proprietary data
when it needs to be longitudinal, and definitional ambiguity lets companies
"operate in ambiguity." The Transparency Coalition mid-year report (07-21)
gives the ban-wave its first aggregate scoreboard. OpenAI's Foundation
funding Child Mind Institute (07-24, amount undisclosed) to find *valid
markers of youth mental state in chat histories* is the vendor itself
paying for the missing evidence layer. **Implication:** rigor is
arriving — but from governance conveners and the regulated party, not yet
from standardized third-party evaluation. **Working-note candidate:** the
evaluation-standards gap is now the named bottleneck; watch who builds it.

### Q4 — How is AI showing up in mental health — and is it safe and governed?

**What moved:** the Kaiser arc (above) — labor + city government are now
the de-facto governance forum a year before most state laws bite. The ban
wave widened (VT, RI, NY under-18 companion ban; HI's Act 248 already
signed 07-14). PA v. Character.AI: still no ruling (checked three times
this week). New OpenAI liability suit (bipolar/manic episode, SF).
ChatGPT Health GA'd to all US adults 18+ (07-24) — the largest
consumer-scale health-AI exposure event so far, landing while the
liability suits accumulate. **Implication:** deployment is outrunning the
forums that would govern it; the near-term governance is happening in
courtrooms, union complaints, and city halls.

### Q6 — What's moving in the market around my work?

Rock Health H1: **MH is the #1-funded clinical category, $7.4B** (07-20).
Handspring $19M · firsthand $10M-of-$32M · Mindoula's 3rd and 4th
acquisitions (Valera, Janus) · Headspace built out specialty care
(Charlie Health, Cortica, Prosper, Equip — 07-21) · UHS–Talkspace $835M
on track · Teladoc One launched. The capital is consolidating platforms
while the governance question stays open.
