## The week's throughline

**Nvidia's Q2 print became the week's central test and answered it
cleanly, but the harder fight underneath — buy loyalty or build
in-house — sharpened on every front at once.** The market spent three
days pricing Nvidia's own August warning that AI-server prices would
rise more than 15% on memory costs: a broad semiconductor selloff into
Wednesday (Intel −5%, AMD −4%, TSMC −3%), a mainstream wire service
(Reuters, not a short-seller newsletter) explicitly framing "circular
financing" as the question the print had to answer, and a five-quarter
pattern of Nvidia selling off 2% even after beats. The print itself
closed the question this map has tracked all month: $96.2B revenue
(+106% YoY), a Q3 guide of $108B — Nvidia's first $100B+ quarter — gross
margin cut to 71-72% for Q4 explicitly on memory costs, and the
top-five hyperscalers' own capex guidance rising to $1.3T next year
from $800B. Hyperscalers are absorbing the price rise, not cutting
orders. Underneath that answer, Nvidia spent the same three days buying
the loyalty its pricing power depends on — reversing a shaky rumor into
a real $6B non-exclusive Poolside licence plus $1B equity and 100+
engineers for its own open-weight Nemotron line, shipping Groq 3 LPX to
full production, and converting a three-day-old Lancium stake into a
15+ GW deployment platform — while OpenAI answered from the other
direction, publishing the first public benchmark for its in-house
Jalapeño ASIC (built with Broadcom) claiming to beat Nvidia's own
flagship on performance-per-watt, one day before Nvidia's print. And a
structural gap this map keeps hitting recurred a third time: Sam Altman
telling a podcast AI hasn't had its "iPhone moment" yet, in the same
week the buildout kept accelerating on every other axis, with still no
thread for industry messaging and public trust as its own subject. The
coverage critic caught something rarer than a miss this week — its own
blind spot: three "new" items it flagged all traced to one Nvidia press
wave timestamped to the same minute, exposing that both this map's
detectors (the benchmark critic and the mechanical world-news pool)
read wire-syndicated stories as independent breaking news.

## By radar question

### Q1 — Who are the players, and what are they DOING?

OpenAI answered by losing the person who ran its buildout: Chris Malone,
who led OpenAI's data-center construction against its own ~$600bn
compute-spend target through 2030, departed (08-25) — the 13th senior
exit of 2026, responsibilities split three ways. The same week OpenAI's
Jalapeño ASIC cleared its first public benchmark at Hot Chips (700W TDP,
1.7 exaflops/pod, claimed 1.9x throughput-per-kilowatt over Nvidia's
flagship), and OpenAI let a 29-signer congressional safety-disclosure
deadline (08-24) pass in total silence before Alabama's AG subpoenaed it
over the same July intrusion (08-25) — voluntary disclosure produced
nothing, compulsory state process moved. Anthropic answered on two
fronts: merging memory across Claude Chat and Claude Cowork (08-25, the
first live entry on the newly-opened enterprise-agent-product-race
thread) and committing $45bn over six years to Nscale's West Virginia
campus (~460MW, Vera Rubin chips, online late 2027) — its fifth
anchor-lease compute counterparty in roughly three months, the same
rent-not-own pattern every time. Nvidia answered loudest: the Poolside
reversal, Groq 3 LPX to full production, the Lancium platform
conversion, and the Q2 print itself. Zhipu/Z.AI resolved a two-run-old
mystery by confirming it built "Ox Alpha" — the anonymous, no-lab-
attached model that had passed DeepSeek on OpenRouter usage — and
open-weighted it the same night (08-26), anonymity used deliberately as
a launch tactic until its leaderboard position was secure. Xiaomi
shipped a 3nm in-house SoC (Xring/Xuanjie O3) fabbed by TSMC rather than
SMIC — Chinese *design* independence built on *deeper* dependence on
Taiwanese fabrication, cutting against this map's usual "stack
independence" framing.

### Q2 — Where is the money going?

The Poolside deal moved from an unverifiable single-newsletter chain to
a WSJ-reviewed shareholder letter this week: $6bn for a non-exclusive
licence to Poolside's training pipeline, a separate $1bn investment at a
$12bn pre-money valuation, and 100+ Poolside engineers moving to Nvidia
to build Nemotron. Two smaller but real power-supply-chain deals landed
the same day (08-24): nVent Electric buying Maverick Power for $1.75bn,
Infineon acquiring C2i Semiconductors. Morgan Stanley put the first
outside dollar figure on the Blackwell-to-Rubin transition — roughly $9bn
of Nvidia's fiscal Q3 sales — against Street consensus of $104.2bn
(+82.8% YoY), a number the actual print then beat outright at $108bn
guided. The semiconductor selloff into Wednesday (Intel −5%, AMD −4%,
TSMC −3%, SOXX −3–4%, Micron/SanDisk/Western Digital each off more than
5%) read the memory-price story as demand-softening; the print itself
read the opposite way once hyperscaler capex guidance came in higher,
not lower. Anthropic's $45bn Nscale commitment is this map's fifth
tracked anchor-lease deal in three months (after SpaceX ~$45B, Volta
$10B, Riot Platforms $9.1B, Theseus/Macquarie-GIC) — the same
rent-the-buildout shape recurring at a new counterparty each time.

### Q5 — Where is frontier AI heading overall?

The containment/governance throughline gained a second registered
response mode to sit beside "convert failure into a regulatory ask"
(the 08-17 close's finding): silence, then compulsion. OpenAI and
Anthropic let Congress's safety-disclosure deadline pass without a word,
and only a state attorney general's subpoena — not voluntary disclosure
— produced movement. Export control widened to a third channel: Chinese
state media publicly urged Dutch retaliation over a threatened ASML
export ban, beyond the existing legislative (MATCH Act) and Dutch-
government tracks. The "stack independence" story got a genuine
complication rather than a confirmation: Xiaomi's TSMC-fabbed 3nm SoC
shows the two halves of the China stack — chip design and chip
fabrication — decoupling from the US at different speeds, one faster
than the other. The power/siting fight escalated to new venues this
week: a state utility regulator's deadline (Georgia PSC on Project
Camellia) rather than a county zoning board, a unanimous Ohio council
rejection with a ballot measure still pending, and — for the first time
this map has logged it as the binding constraint — water: Amazon
withdrew a Virginia groundwater application after the state's own DEQ
warned the aquifer couldn't absorb it. And OpenAI's Jalapeño benchmark,
landing one day before Nvidia's print, is this map's first evidence-
stage sign that a negotiating chip (custom silicon as leverage) is
becoming a real second source rather than a threat.
