## 1. The question

> "Who's buying inference? How much? And what are those committed
> capacity contracts worth?"

Three asks, three measurement objects: **WHO** (classification),
**HOW MUCH** (realized revenue flows), **WORTH** (forward obligations —
neither of the first two). Press smears all three into one number; the
design keeps them apart.

## 2. Where q2 sits on the q1 model

**q2 is a view of the q1 map plus one promoted object.** Corrected per
round 1 (and recorded against q1's R-13): this is *no redesign — one
promoted object plus additive fields, cheap before sourcing*, not
literally zero schema changes. The additions, all landed in q1 v3 or
specified here:

- `commitment_ref` (nullable) on flow edges — the join that makes
  amortization computable (q1 v3 §3).
- stage/basis on observations + typed values with bound semantics
  (q1 v3 §4).
- the commitment object's relations and the `ext/*` node class (below).

**The consolidation frame, filter-wise (R-16).** External revenue is
revenue crossing a **named cut**, not a property of a buyer. q2 ships
two revenue-side starting cuts ⚙:

- **`cut:cloud-frontier`** — the group ends at cloud/compute providers;
  everything beyond them (aggregators, AI-app companies, enterprises,
  consumers, governments) is external. Computable now.
- **`cut:retail-inference`** — resellers of AI capability are inside;
  external means the ultimate non-reselling buyer. Computable **where
  the map decomposes that far** — and where it doesn't, the surface
  says so (R-17: name the ambiguity, never legislate it).

**There is no F9.** Round 1 proposed a threshold rule for when a
reseller counts as external; Ben rejected it: *"It's a filter not a
rule… Different ways of cutting."* The data mandate replaces the rule:
**reseller-level flows are collected** — aggregators and AI-app
companies become entities in the map with their purchase and revenue
flows recorded where gettable. Which cut a total uses is stated on its
face; the burn metric and the §7 hypothesis are cut-stamped like every
consolidated number (q1 v3 §3 delta/stamp rules).

**Grounding.** The July capex crawl's own finding — the payoff layer is
the weakest in the map ("press-only backlogs; the $250B OpenAI→capex
mapping doesn't exist publicly") — is the gap this question works.

## 3. WHO — buyer taxonomy and endpoints

**Taxonomy ⚙ (reporting buckets, not boundary verdicts):** consumer
subscriptions · enterprise (coarse sector) · government & defense ·
AI-capability resellers (aggregators, API middlemen, AI-app companies) ·
in-group AI companies. A buyer's boundary status is given by the applied
cut, per R-16 — the taxonomy never decides it.

**The `ext/*` population node class (round-1 fix).** q2's external
buyers are populations, not nameable entities. Sanctioned node class:
`ext/consumer` · `ext/enterprise-<sector>` · `ext/gov-<jurisdiction>`,
mirroring q1's F6: inbound allocation to a population node is a claim
with observations and reliability. Survey observations ("financial
services ≈ N% of enterprise AI spend") now have a legal endpoint.

**Buyer classification is a claim** (q1 R-04): a reseller's AI-resale
share, where needed by a cut, is recorded with observations, rationale,
and reliability — never a silent sourcer judgment. The gray zone
(reselling AI capability vs. selling a product that embeds AI) is not
legislated; where a case is ambiguous the classification claim says so,
and cut-stamped totals inherit the flag.

**The government seam, respecced (round-1 fix — the sweep was measuring
ceilings and middlemen):**

- sweep **task-order-level actions and outlays**, never IDIQ award
  ceilings (ceilings are stage-guidance/commitment, not revenue);
- capture `contract_vehicle` per record, and maintain the
  **vehicle→OEM unwinding map** (Carahsoft, CSP consumption vehicles →
  the actual AI seller) as a named Tier A deliverable;
- **nominal-price awards** ($1-per-agency deals) are recorded as
  adoption evidence, never revenue;
- the seam's chief value is as a **calibration set** — the one domain
  with named-buyer/named-amount ground truth, against which the overlay
  and survey methods are graded before they carry weight elsewhere.

## 4. HOW MUCH — realized revenue, on the ladder

**The revenue ladder:** claimed run-rate → booked → recognized →
collected, carried by the observation's `stage` field (q1 v3 §4), so
"never mixed into a period total" is machine-enforceable, not prose.

**Placement rule (corrected in round 1 by White):** stage-2 (booked
contract) observations attach to the **commitment object**; stage-3/4
(recognized/collected) attach to **revenue edges**; joined by
`commitment_ref`. **Stage-1 (claimed run-rate) attaches to the seller's
aggregated revenue edge to `ext/*` (unallocated)** — it has no
counterparty and no contract, so it attaches to no commitment — stage-
marked, excluded from period totals, eligible to spawn derived
estimates.

**Decision (d), amended (round-1 fix — the recognized-only rule was
zeroing the largest external sellers):** "recognized" includes **derived
recognized-revenue estimates** — annualization unwound, haircut applied,
method noted, provenance `inference from other claims`, low reliability
— wherever a seller (OpenAI, Anthropic, xAI) discloses no recognized
figure. The ladder gates raw claims by measurand; it never ranks sources
by type. Headline totals quote solid+hatched with the split stated, as
everywhere else.

**The inference-share overlay, respecced (round-1 fix):** the overlay's
output is a **revenue share** — utilization mix (tokens, traffic,
subscriber math) × **price mix** (price cards, disclosed contract
rates). An overlay lacking the price term is a *capacity share*,
labelled as such and banned from multiplying into dollars. The Tier B
probe grades the reliability ceiling of the revenue-space method before
any output is used.

**Consumer sourcing (line fix):** the ~1B weekly-actives figure stays
what it is — a recorded benchmark. The method's dominating inputs get
their own named chase: paying-subscriber counts and paying-conversion
rate, each a sourced, estimated claim.

## 5. WORTH — the commitment register

**The commitment object, v2 (round-1 seam package):**

```
commitment := {parties, side (supply-build | demand-capacity),
               face (typed value), term, structure, termination/
               contingency clauses, funding_linkage (origination refs),
               serves_ref (the upstream obligation this commitment
               exists to fulfil — pass-through chains),
               part_of_ref (frame ⊃ constituent containment),
               amortized_to_date (derived via commitment_ref'd flows),
               observations[]}
```

- **Every worth-bearing field is observation-bearing** (round-1 fix for
  term movement): structure, clauses, and linkage states are dated,
  sourced, superseding observations under the one evidence model — a
  commitment's term history is a sequence, never a mutable scalar. The
  canonical case (face reiterated at $250B while exclusivity and refusal
  rights transformed) is now representable as what it was: a worth
  event at constant face.
- **`amortized_to_date` is derived**, never stored independently — the
  sum of flows carrying this commitment's `commitment_ref`. One source
  of truth; q1's F4 consumes it.
- **v1 stops at face + structure + clauses + linkage + flags.**
  Expected-realization modeling stays deferred: the discriminating
  variable (structure) is the undisclosed one, and an EV model now would
  be face × assumed-haircut — the press's error with extra steps.

**Register hygiene (round-1 fix):** the seed table splits three ways —

1. **Commitment entries** (parties, side, face…): OpenAI→Microsoft
   $250B (demand-capacity) · OpenAI→Oracle >$300B/5yr (demand-capacity;
   `part_of_ref` → Stargate frame if evidence places it there) ·
   OpenAI→Amazon $100B/8yr compute (demand-capacity) · Stargate "up to
   $500B/4yr" (supply-build **frame**, constituents attach via
   `part_of_ref`) · Anthropic→Amazon $100B+/decade · Anthropic→Google
   TPU (face unknown, flagged) · Anthropic→Microsoft/Azure $30B ·
   OpenAI↔AMD 6GW/warrants (non-cash consideration; structure =
   milestone-vested warrants).
2. **Node-level backlog observations** (party-less RPO aggregates —
   never register rows, never summed): CoreWeave backlog · AWS backlog
   (the $496B single-source figure and the $364B
   excluding-Anthropic figure, the latter now representable as a
   scope-qualified value).
3. **Financing components** route to q1 financing edges, surfacing on
   commitments only as `funding_linkage` (the OpenAI→Amazon $50B
   investment leg is a q1 edge, not a register row).

**Backlog reconciliation — the demand-side conservation check
(round-1's best constructive finding):** per node, Σ active register
faces vs. disclosed backlog; the gap = unregistered committed inbound.
A first-class coverage surface, cut-stamped like every total.

## 6. The chain rule for commitments — sanctioned aggregates

Backlogs chain like flows (OpenAI→Microsoft→neocloud is one future
dollar at multiple hops), and frames contain constituents. Sanctioned
aggregates, each rendered with its dedup method and cut on its face:

- **(a) boundary-committed** — external buyers' commitments into the
  group, under a named cut, deduped via `part_of_ref` (containment binds
  on the external side too: a government frame agreement and its
  per-agency commitments are all external, and sum wrong without it).
- **(b) per-node registers** — deduped via both relations.
- **(c) deduped intra-group committed face** — side-filtered
  (demand-capacity only), deduped via `serves_ref` + `part_of_ref`,
  always rendered with method + cut stated. *(Round-1 fix: this
  aggregate is sanctioned rather than smuggled — computed numbers become
  product surfaces here, so the internal-vs-published distinction was
  not load-bearing.)*

An undeduped "industry backlog" headline remains unconstructible.

## 7. The headline hypothesis — restated gradeable (round-1 fix)

> **Under `cut:cloud-frontier` ⚙: demand-side intra-group committed face
> amortizing into the next 12 months (per F4 mechanics, deduped via
> `serves_ref`/`part_of_ref`) exceeds trailing-12-month
> recognized-or-derived external inference revenue by ≥10×.**

The stock-vs-flow ambiguity is closed (both sides are trailing/next
twelve months); the unit convention is pinned before grading; the cut is
named. The burn metric pins the **same window convention** on both sides
when it lands with q2 (q1 R-18).

**Prediction table, split per worth-bearing field (line fix):**

## 8. Tiers ⚙

- **Tier A:** the commitment register from §5's seeds (audited first,
  per q1 R-12) · realized-revenue observations for every seller that
  discloses anything, with derived estimates where they don't ·
  **reseller-level flow collection** (aggregators, AI-app companies —
  entities in the map, purchases and revenue both) · the government
  sweep as specced in §3, including the vehicle→OEM unwinding map.
- **Tier B probes:** the revenue-space overlay method (graded before
  use) · enterprise surveys · app-store/subscriber data paths.
  Probes output collectors (q1 v3 §5).

## 9. What q2 deliberately is not

Not a re-derivation of q1; not an EV model of contract worth; not a
boundary rulebook (R-17 — cuts, not rules); and not the bar, which is
Ben's.
