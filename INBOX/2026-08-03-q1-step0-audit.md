## The four findings that matter beyond the counts

1. **Rot is fast and eats primaries first.** Cerebras and Groq press
   releases — primary sources — died within ~2 years; Microsoft's old
   `news.microsoft.com` path 404s after a URL migration while
   `blogs.microsoft.com` lives. The capture asymmetry ("you can rework
   anything except what you didn't capture") is demonstrated on our own
   shelf.
2. **The bot-wall census, by domain:** `cnbc.com` blocked ~10/10 ·
   `openai.com/index/*` 6/6 · `federalregister.gov` 3/3 (CAPTCHA
   redirect) · `commerce.gov`, `datacenterdynamics.com`, CNN (HTTP 451)
   one each. The highest-authority sources are the least scrapable —
   the capture backlog is a *tooling* problem (archive.org, alternate
   networks, manual capture), not a sourcing problem.
3. **The insidious class: claims not in the cited article.** Six
   reachable, legitimate citations carry notes whose key figures the
   page never states: Anthropic's ~$18.1B Series D valuation · DeepSeek's
   High-Flyer-financing and VC-decline narrative · the Inflection ~$650M
   licensing figure · DeepSeek's corporate co-investor list · the
   circular-financing anchor's Oracle/AMD/Broadcom extension · Meta's
   capex figures. These are figures sourced *somewhere else* and
   attached to a citation that doesn't carry them — Red's backfill
   question ("is this source the actual basis for the claim?") observed
   in the wild. Each needs a source swap or supplement at capture time.
4. **Zero fabrications.** Both MISMATCHes are clerical: one record cites
   a different Amazon announcement than its figures describe; one
   carries a date ~8 months off the page's own byline. The staging was
   honest; the links and attributions are the perishable parts.
