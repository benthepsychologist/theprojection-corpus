Watch who builds the evaluation standard.
  - week 08-04 (/week, closing week 07-27–08-02): the evidence kept
    arriving, and it was mostly bad news about the current generation of
    tools rather than vindication. A Northeastern preprint tested 8
    chatbots across 16 psychiatric conditions — suicide/self-harm
    safeguards had genuinely improved, but every other sensitive
    mental-health question still failed about 81% of the time for
    ChatGPT/Gemini/DeepSeek (Claude performed best). A companion paper
    named five specific risky interaction patterns (delayed care,
    reinforced compulsions, social withdrawal, reinforced delusions, loss
    of independent judgment), moving "AI can be bad for you" from a vague
    worry to a named taxonomy. First concrete attempt at the missing
    evaluation-standard layer surfaced too: a proposed unified benchmark,
    "CARE-MH," early-stage. Working-note candidate: watch whether CARE-MH
    or something like it gets cited by anyone outside its own author
    list — that's the actual test of whether the missing standard is
    finally getting built, versus one more preprint nobody adopts.
  - week 08-09 (/week, closing week 08-03–08-09): evidence-based practice
    was promoted to a first-class strand this week (`mh-evidence-watch` +
    five children, plus `ai-psychosis` and `neuromodulation-evidence`), and
    a ten-agent research wave gave the question its sharpest evidentiary
    grounding yet. The field's one direct era-moderator test — does
    therapy get more effective over time — resolved to FLAT, not falling
    or rising, once a reanalysis with 30 additional RCTs corrected for a
    US-only-subsample artifact; reach keeps scaling ~5x ahead of outcomes
    across every delivery model examined (NHS Talking Therapies,
    task-sharing, single-session interventions). Neuromodulation is the
    one place evidence genuinely caught up to a marketed claim — SAINT/SNT
    went from a 30-patient trial underpinning 2022's FDA clearance to a
    sham-controlled confirmatory RCT four years later, replicating

    vs. 20.8% sham remission, not the marketed "80-90%"). Zero independent

    specifically: Consumer Reports caught confident wrong health answers
    the same week Anthropic published real generation-over-generation
    safety improvement (self-harm appropriate-response 56%→86%,
    Opus 4.1→4.5) — rigor improving, but still vendor-graded, not
    independently verified. Watch: whether the psychedelic sprint's Q4
    2026 NDA filing draws scrutiny of a trial-design mismatch (pivotal
    trials enrolled before the FDA guidance they'll be judged against
    existed even in draft), and whether VERA-MH (the one
    clinician-validated AI-safety benchmark) gains adoption beyond its
    industry-convened origin.
